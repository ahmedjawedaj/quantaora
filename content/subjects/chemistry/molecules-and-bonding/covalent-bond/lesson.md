# Covalent Bond

*Chemistry > Molecules And Bonding > How Atoms Bond > The two great bonds > Covalent Bond*

A covalent bond is a chemical bond formed when atoms share a pair of electrons. It is the glue that holds molecules like water, oxygen, and methane together.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a water molecule — one red sphere (oxygen) with two pale spheres (hydrogen) — floating slowly, with two glowing bands between them. Tap the `hs-covalent-bond` hotspot to open its card. Each band is a shared pair of electrons.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the water molecule and zoom into a bond. Toggle the 'shared pair' layer to see two small electron dots sitting between the oxygen and hydrogen, pulled on by both atoms. Toggle the 'double bond' layer to swap to an oxygen molecule (O₂) and see two shared pairs at once.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-covalent-bond` to label a bond, `hs-shared-pair` to label the shared electrons, and `hs-bond-atoms` to label the oxygen and hydrogen atoms the bond joins.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through how a covalent bond works: two nonmetal atoms each need one more electron for a full shell, so they share a pair between them. Both nuclei pull on the shared pair, and that double pull is what holds the atoms together in a stable molecule.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the double-bond layer on, predict how many electron pairs you think O₂ shares between its two atoms — then count.

## 5. See where it belongs

A covalent bond belongs to the **molecule** — the object it builds. From there it connects downward to the **valence electrons** that get shared, and across to the **nonmetals** that form these bonds.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the **covalent bond** with the **ionic bond**. A covalent bond shares electrons — two nonmetals hold a pair together. An ionic bond transfers electrons — a metal hands electrons to a nonmetal, and the opposite charges attract. Sharing versus transferring, molecules versus salts.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Oxygen needs two more electrons for a full shell, and each hydrogen needs one. Why does sharing solve both problems at once?* Use the shared-pair layer to reason it out.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to the shared electron pair and explain aloud why both atoms are pulled toward it.
