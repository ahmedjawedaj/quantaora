# Lewis Structure

*Chemistry > Molecules And Bonding > Electrons That Bond > Drawing the electrons > Lewis Structure*

A Lewis structure is a shorthand diagram that shows an element's symbol with dots around it for its valence electrons. It is a fast way to predict how atoms will bond.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: three flat tiles floating in a row — sodium (Na), carbon (C), and chlorine (Cl) — each with dots arranged around its symbol. Tap the `hs-lewis-dots` hotspot to open its card. Count the dots: 1, 4, and 7.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the tiles and zoom in. Toggle the 'bond forming' layer to watch carbon's four dots reach toward four hydrogen atoms and pair up — shared pairs become covalent bonds, building a methane molecule. Toggle the 'dot placement' layer to see the rule — one dot per side first, then pairing.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-lewis-symbol` to label the element symbol at the center, `hs-lewis-dots` to label the valence electron dots, and `hs-lewis-bonding` to label the shared dot pairs that become bonds.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through how a Lewis structure works: write the symbol, place one dot per valence electron around its four sides (first one per side, then pair), and read the dots to predict bonding. Carbon's four dots are four electrons ready to share — building four bonds in methane.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the bond-forming layer on, predict how many hydrogen atoms you think carbon's four dots will bond with.

## 5. See where it belongs

A Lewis structure belongs to the **valence electron** idea — it is that idea drawn out. From there it connects to the **covalent bonds** that shared dot pairs represent, and to the periodic table **groups** whose dot patterns it repeats.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a **Lewis structure** with a **shell model**. The shell model draws every shell and every electron. The Lewis structure shows only the valence electrons as dots around the symbol — a stripped-down version focused purely on bonding. Same idea, faster drawing.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Chlorine's Lewis structure shows seven dots. What does the seventh, unpaired dot tell you chlorine is ready to do?* Use the dots to reason about chlorine's bonding.

**Practice item:** On the bond-forming layer, point to a shared dot pair between carbon and hydrogen and explain aloud what that pair represents.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What does a Lewis structure show, and how do you read its dots to predict bonding?
