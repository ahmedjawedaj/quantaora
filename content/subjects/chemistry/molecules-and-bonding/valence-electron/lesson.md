# Valence Electron

*Chemistry > Molecules And Bonding > Electrons That Bond > The outer-shell electrons > Valence Electron*

A valence electron is an electron in an atom's outermost shell. It is the electron that does the bonding — the one shared or transferred when atoms react.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a sodium atom with its shell rings, the outer ring carrying one bright electron. Tap the `hs-valence-shell` hotspot to open its card. The electron on the outer ring is the valence electron.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the atom and zoom in. Toggle the 'bonding' layer to watch the valence electron reach toward a chlorine atom and transfer over, forming an ionic bond. Toggle the 'shell count' layer to see the inner electrons dim and the outer electron highlight — the outer one is the one that matters.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-valence-shell` to label the outermost shell, `hs-valence-electron` to label the electron on it, and `hs-inner-shell` to label the inner electrons that stay out of bonding.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what a valence electron does: it lives on the outermost shell, and it is the electron shared in covalent bonds or transferred in ionic bonds. Its count decides how many bonds an atom can form — sodium's one valence electron lets it give up just one bond to chlorine.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the bonding layer on, predict whether sodium's valence electron will be shared with chlorine or given away completely.

## 5. See where it belongs

A valence electron belongs to the **atom** as its outermost-shell electron. From there it connects to the **bonds** it makes (covalent sharing, ionic transfer), and to the periodic table **group** whose behavior it explains.

## 6. Compare it

Compare **valence electrons** with **inner electrons**. Valence electrons live on the outermost shell and do all the bonding — sharing and transferring. Inner electrons are buried in closer shells and just stay put. One ring decides chemistry; the others just fill space.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Sodium has one valence electron and chlorine has seven. Why do they bond so readily?* Use the bonding layer to watch the transfer and explain the attraction.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to the valence electron and explain aloud what happens to it when sodium bonds with chlorine.
