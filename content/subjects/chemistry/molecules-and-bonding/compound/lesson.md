# Compound

*Chemistry > Molecules And Bonding > What Bonds Build > Bonded different elements > Compound*

A compound is a pure substance made of two or more different elements chemically bonded together. When elements bond, they gain a brand-new identity with brand-new properties.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: three substances — water (H₂O), carbon dioxide (CO₂), and table salt (NaCl) — floating together. Tap the `hs-compound-examples` hotspot to open its card. Notice each one is built from atoms of different kinds.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the substances and zoom in. Toggle the 'element split' layer to pull each compound apart into its separate elements — watch the water split into hydrogen and oxygen atoms. Toggle the 'identity' layer to see the elements' old properties (flammable gas, metal, poison) flip to the compound's new ones.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-compound-examples` to label the compounds, `hs-compound-elements` to label the different elements inside, and `hs-compound-new-identity` to label the brand-new substance they form.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what a compound is: different elements bonded in a fixed ratio, gaining a new identity. Water is always 2H + 1O; break it and you lose the water. The elements' old properties vanish — sodium plus chlorine no longer makes a dangerous metal and a poison, it makes table salt.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the element-split layer on, predict what hydrogen and oxygen atoms look like when water is pulled apart — are they still water?

## 5. See where it belongs

A compound belongs to the family of **substances** — pure matter with a fixed recipe. From there it connects downward to the **elements** that bond together, and across to the **molecule** idea (most compounds are molecules) and the **bonds** that hold them.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a **compound** with a **molecule**. A compound is a substance of DIFFERENT elements chemically bonded. A molecule is any two or more atoms bonded — same element (O₂) or different (H₂O). O₂ is a molecule but not a compound; H₂O is both.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Salt is made of sodium, a reactive metal, and chlorine, a poisonous gas. Why is table salt safe to eat?* Use the identity layer to follow the elements becoming a new substance.

**Practice item:** On the element-split layer, point to a compound and its separated elements, and explain aloud why the compound's properties are not the elements' properties.
