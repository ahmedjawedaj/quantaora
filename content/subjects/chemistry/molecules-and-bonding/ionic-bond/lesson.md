# Ionic Bond

*Chemistry > Molecules And Bonding > How Atoms Bond > The two great bonds > Ionic Bond*

An ionic bond is a chemical bond formed when atoms transfer electrons — a metal gives electrons to a nonmetal, and the resulting opposite charges attract. It is the bond that builds salts like table salt.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a sodium atom (soft violet) and a chlorine atom (muted green) side by side, with a glowing path between them. Tap the `hs-ionic-transfer` hotspot to open its card. Notice the one small electron on sodium's outer ring.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the atoms. Toggle the 'transfer' layer to watch sodium's lone outer electron glide across the gap to chlorine. Toggle the 'lattice' layer to see the model expand into a small cube of alternating violet and green ions — the repeating crystal structure of salt.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-ionic-transfer` to label the electron move, `hs-ions-attract` to label the attracting ions, and `hs-ionic-lattice` to label the salt crystal grid.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through how an ionic bond forms: sodium (a metal) gives its outer electron to chlorine (a nonmetal). Sodium becomes positive Na⁺, chlorine becomes negative Cl⁻, and the opposite charges attract and lock together. Millions of these ions stack into a repeating lattice — a salt crystal.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the transfer layer on, predict what happens to sodium's charge when it loses an electron — positive or negative?

## 5. See where it belongs

An ionic bond belongs to the **compound** it builds — an ionic compound like table salt. From there it connects downward to the **metal** that gives and the **nonmetal** that takes, and across to its rival, the **covalent bond**.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the **ionic bond** with the **covalent bond**. An ionic bond TRANSFERS electrons — metal to nonmetal, then opposite charges attract. A covalent bond SHARES electrons — two nonmetals hold a pair together. Transferring builds salts; sharing builds molecules.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Sodium has one outer electron and chlorine needs exactly one more. Why do the two atoms attract each other after sodium gives its electron away?* Use the transfer layer to trace the exchange.

**Practice item:** On the lattice layer, point to a Na⁺ ion and a Cl⁻ ion and explain aloud why they stick together.
