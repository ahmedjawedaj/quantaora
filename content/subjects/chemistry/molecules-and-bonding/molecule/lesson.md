# Molecule

*Chemistry > Molecules And Bonding > What Bonds Build > Covalently bonded atoms > Molecule*

A molecule is two or more atoms held together by covalent bonds. It is the smallest unit of a substance that still keeps that substance's identity.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: four floating ball-and-stick structures — O₂ (two red spheres), H₂O (one red, two pale), CO₂ (one gray, two red), and CH₄ (one gray, four pale). Tap the `hs-molecule-examples` hotspot to open its card. Each one is a molecule.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate each molecule and zoom in. Toggle the 'bond highlight' layer to make every covalent bond glow, showing the joints between atoms. Toggle the 'break apart' layer to split one molecule into its separate atoms — notice what happens to its identity as a substance.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-molecule-examples` to label the molecules, `hs-molecule-atoms` to label the atoms that team up, and `hs-molecule-bonds` to label the covalent bonds holding them together.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what a molecule is: two or more atoms joined by covalent bonds, acting as one unit. It is the smallest particle that is still the substance — break a water molecule and you have hydrogen and oxygen, not water.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the break-apart layer on, predict what a water molecule becomes when it is split — do you still have water?

## 5. See where it belongs

A molecule belongs to the **substance** it forms — water, oxygen, methane. From there it connects downward to the **atoms** that team up and the **covalent bonds** that hold them, and across to the **compound** idea, which covers molecules made of different elements.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a **molecule** with a **compound**. A molecule is any two or more atoms bonded covalently — same element (O₂) or different (H₂O). A compound is specifically a substance made of DIFFERENT elements bonded together. Every mixed-element molecule is a compound; a same-element molecule like O₂ is a molecule but not a compound.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Which of the four molecules in front of you are compounds, and which is not?* Sort them by checking whether their atoms are all the same element.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to the atoms inside one molecule and explain aloud what holds them together as a team.
