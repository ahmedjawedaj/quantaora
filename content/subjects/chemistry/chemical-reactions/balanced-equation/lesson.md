# Balanced Equation

*Chemistry > Chemical Reactions > Reading Reactions > The quantities > Balanced Equation*

A balanced equation is a chemical equation with equal numbers of each atom on both sides — obeying conservation of mass. It is the precise recipe of a reaction.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: the equation `2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O` with the molecules of each side arranged beneath. Tap the `hs-balanced-pair` hotspot to open its card. Notice the atom groups on each side.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the equation and zoom in. Toggle the 'count' layer to split every molecule into atoms and see them match side by side. Toggle the 'unbalance' layer to remove a coefficient — watch the equation tip out of balance and see why it matters.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-balanced-pair` to label the balanced equation, `hs-balanced-count` to label the matching atom counts, and `hs-balanced-coefficient` to label the coefficients doing the work.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what a balanced equation does: it keeps equal numbers of each atom on both sides, obeying conservation of mass. You balance by adjusting coefficients — never subscripts, because subscripts define the substance. The result is a true recipe.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the count layer on, predict whether the right side has the same number of hydrogens as the left.

## 5. See where it belongs

A balanced equation belongs to the **chemical reaction** it describes. From there it connects to the **coefficients** that balance it, the **reactants** and **products** it matches, and the **conservation of mass** it obeys.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a **balanced equation** with an **unbalanced one**. A balanced equation has equal atoms on both sides — it matches reality. An unbalanced equation has mismatched atoms — it would describe atoms appearing or vanishing. Balancing fixes the count, never the substances.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *In `2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O`, how many oxygen atoms are on each side, and what balances them?* Use the count layer to verify.

**Practice item:** On the unbalance layer, point to the equation after a coefficient is removed and explain aloud why it no longer matches reality.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What makes an equation balanced, and why are coefficients (not subscripts) the tools for balancing?
