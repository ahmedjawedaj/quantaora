# Endothermic Reaction

*Chemistry > Chemical Reactions > Energy Changes > Reactions and energy > Endothermic Reaction*

An endothermic reaction absorbs energy from its surroundings, usually as heat — like a cold pack getting cold or a plant using sunlight. It is the opposite of exothermic.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a cracked cold pack on one side and a small leaf on the other, with energy arrows flowing INTO both. Tap the `hs-endothermic-pack` hotspot to open its card. The energy arrows coming in show the absorption.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the scene and zoom in. Toggle the 'energy in' layer to watch energy waves flow from the surroundings INTO the reaction — the pack getting colder as heat is pulled out. Toggle the 'energy levels' layer to see a simple bar — the reactants' energy low, the products' high, with the gap being the energy pulled in.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-endothermic-pack` to label the absorbing reaction, `hs-endothermic-energy` to label the energy flowing in, and `hs-endothermic-levels` to label the energy rise from reactants to products.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what an endothermic reaction does: it absorbs energy because the products hold more energy than the reactants. The extra is pulled in from the surroundings — which is why cold packs freeze and why plants need sunlight. It is the opposite of exothermic.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the energy-levels layer on, predict whether the products hold more or less energy than the reactants.

## 5. See where it belongs

An endothermic reaction belongs to the family of **chemical reactions**. From there it connects to the **reactants** that pull in the energy, across to **photosynthesis** (the biggest example), and to its opposite partner, the **exothermic reaction**.

## 6. Compare it

Compare an **endothermic reaction** with an **exothermic one**. Endothermic ABSORBS energy — it cools its surroundings, like a cold pack. Exothermic RELEASES energy — it warms its surroundings, like a fire. One takes in; the other gives out.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *You crack a cold pack and it turns freezing cold. Is the reaction inside absorbing or releasing energy, and how do you know?* Use the energy-in layer to reason it through.

**Practice item:** On the energy-levels layer, point to the energy gap and explain aloud where the missing energy comes from.
