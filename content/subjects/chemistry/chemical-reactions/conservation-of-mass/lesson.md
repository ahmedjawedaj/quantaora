# Conservation Of Mass

*Chemistry > Chemical Reactions > Reading Reactions > The quantities > Conservation Of Mass*

The law of conservation of mass says matter is never created or destroyed in a chemical reaction — the same atoms are rearranged. Mass in equals mass out.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: two identical scales floating side by side. On the left scale, the reactant molecules; on the right, the product molecules. Tap the `hs-mass-scales` hotspot to open its card. The scales are balanced — the mass is equal.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the scales and zoom in. Toggle the 'count' layer to see every atom split out and counted — the same tally on both sides. Toggle the 'rearrange' layer to watch the reactant atoms break apart and reassemble as products — same atoms, new arrangement.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-mass-scales` to label the balanced scales, `hs-mass-count` to label the equal atom counts, and `hs-mass-rearrange` to label the atoms being rearranged.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through the law: matter is never created or destroyed in a reaction — atoms just rearrange. The products contain exactly the atoms the reactants provided. Weigh everything together and the two sides match. That is why equations must be balanced.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the count layer on, predict whether the product side will have more atoms, fewer, or the same as the reactants.

## 5. See where it belongs

Conservation of mass belongs to every **chemical reaction**. From there it connects to the **balanced equation** that obeys it, the **coefficients** that enforce it, and the **reactants** and **products** whose masses it equates.

## 6. Compare it

Compare **conservation of mass** with **balancing an equation**. Conservation of mass is the law of nature — matter cannot appear or vanish. Balancing is the written skill — choosing coefficients so the equation matches the law. The law is the truth; balancing is how we write it.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *A log burns to ash that is lighter than the log. Where did the missing mass go?* Use the model's reasoning — the atoms went into smoke and gases, they did not vanish.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to an atom on the product side and explain aloud which reactant atom it used to be.
