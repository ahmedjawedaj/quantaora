# Coefficient

*Chemistry > Chemical Reactions > Reading Reactions > The quantities > Coefficient*

A coefficient is the number written in front of a chemical formula that tells how many units of that substance take part in a reaction. It is the key to balancing equations.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: an equation floating in space — `2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O` — with the numbers in front of the formulas highlighted. Tap the `hs-coefficient-number` hotspot to open its card. Those front numbers are the coefficients.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the equation. Toggle the 'count' layer to see each formula expand into its actual molecules — the coefficient 2 turns into two water molecules. Toggle the 'balance' layer to watch the atom counts on each side match up, side by side.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-coefficient-number` to label a coefficient, `hs-coefficient-multiplies` to label what it multiplies, and `hs-coefficient-balance` to label the balancing it achieves.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what a coefficient does: the number in front of a formula counts whole units and multiplies every atom inside. `2H₂O` means two water molecules — 4 hydrogens, 2 oxygens. Choosing the right coefficients makes every atom match on both sides — that is balancing.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the count layer on, predict how many hydrogen atoms are in `2H₂O` — one molecule or both.

## 5. See where it belongs

A coefficient belongs in a **chemical equation**, in front of each formula. From there it connects to the **balanced equation** it helps create and the **conservation of mass** it enforces.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a **coefficient** with a **subscript**. The coefficient is the big number in front — it counts whole units. The subscript is the small number inside — it counts atoms within one unit. `2H₂O`: the 2 means two molecules, the ₂ means two hydrogens each.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *In `2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O`, how many hydrogen atoms are on each side?* Use the count layer to count and verify the balance.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to a coefficient and explain aloud why it multiplies every atom in its formula.
