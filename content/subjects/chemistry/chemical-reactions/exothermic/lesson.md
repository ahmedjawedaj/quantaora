# Exothermic Reaction

*Chemistry > Chemical Reactions > Energy Changes > Reactions and energy > Exothermic Reaction*

An exothermic reaction releases energy to its surroundings, usually as heat or light — like a fire burning or a hand warmer heating up.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a small campfire scene — a burning log with reactant molecules floating above it. Tap the `hs-exothermic-flame` hotspot to open its card. The heat and light streaming out are the released energy.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the scene and zoom in. Toggle the 'energy out' layer to watch glowing energy waves stream from the reaction into the surroundings. Toggle the 'energy levels' layer to see a simple bar — the reactants' energy high, the products' low, with the difference released outward.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-exothermic-flame` to label the releasing reaction, `hs-exothermic-energy` to label the escaping energy, and `hs-exothermic-levels` to label the energy drop from reactants to products.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what an exothermic reaction does: it releases energy because the reactants hold more energy than the products. The extra energy leaves as heat and light, warming the surroundings. Fires, hand warmers, and your body's metabolism are all exothermic.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the energy-levels layer on, predict whether the products hold more or less energy than the reactants.

## 5. See where it belongs

An exothermic reaction belongs to the family of **chemical reactions**. From there it connects to the **reactants** that release the energy, across to **combustion** (burning), and to its opposite partner, the **endothermic reaction**.

## 6. Compare it

Compare an **exothermic reaction** with an **endothermic one**. Exothermic RELEASES energy — it warms its surroundings, like a fire or hand warmer. Endothermic ABSORBS energy — it cools its surroundings, like a cold pack. One gives out; the other takes in.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *A hand warmer gets hot when you crack it. Is the reaction inside releasing or absorbing energy, and how do you know?* Use the energy-out layer to reason it through.

**Practice item:** On the energy-levels layer, point to the energy drop and explain aloud where the released energy goes.
