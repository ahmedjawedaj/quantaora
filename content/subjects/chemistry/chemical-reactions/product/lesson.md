# Product

*Chemistry > Chemical Reactions > Reading Reactions > The starting materials > Product*

A product is a new substance created by a chemical reaction — written on the right side of the equation. Water is the product when hydrogen and oxygen react.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: the same miniature equation — reactants on the left, an arrow, and on the right a newly formed water molecule. Tap the `hs-product-made` hotspot to open its card. The molecule on the right is the product.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the equation and zoom into the right side. Toggle the 'form' layer to watch the water molecule assemble from the reactant atoms, atom by atom. Toggle the 'atoms' layer to highlight how every atom in the product came from the reactants — none are new.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-product-made` to label the product, `hs-product-form` to label it forming, and `hs-product-atoms` to label the atoms it inherits from the reactants.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what a product does: it is the new substance the reaction creates, on the right of the arrow. Its atoms are exactly the reactant atoms, rearranged — so products weigh exactly what the reactants weighed. New substance, same matter.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the atoms layer on, predict whether the product contains any atoms that were not present in the reactants.

## 5. See where it belongs

A product belongs in a **chemical equation**, on the right of the arrow. From there it connects back to the **reactants** that provided its atoms, and to **conservation of mass** — the rule that product mass equals reactant mass.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a **product** with a **reactant**. A product is the new substance created on the right of the arrow — the dish. A reactant is the starting material consumed on the left — the ingredients. The reaction turns one into the other.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *In hydrogen + oxygen → water, what is the product, and where do its atoms come from?* Point to the right side and trace the atoms back.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to an atom in the water product and explain aloud which reactant it came from.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What is a product, and why do its atoms match the reactants' atoms?
