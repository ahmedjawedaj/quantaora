# Metal

*Chemistry > Periodic Table > Reading the Periodic Table > The great elemental families > Metal*

A metal is an element that is shiny, malleable, and an excellent conductor of heat and electricity. About 80% of the elements are metals, filling the left and middle of the periodic table.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a polished metal sample — a smooth ingot — floating above a section of the periodic table with the metal region highlighted. Tap the `hs-metal-sample` hotspot to open its card. Notice the gleam on its surface.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the ingot and watch the light move across its polished face. Toggle the 'free electrons' layer to see tiny dots drifting freely through the metal — the loose electrons that carry electricity. Toggle the 'table region' layer to highlight where metals sit on the table.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-metal-sample` to label the metal ingot, `hs-metal-shine` to label its luster, `hs-metal-conductivity` to label the free electrons that conduct, and `hs-metal-region` to label the metal region of the table.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through how a metal works: its atoms release loose outer electrons that wander freely through the metal, which is why metals shine, bend without shattering, and conduct heat and electricity so well. In reactions, metals tend to lose those electrons and become positive ions.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the free-electrons layer on, predict whether the loose electrons would be moving or sitting still inside the metal — then watch.

## 5. See where it belongs

Metals belong to the **periodic table** as the great family filling its left side and middle. From there they connect downward to the loose **electrons** that create their properties, and across to the **ionic bonds** they form with nonmetals.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a **metal** with a **nonmetal**. Metals are shiny, malleable, and conductive; nonmetals are dull, often brittle, and poor conductors. Metals sit left and center on the table; nonmetals sit on the right. They are the table's two great opposite personalities.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Why are the wires in your house made of copper, a metal, instead of sulfur, a nonmetal?* Point to the conductivity layer and explain the metal's advantage.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to the ingot and the free electrons, and explain aloud two reasons metal makes good electrical wiring.
