# Alkali Metal

*Chemistry > Periodic Table > Reading the Periodic Table > Famous groups > Alkali Metal*

The alkali metals are Group 1's soft, shiny, explosively reactive family. Their whole personality comes from one lonely valence electron they are desperate to give away.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a shiny soft-metal cube floating above a periodic table with the first column highlighted. Tap the `hs-alkali-column` hotspot to open its card. Notice the column sits at the far left of the table.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the metal cube and watch the light catch its fresh shine. Toggle the 'one electron' layer to show a sodium atom with a single electron on its outer shell, highlighted. Toggle the 'reaction test' layer to watch a small drop of the metal meet water — with care, it skitters and gives off heat.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-alkali-column` to label Group 1, `hs-alkali-valence` to label the single valence electron, and `hs-alkali-reactivity` to label the water reaction.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through why alkali metals react so eagerly: each has exactly one valence electron, and losing it leaves a full, stable outer shell. Because giving away one electron is fast and easy, the reaction is fast and violent — sodium skitters on water, potassium can catch fire.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the one-electron layer on, predict how many valence electrons you think an alkali metal atom has — then count them.

## 5. See where it belongs

The alkali metals belong to the **periodic table** as **Group 1**, the first column. From there they connect down to the single **valence electron** that drives them, and across to the **+1 ions** they form and the **bonds** they build.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the alkali metals with the **noble gases**. Alkali metals have one lonely outer electron and explode into action to be rid of it. Noble gases have full outer shells and almost never react. One lonely electron versus a full shell — the two poles of reactivity.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Why do chemists store potassium under oil instead of leaving it in the air?* Point to the reaction-test layer and explain what the metal would do if it met air or water.

**Practice item:** On the one-electron layer, point to the single valence electron and explain aloud why losing it makes the atom calm and stable.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** How many valence electrons does an alkali metal have, and why does that make the family so reactive?
