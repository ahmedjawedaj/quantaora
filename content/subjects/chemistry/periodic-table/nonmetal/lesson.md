# Nonmetal

*Chemistry > Periodic Table > Reading the Periodic Table > The great elemental families > Nonmetal*

A nonmetal is an element that is dull, often brittle, and a poor conductor of heat and electricity. Nonmetals live in the upper right of the periodic table and include the gases you breathe.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a collection of nonmetal samples — a matte lump of sulfur, a dark block of carbon, and a small cloud of gas — floating above a periodic table with the nonmetal region highlighted. Tap the `hs-nonmetal-samples` hotspot to open the sample card. Notice the dull, matte surfaces.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the samples. Toggle the 'brittle test' layer to drop a small mallet on the sulfur lump and watch it shatter into fragments instead of bending. Toggle the 'table region' layer to highlight where nonmetals sit on the table, and the 'gas state' layer to see the oxygen cloud drift.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-nonmetal-samples` to label the samples, `hs-nonmetal-brittle` to label the shattering sulfur, and `hs-nonmetal-region` to label the nonmetal region of the table.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what defines a nonmetal: dull surfaces, brittle solids or gases, and poor conduction of heat and electricity. In bonding, nonmetals tend to gain electrons or share them in covalent bonds, forming molecules.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the brittle-test layer on, predict whether the sulfur lump will bend like a metal or shatter — then watch the mallet.

## 5. See where it belongs

Nonmetals belong to the **periodic table** as the family on its upper right. From there they connect to the **gases** and brittle solids in the world around you, and to the **covalent bonds** they form with each other.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a **nonmetal** with a **metal**. Nonmetals are dull, brittle or gaseous, and poor conductors. Metals are shiny, bendable, and conductive. Nonmetals sit on the right of the table; metals on the left and middle. Two opposite families in every way.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Oxygen and nitrogen are nonmetals. Why is the air you breathe mostly gases, while copper and iron are solid metal bars?* Point to the samples and connect their family to their state.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to the dull sulfur lump and explain aloud two ways it is the opposite of a polished metal ingot.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** Name three properties of nonmetals, and explain where they sit on the periodic table.
