# Noble Gas

*Chemistry > Periodic Table > Reading the Periodic Table > Famous groups > Noble Gas*

The noble gases are Group 18's famously unreactive family. Every one of them has a completely full outer shell — which is exactly why they almost never react.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a floating balloon and a softly glowing neon tube above a periodic table with the last column highlighted. Tap the `hs-noble-gas-column` hotspot to open its card. Notice the column sits at the far right of the table.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the scene. Toggle the 'shell view' layer to show a helium atom's single full shell and a neon atom's full outer shell — every electron space filled. Toggle the 'reaction test' layer to drop a hot spark near the gas and watch it refuse to react.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-noble-gas-column` to label Group 18, `hs-noble-gas-full-shell` to label the full outer shell, and `hs-noble-gas-gases` to label the balloon and neon tube made from these gases.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through why noble gases behave as they do: their outer shell is completely full — 2 for helium, 8 for the rest — and a full shell is the most stable arrangement possible. With nothing to gain or lose, the atoms have no reason to bond, so the gases almost never react.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the shell-view layer on, predict whether the outer shell will be completely filled or partly empty — then count the electrons.

## 5. See where it belongs

The noble gases belong to the **periodic table** as **Group 18**, the final column. From there they connect down to the **electron shells** that make them stable, and across to their everyday uses like **balloons** and **neon signs**.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the noble gases with the **alkali metals**. The noble gases have full outer shells and almost never react. The alkali metals have a single lonely outer electron and react explosively. Full versus empty, calm versus frenzy — the two poles of reactivity.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Why is helium safe to pump into a balloon that floats up into the sky, where it could meet sparks or heat?* Point to the reaction-test layer and explain why noble gases stay calm.

**Practice item:** On the shell view, point to the full outer shell and explain aloud why there is no room — or reason — for a noble gas atom to bond.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What is special about a noble gas's outer shell, and how does that make the family behave?
