# Atomic Mass

*Chemistry > Periodic Table > Reading the Periodic Table > The numbers on an element card > Atomic Mass*

Atomic mass is the average mass of an element's atoms, in atomic mass units. It is usually a decimal because it averages across all the element's isotopes.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: an element card (carbon) floating with a small nucleus cluster beside it. Tap the `hs-atomic-mass` hotspot to open its card. Notice the atomic mass printed below the atomic number — it reads 12.011, a decimal.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model and zoom into the nucleus cluster. Toggle the 'mass balance' layer to watch a little scale weigh the cluster — the protons and neutrons each count as about 1 unit. Toggle the 'isotope mix' layer to see a row of carbon atoms with slightly different masses (carbon-12, -13, -14), showing what the average is built from.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-atomic-mass` to label the mass on the card, `hs-mass-units` to label the atomic mass unit scale, and `hs-proton-neutron-weight` to label the protons and neutrons that make up the weight.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what atomic mass does: it weighs the protons and neutrons (each about 1 amu), then averages across the element's isotopes, weighted by how common each is. That is why carbon's mass is 12.011 instead of exactly 12.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the isotope-mix layer on, predict whether the heaviest carbon isotope would raise or lower the average mass compared with 12 — then look.

## 5. See where it belongs

Atomic mass belongs on every **element card** in the **periodic table**, printed beside the atomic number. From there it connects downward to the **protons** and **neutrons** that supply the weight, and across to the **isotopes** that the number averages.

## 6. Compare it

Compare **atomic mass** with **atomic number**. Atomic number counts protons — a clean whole number that names the element. Atomic mass averages the mass of all the element's atoms — a decimal that describes weight. Identity versus weight: the two numbers on the card do different jobs.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *A carbon atom has 6 protons and 7 neutrons. About how many amu does it weigh — and would that make it lighter or heavier than the average carbon atom?* Use the mass-balance layer to reason it out.

**Practice item:** On the isotope-mix row, point to the most common isotope and explain aloud why the average mass lands so close to 12.
