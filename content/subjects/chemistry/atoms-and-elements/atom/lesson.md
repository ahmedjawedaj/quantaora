# Atom

*Chemistry > Atoms And Elements > The Atom > The smallest piece of matter > Atom*

An atom is the smallest particle of an element that is still that element — a nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by shells of electrons. Everything is built from atoms.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a large, mostly-empty sphere with a tiny nucleus at the center and electrons circling far out. Tap the `hs-atom-structure` hotspot to open its card. This whole structure is one atom.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the atom and zoom into the tiny nucleus. Toggle the 'build-a-molecule' layer to watch two atoms bond into a molecule. Toggle the 'zoom' layer to pull the camera from a fingertip down to the atom itself — feel how small atoms really are.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-atom-structure` to label the whole atom, `hs-atom-nucleus` to label the dense core, and `hs-atom-electron-cloud` to label the electrons orbiting far outside.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what an atom is: the smallest particle that is still its element, made of a tiny positive nucleus plus shells of electrons orbiting in mostly-empty space. Atoms bond into molecules, and molecules build everything.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the build-a-molecule layer on, predict what you get when two atoms of hydrogen bond with one atom of oxygen.

## 5. See where it belongs

The atom belongs to the **element** — a substance made of one kind of atom. From there it connects up to **molecules**, which atoms build by bonding, and down to the **nucleus**, **protons**, and **electron shells** inside it.

## 6. Compare it

Compare an **atom** with an **element**. An atom is the single smallest particle of a substance — one brick. An element is the pure substance made of only one kind of atom — the whole wall. You can hold gold as an element; you can only imagine a single gold atom.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Why is an atom mostly empty space, and where is its mass?* Point to the nucleus and the electron cloud, and reason about what sits where.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to the nucleus and explain aloud why it holds nearly all the atom's mass even though the cloud is huge.
