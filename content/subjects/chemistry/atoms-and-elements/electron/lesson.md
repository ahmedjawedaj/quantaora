# Electron

*Chemistry > Atoms And Elements > Inside The Atom > Subatomic particles > Electron*

An electron is a tiny, negatively charged particle that orbits the nucleus in shells. It is the particle that does chemistry — the one shared, lost, or gained in every reaction.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a small nucleus at the center with a large shell-like cloud around it, and small teal dots circling through it. Tap the `hs-electron-cloud` hotspot to open its card. The teal dots are electrons.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the atom and watch the electrons circle in their shells. Toggle the 'shells' layer to see the electron paths resolve into clear ring layers. Toggle the 'bonding' layer to watch an outer electron reach out and share with another atom — chemistry in action.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-electron-cloud` to label the electron cloud, `hs-electron-shell` to label a shell layer, and `hs-electron-bonding` to label the outer electron doing chemistry.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what an electron does: it carries a -1 charge, orbits the nucleus in shells far outside, and is the particle rearranged in every chemical reaction — shared, given away, or grabbed. The outermost electrons, the valence electrons, decide bonding.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the bonding layer on, predict which electrons — inner or outer — you think will reach out to the other atom.

## 5. See where it belongs

An electron belongs in the **electron shell** — the layer it orbits in. From there it connects to the **proton** it is attracted to, and to the **valence electrons** that drive bonding and the **ions** formed when electrons are gained or lost.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the **electron** with the **proton**. The electron is negative and orbits far outside the nucleus; the proton is positive and packed inside. The electron is about 1,836 times lighter, yet it is the particle that does the chemistry.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *An atom's outermost shell has one electron. If that atom gives the electron away, what happens to its charge?* Use the bonding layer to follow the electron leaving.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to an electron in the outermost shell and explain aloud why it is the one that gets involved in reactions.
