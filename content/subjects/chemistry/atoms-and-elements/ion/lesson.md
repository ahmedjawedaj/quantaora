# Ion

*Chemistry > Atoms And Elements > Atoms That Vary > Variations of an atom > Ion*

An ion is an atom that has gained or lost electrons, giving it an electric charge. Sodium becomes Na⁺ by losing an electron; chlorine becomes Cl⁻ by gaining one.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a neutral sodium atom and a neutral chlorine atom side by side, each with its shell rings and electrons. Tap the `hs-ion-pair` hotspot to open its card. Now notice the electrons.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the atoms. Toggle the 'transfer' layer to watch sodium's lone outer electron glide across to chlorine. Toggle the 'charge' layer to see the + over sodium and − over chlorine appear, and the two atoms pull together into an ionic bond.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-ion-pair` to label the atom pair, `hs-ion-loses` to label sodium losing an electron and becoming positive, and `hs-ion-gains` to label chlorine gaining one and becoming negative.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through how an ion forms: an atom becomes an ion by gaining or losing electrons. Losing electrons leaves more protons than electrons → positive (Na⁺). Gaining electrons leaves more electrons than protons → negative (Cl⁻). Opposite ions then attract and build salts.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the charge layer on, predict whether sodium becomes positive or negative after losing an electron.

## 5. See where it belongs

An ion belongs to an **element** — it is a charged version of that element's atoms. From there it connects to the **electrons** that get gained or lost, and across to **ionic bonds**, which form between opposite-charged ions.

## 6. Compare it

Compare an **ion** with an **isotope**. An ion has a different electron count — same element, different charge. An isotope has a different neutron count — same element, different mass. Electrons change the charge; neutrons change the weight.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *After sodium gives its electron to chlorine, why do the two atoms attract?* Use the transfer and charge layers to trace the exchange and the attraction.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to the electron that moves and explain aloud why sodium becomes positive when it loses it.
