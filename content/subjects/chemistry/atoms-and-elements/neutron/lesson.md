# Neutron

*Chemistry > Atoms And Elements > Inside The Atom > Subatomic particles > Neutron*

A neutron is a particle in the nucleus that carries no electric charge. It adds mass to the atom, and its count is what varies between isotopes.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a cutaway nucleus with gray neutrons and red protons bundled together. Tap the `hs-neutron-nucleus` hotspot to open its card. The gray particles are the neutrons — notice they carry no charge marker at all.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the cutaway and zoom in. Toggle the 'mass' layer to see the neutrons' weight compared against the tiny electron outside — the neutrons make the atom heavy. Toggle the 'isotopes' layer to see two carbon atoms side by side, one with 6 neutrons and one with 8 — same element, different mass.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-neutron-nucleus` to label the neutrons in the nucleus, `hs-neutron-neutral` to label their zero charge, and `hs-neutron-isotopes` to label the isotope pair.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what a neutron does: it carries no charge, it adds mass to the atom, and its count is the only one that can change without changing the element — giving us isotopes like carbon-12 and carbon-14. It also helps glue the nucleus together.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the isotope layer on, predict whether the two carbon atoms are still both carbon if they have different neutron counts.

## 5. See where it belongs

A neutron belongs in the **nucleus**, beside the protons. From there it connects to the **isotopes** its count creates, and to **atomic mass**, which includes the neutrons' weight.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the **neutron** with the **proton**. They live in the same nucleus and weigh about the same, but the proton carries a +1 charge and defines the element, while the neutron carries no charge at all and only changes the mass.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are both carbon. What is different about them, and why are they both still carbon?* Compare the two atoms in the isotope layer.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to a neutron and explain aloud why changing its count does not change the element.
