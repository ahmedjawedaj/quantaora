# Isotope

*Chemistry > Atoms And Elements > Atoms That Vary > Variations of an atom > Isotope*

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons. Same protons, same element — different mass.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: two carbon atoms side by side — each with the same red protons, but one with 6 gray neutrons and the other with 8. Tap the `hs-isotope-pair` hotspot to open its card. Both are carbon.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the two atoms and compare their nuclei. Toggle the 'count' layer to highlight and count the protons and neutrons in each. Toggle the 'uses' layer to see icons for carbon-14's famous uses — dating bones, medicine, and power.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-isotope-pair` to label the isotope pair, `hs-isotope-protons` to label the identical protons, and `hs-isotope-neutrons` to label the differing neutrons.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what an isotope is: same proton count (so same element), different neutron count (so different mass). Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are both carbon — the same chemistry, but carbon-14 is heavier and radioactive, which is exactly why it can date ancient bones.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the count layer on, predict which part of the two atoms is different — protons, neutrons, or electrons?

## 5. See where it belongs

An isotope belongs to an **element** — it is a version of that element's atoms. From there it connects to the **neutron** count that varies and the **proton** count that stays fixed, and across to **atomic mass**, the average the periodic table lists.

## 6. Compare it

Compare an **isotope** with an **ion**. An isotope varies the neutron count — same element, different mass. An ion varies the electron count — same element, different charge. Neutrons change the weight; electrons change the charge. Different particles, different effects.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Carbon-12 has 6 neutrons and carbon-14 has 8. Are they the same element? What is actually different about them?* Count and compare the two nuclei.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to the identical protons in both atoms and explain aloud why both isotopes are still carbon.
