# Proton

*Chemistry > Atoms And Elements > Inside The Atom > Subatomic particles > Proton*

A proton is a positively charged particle in the nucleus of an atom. The number of protons decides which element an atom is.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a cutaway view of an atom — the dense nucleus at the center with its red protons, and the electron cloud far outside. Tap the `hs-proton-nucleus` hotspot to open its card. The red particles inside the core are the protons.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the cutaway and zoom into the nucleus. Toggle the 'proton count' layer to highlight the protons one at a time and count them — this is the atom's identity number. Toggle the 'pulls electrons' layer to see the protons' positive charge tugging the electron cloud toward the nucleus.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-proton-nucleus` to label the protons in the nucleus, `hs-proton-charge` to label their positive charge, and `hs-proton-count` to label the count that defines the element.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what a proton does: it carries a +1 charge, lives in the nucleus, and its count defines the element. Six protons make carbon, ninety-two make uranium — change the count and you change the element. The positive charge also pulls the negative electrons, holding the atom together.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the proton-count layer on, predict what element an atom with 6 protons is — then count and check.

## 5. See where it belongs

A proton belongs in the **nucleus**, the atom's core. From there it connects upward to the **element** it defines, and across to the periodic table's **atomic number**, which is just the proton count.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the **proton** with the **neutron**. Both live in the nucleus and weigh nearly the same, but a proton carries a +1 charge and defines the element, while a neutron carries no charge and only adds mass.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *An atom has 8 protons. What element is it, and why?* Count the highlighted protons and find the element on the atomic number line.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to a proton and explain aloud why its count — not its mass — is what makes an element what it is.
