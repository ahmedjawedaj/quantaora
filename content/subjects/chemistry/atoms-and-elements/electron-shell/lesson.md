# Electron Shell

*Chemistry > Atoms And Elements > Inside The Atom > The atom's core > Electron Shell*

An electron shell is a layer around the nucleus where electrons orbit. Each shell sits at a fixed distance from the core, and the outermost shell decides the atom's chemistry.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a sodium atom with its nucleus at center and two clear ring layers around it, electrons sitting on each ring. Tap the `hs-shell-layers` hotspot to open its card. The rings are the shells.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the atom and zoom in on the rings. Toggle the 'fill order' layer to watch electrons fill the inner shell first, then the outer one. Toggle the 'reactivity' layer to highlight the single lonely electron on the outer shell — and see how a full outer shell (like neon's) would stay calm.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-shell-layers` to label the shells, `hs-shell-inner` to label the inner shell, and `hs-shell-outer` to label the outermost shell and its bonding electrons.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what shells do: they organize electrons into layers at fixed distances, they fill inner-first then outer, and the outermost shell — with its valence electrons — decides how the atom reacts. Full outer shell means calm; a lonely outer electron means desperate to bond.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you toggle the reactivity layer on, predict whether sodium's one outer electron makes it reactive or calm.

## 5. See where it belongs

The shell belongs around the **nucleus** of the **atom**. From there it connects to the **electrons** that live in it, and to the **valence electrons** on its outermost layer that drive bonding.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the **electron shell** with the **nucleus**. The nucleus is the tiny dense core holding the mass; the shells are the huge layered space around it holding the electrons. One is the engine, the other is where chemistry happens.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *Neon has a full outer shell of 8 electrons. Why does it almost never react with anything?* Use the reactivity layer to compare neon's calm full shell with sodium's lonely electron.

**Practice item:** On the model, point to the outermost shell and explain aloud why its electrons — not the inner ones — decide how the atom bonds.
