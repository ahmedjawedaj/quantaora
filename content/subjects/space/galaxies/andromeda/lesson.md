# Andromeda

*Space > Galaxies > Our Galaxy > Neighbor galaxies > Andromeda*

The galaxy that's coming our way — a trillion stars, 2.5 million light-years off, falling toward us.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a large, smooth spiral glowing softly — warmer golden core, hazy blue arms, no bar. Toggle 'naked-eye view' to see it shrink to the faint oval patch you'd actually see in the sky.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model, then turn on the merger timeline: watch the Milky Way model slide in from the far side and pass through in a slow, harmless tumble. Toggle `hs-central-bulge` to see the bright heart, `hs-disk` to sweep the arms.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-central-bulge` for the bright hub, `hs-spiral-disk` for the smooth classic spiral, `hs-satellite-galaxy` for its two small companion galaxies (M32 and M110), and `hs-access-distance` for the 2.5-million-light-year marker.

## 4. Explain it

Walk the story: Andromeda is the nearest large spiral — about 2.5 million light-years away, hosting ~a trillion stars → no bar, but a very bright bulge → seen without a telescope it is a faint oval smudge, showing that galaxies exist far beyond ours → the galaxy is gravitationally bound to ours and falling in at ~110 km/s → in ~4 billion years the pair merges into one galaxy, stars passing stars harmlessly in the empty space between.

## 5. Predict it

Before you run the timeline: *A galaxy with a trillion stars meets another with 400 billion. Predict how many stars will actually crash into each other.* The readout says near zero — the inter-star space is that vast.

**Prediction prompt:** Andromeda spans twice the Moon's width in our sky. Predict what fraction of it you see when it passes near: half, a sliver, or the whole glowing spindle — then check.

## 6. Belongs to

Andromeda is the Milky Way's counterpart in the Neighbor galaxies group of the Galaxies branch — spiral type, Local Group member; it is the concrete 'someone else' in a universe of galaxon.

## 7. Compare it

Compare with the Milky Way component: which galaxy has a bar? Which has more stars? Which will absorb the other? The two make one story — the merger — told from both sides.

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Name three facts — distance, star count, and the merger schedule.

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain why stars will not collide even when two whole galaxies merge, and why we can see a trillion-star galaxy with the naked eye?
