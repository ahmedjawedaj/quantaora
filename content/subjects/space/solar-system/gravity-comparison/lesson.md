# Gravity Comparison

*Space > Solar System > Sun and Planets > Planets (gravitational comparison) > Gravity Comparison*

Your mass never changes. Your weight does — because weight is how hard gravity pulls on your mass, and gravity is different on every world.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a row of six low platforms, each topped with a small planet or Moon sphere and a simple astronaut figure. A scale readout floats beside the astronaut and updates as the figure hops from platform to platform.

## 2. Touch it

Move the astronaut onto the `hs-earth-scale` platform first — the readout shows 1.0 g, your home weight. Now hop onto `hs-jupiter-scale` and watch the number jump to 2.5 g. Then visit `hs-moon-scale` and feel the lightest platform of the row.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-moon-scale` to name the one-sixth-gravity platform, `hs-mars-scale` for the 0.38 g world, and `hs-saturn-scale` for the giant with the almost-Earth weight. Tap `hs-mass-weight-note` to label the little card explaining the difference between mass and weight.

## 4. Explain it

Walk through the numbers on each platform: Earth 1 g, Moon ~0.17 g, Mars 0.38 g, Saturn ~1.07 g, Jupiter 2.5 g, Sun ~28 g. Explain that the readout is weight — and that flipping the toggle to 'mass' shows the exact same number on every platform, because the astronaut's mass never changes.

## 5. Predict it

Before moving the astronaut to `hs-saturn-scale`, ask yourself: *Saturn is nine times wider than Earth — do you think you'd weigh nine times as much, less, or about the same?* Now hop over and check the readout.

**Prediction prompt:** Jupiter is the biggest planet by far. Predict its gravity number before you place the astronaut there — then compare your guess with the readout.

## 6. Belongs to

Gravity comparison belongs to the Solar System branch's portrait of the planets. The Planet Cards carry the same numbers as a data row; this model makes the rows feel physical. Gravity is the same force the Orbit Simulator draws as tug arrows — here it is measured at each world's surface instead.

## 7. Compare it

Compare `hs-saturn-scale` with `hs-earth-scale`. Saturn is vastly bigger and more massive than Earth, yet its gravity is almost identical. How can that be? Think about what 'surface' means on a gas giant, and what happens to gravity when the surface is much farther from the center of mass.

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** With the astronaut on Earth's platform, predict the weight readout for Mars, Jupiter, and the Moon — then hop across and check all three.

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain the difference between mass and weight, and name the platform where you'd weigh the most and the one where you'd weigh the least?
