# Distance From Sun Timeline

*Space > Solar System > Sun and Planets > Planets (distance order) > Distance From Sun Timeline*

One street, starting at the Sun. Walk it planet by planet and watch the gaps widen.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a long straight road with the Sun at the left end and eight small planet spheres standing along it at their real relative distances. A single AU ruler runs the full length of the road, and a small clock floats at the Sun.

## 2. Touch it

Walk the road with your finger or the camera. Start at `hs-sun-start` and note how tight the first four stops are — Mercury to Mars all fit before the road opens up. Then stretch the camera toward `hs-neptune-mark` at the far end and feel what 30 AU of emptiness looks like.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-earth-mark` to name the 1 AU stop, `hs-asteroid-belt-mark` to name the boundary rubble, and `hs-jupiter-mark` for the first giant after the gap. Tap `hs-au-ruler` to label the 0–30 ruler running the whole way.

## 4. Explain it

Walk the road stop by stop: Mercury 0.39 AU, Venus 0.72, Earth 1, Mars 1.52, the belt, Jupiter 5.2, Saturn 9.6, Uranus 19.2, Neptune 30. Point out the pattern — the stops get farther apart as you go, and the belt is the divide between the compact inner street and the empty outer road.

## 5. Predict it

Before you run the light counter, ask yourself: *Sunlight takes 8 minutes to reach Earth at 1 AU. How many minutes do you think it takes to reach Jupiter's stop at 5.2 AU?* Press play and watch the counter run along the ruler to check.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict which is wider — the Sun-to-Earth distance or the Mars-to-Jupiter gap — before the road scrolls past the belt.

## 6. Belongs to

This timeline is the distance half of the Solar System story: the Scale component measured the same distances, the Orbit Simulator turns them into years, and the Planet Cards write them down as facts. Here the distances alone are the lesson.

## 7. Compare it

Compare the walk from `hs-sun-start` to Mars with the walk from Jupiter to Neptune. The first walk visits four planets in 1.5 AU; the second visits four planets across 25 AU. Which neighborhood is crowded, and which is empty?

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Cover the labels and walk the road from memory, naming each planet as you pass its stop — then check your answers against the labels.

**Checkpoint:** Can you list the planets in order from the Sun, name the gap that divides the inner from the outer solar system, and describe how the spacing changes as you go outward?
