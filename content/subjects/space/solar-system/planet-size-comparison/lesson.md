# Planet Size Comparison

*Space > Solar System > Sun and Planets > Planets (size comparison) > Planet Size Comparison*

Photos of planets never line them up next to each other. This shelf does — all eight worlds at one scale, plus the Sun.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a long shelf with eight spheres standing on it in a row, Mercury at one end and Jupiter at the other, each with a small radius label. A much larger warm sphere floats behind the shelf — the Sun at the same scale.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom along the shelf. Stand next to `hs-earth` first — the reference sphere every other label compares against. Then walk the shelf to `hs-jupiter` and feel how the biggest planet towers over the row. Duck around to `hs-mercury` and see how small the smallest planet really is.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-earth` to label the 1 × reference, `hs-jupiter` for the 11 × giant, `hs-mercury` for the 0.38 × smallest, and `hs-sun` for the 109 × sphere behind the shelf. Tap `hs-volume-note` to label the card that explains why Jupiter holds 1,300 Earths, not 11.

## 4. Explain it

Walk the shelf left to right: Mercury 0.38 ×, Mars 0.53 ×, Venus 0.95 ×, Earth 1 ×, Neptune 3.9 ×, Uranus 4.0 ×, Saturn 9.1 ×, Jupiter 11 ×. Then explain the jump: radius multiplies, but volume multiplies by the radius cubed — 11 × 11 × 11 is about 1,300, which is why the volume toggle can pour over a thousand Earths into Jupiter.

## 5. Predict it

Before you look behind the shelf at `hs-sun`, ask yourself: *The Sun's radius is 109 times Earth's. If a sphere's volume grows with radius cubed, how many Earths do you think could fit inside the Sun?* Turn the toggle and compare your guess.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict how many Earths wide Jupiter is before you check its label.

## 6. Belongs to

Size comparison belongs to the Solar System branch's portrait of the Sun and planets — the same relative sizes the Planet Cards list as numbers and the same worlds the Scale component separates into a vast emptiness.

## 7. Compare it

Compare `hs-earth` with `hs-venus` — the two most similar-sized planets in the whole family — then compare `hs-jupiter` with `hs-mercury`. The Earth–Venus pair shows size says nothing about habitability; the Jupiter–Mercury pair shows how wide the planet size range really is.

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Cover the labels and try to order the shelf from smallest to largest from memory — then reveal the labels and check yourself.

**Checkpoint:** Can you name the largest and smallest planets, and explain why Jupiter holds over a thousand Earths when its radius is only 11 times bigger?
