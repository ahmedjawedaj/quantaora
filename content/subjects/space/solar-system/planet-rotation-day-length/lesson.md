# Planet Rotation and Day Length

*Space > Solar System > Sun and Planets > Planets (rotation and day length) > Planet Rotation and Day Length*

One day on a planet is one complete spin. These five spheres spin at their real speeds, side by side, so you can compare the clocks.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: five planet spheres floating in a row — Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, and Jupiter — each with a small rotating day counter beneath it. A fixed pointer dot sits on each sphere so you can actually see the surface carry past.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom into `hs-earth-spin` first: the pointer dot circles and the counter ticks past 24 hours as Earth makes one turn. Then speed up the clock at `hs-jupiter-spin` and watch its counter race — a full Jupiter day takes just 9.9 hours.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-mercury-spin` and `hs-venus-spin` to name the two strangest spinners. On Venus, watch `hs-retrograde-marker` — the dot travels the wrong way around the planet compared with all the others. Tap `hs-rotation-axis` to label the invisible rod each sphere turns around.

## 4. Explain it

Walk through one rotation of Earth alongside the day counter: 1 mark = 24 hours. Then recount Jupiter — mark 1 through 2.4 per Earth day. Explain that the very same 'mark rotations' logic is how astronomers measure days everywhere: count one full turn, call it one day. Venus simply turns the opposite way, which is why it's called retrograde.

## 5. Predict it

Before you fast-forward the counter at `hs-mercury-spin`, ask yourself: *Mercury is the smallest and closest planet — do you think its day is shorter or longer than Earth's?* Run it and be surprised.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict how many Earth days a single Mercury day will last before you check the counter.

## 6. Belongs to

Rotation is the 'day' half of the Solar System story; the ‘year’ half belongs to the Orbit Simulator. Day length is also the foundation of the Earth In Space branch's components — Rotation and Day and Night are the same process zoomed in to your home world.

## 7. Compare it

Put Earth next to Jupiter and compare the counters directly: Earth ticks a full-day mark every 24.0 hours, Jupiter every 9.9. Now compare Jupiter's counter to Venus's — the largest planet spins fast, yet Venus, nearly Earth's size, crawls backwards. Size tells you nothing about spin.

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** While Earth's counter ticks one full day, count how many ticks Jupiter's counter makes in the same real time. Then predict: about how long is one Jupiter day?

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain what makes a planet's day, name the fastest-spinning planet, and describe Venus's backwards spin?
