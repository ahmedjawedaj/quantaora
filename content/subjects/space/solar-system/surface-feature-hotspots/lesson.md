# Planet Surface Features

*Space > Solar System > Sun and Planets > Planets (surface landmarks) > Planet Surface Features*

Every world has a famous landmark. This gallery lines six of them up — each one teaching a different idea about how planets work.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: six floating worlds — Jupiter, Mars, Saturn, Earth, and the Moon — arranged around the view, each wearing glowing surface details. Bright hotspot dots mark the famous landmarks on each sphere.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the view toward `hs-great-red-spot` first and zoom into the swirling oval — compare its size with the Earth-reference disc floating beside it. Then swing over to Mars and inspect `hs-olympus-mons` rising above the red plains.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-great-red-spot` to name Jupiter's storm, `hs-olympus-mons` for the giant volcano, `hs-valles-marineris` for the canyon system, and `hs-saturn-rings` for the ring system. Tap `hs-moon-maria` to label the dark lava plains on the Moon's face.

## 4. Explain it

Work through what each feature is really made of: the Great Red Spot is moving gas, not a solid mark — a hurricane twice Earth's width. Olympus Mons is a shield volcano built by lava flows over billions of years, with no plate tectonics to move the hotspot. Valles Marineris is a rift canyon that records the planet's ancient history. Saturn's rings are trillions of separate ice chunks. The Moon's maria are frozen lava floods.

## 5. Predict it

Before zooming into `hs-saturn-rings`, ask yourself: *The rings look like solid sheets in pictures. If they were solid, how could they stay around Saturn without falling in? What do you think they're actually made of?* Now zoom in and see the chunks.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict how the height of Olympus Mons compares with Mount Everest before you check its label.

## 6. Belongs to

These landmarks belong to the Solar System branch's portrait of the planets — the same worlds the Planet Cards summarize, with the landmark details those cards can only mention by name.

## 7. Compare it

Compare `hs-great-red-spot` with a storm you know: Earth hurricanes. Both are swirling weather systems — but the Great Red Spot is wider than Earth and has lasted nearly two centuries, while Earth hurricanes last days to weeks. What does the comparison suggest about what keeps the Jovian storm going?

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Cover the labels and try to name each world's landmark from the hotspot dots alone — then reveal and check.

**Checkpoint:** Can you name three of the six landmarks, and explain what each one really is (storm, volcano, canyon, ring, ocean, lava plain)?
