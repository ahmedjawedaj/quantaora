# Planet Cards

*Space > Solar System > Sun and Planets > Planets (card catalog) > Planet Cards*

A planet card is like a baseball card, but for a world: a picture on one side, a list of stats on the other. Flip through the deck and every planet becomes a readable fact sheet.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a fan of eight floating cards, each showing a planet sphere and its stats. All the cards share the same rows, so any two can be compared directly.

## 2. Touch it

Flip the deck by dragging or tapping the card edges. Pull one card forward — like `hs-mercury-card` — and tilt it to read every row: composition, radius, distance, day, year, gravity, moons. Flip back and forth between `hs-earth-card` and `hs-jupiter-card` to see how wildly the numbers change.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-mercury-card` to name the smallest planet's card, `hs-jupiter-card` for the largest planet's, and `hs-saturn-card` for the ringed giant. Tap `hs-family-tag` to label the two family groups — rocky worlds and gas/ice giants — that the deck sorts itself into.

## 4. Explain it

Pick Earth's card, then walk the deck outward. Each step away from the Sun makes the year row longer (Mercury 88 days → Neptune about 165 years). Meanwhile the composition row splits the deck into two families: the four inner cards say "rocky world" and the four outer cards say "gas giant" or "ice giant." The radius row shows the outer family is enormous by comparison.

## 5. Predict it

Before you flip the deck to the last card, ask yourself: *Jupiter's day is about 9.9 hours and Earth's is 24 — do you think the biggest planet or the smallest planet has the longest year?* Now check Mercury's and Neptune's year rows.

**Prediction prompt:** Venus's day is about 117 Earth days long. Before you tap `hs-venus-card`, predict whether a year on Venus is longer or shorter than its own day.

## 6. Belongs to

The cards belong to the Solar System branch's catalog of the Sun and planets. Every other component in the branch is a deep dive into one of these rows: the Orbit Simulator animates the year row, the Gravity Comparison weighs the gravity row, and the Rotation component spins the day row.

## 7. Compare it

Put Earth's card next to Jupiter's card. Compare row by row: composition (rocky vs. gas), radius (1 × vs. 11 × Earth), day (24 h vs. 9.9 h), year (365 days vs. 12 years), gravity (1 g vs. 2.5 g). Which row surprises you the most? Why?

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Without flipping the cards, try to predict the gravity row for Mars and Saturn — then check your answer by opening those two cards side by side.

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain why the planets sort into two families, and name one way day length, year length, and gravity each vary across the deck?
