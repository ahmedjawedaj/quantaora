# Solar System Scale

*Space > Solar System > Sun and Planets > Solar system (scale model) > Solar System Scale*

If you drew the solar system with all the planets visible, you'd be drawing a lie — not on purpose, but because the real distances are so huge that the planets would be invisible specks. This model shows you both versions so you can feel the difference.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a large warm sphere for the Sun at one end with a line of small planets stretching away from it. A thin scale bar floats above the line, and a toggle switches the whole scene between two arrangements — sizes-only and true distance.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom along the planet line. In size-only mode, move to the `hs-sun` hotspot and feel how much bigger the Sun is than the tiny planet specks beyond it. Then toggle the scene to true-distance mode and watch the planets jump apart — Earth's dot barely moves while the outer planets recede far into the distance.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-sun` to label the Sun, then `hs-earth-speck` to label Earth's dot at true scale. Tap `hs-au-scale` to label the astronomical-unit scale bar, and `hs-distance-gap` to label the empty stretch between the inner and outer planets.

## 4. Explain it

Step along the line from the Sun outward: Mercury ~0.4 AU, Venus ~0.7 AU, Earth 1 AU, Mars ~1.5 AU, then a gap, then Jupiter ~5 AU, Saturn ~9.6 AU, Uranus ~19 AU, Neptune ~30 AU. The distance between Mars and Jupiter is wider than the whole distance from the Sun to Mars — and that's exactly where the asteroid belt lives.

## 5. Predict it

Before you toggle the model into true-distance mode, ask yourself: *If the planets keep their real relative sizes, do you think they will stay visible as small balls, or shrink to almost-invisible dots?* Watch what happens when you flip the toggle.

**Prediction prompt:** If sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth at 1 AU, about how long do you predict it takes to reach Neptune at 30 AU?

## 6. Belongs to

This model belongs to the Solar System branch's story of the Sun and planets. Scale is the foundation every other solar system component stands on — the Orbit Simulator animates planets along these same huge distances, and the Distance From Sun Timeline walks them out from the Sun one at a time.

## 7. Compare it

Compare the size-only view with the true-distance view side by side. Which view makes it easier to name the planets? Which view is more honest about what the solar system is actually like? Why do textbook diagrams almost always choose the first one?

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Use the light-travel-time counter on the `hs-au-scale` bar to check your prediction from step 5 — then try toggling the model back and forth and explain the difference you see between the two modes to a partner.

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain, in your own words, why a true-to-scale solar system model makes the planets almost invisible, and why astronomers measure distances in AU instead of kilometres?
