# Red Giant

*Space > Stars > Stellar Lifecycle > Post-main sequence > Red Giant*

The bloated, cooling endgame of a star — and, in about five billion years, the Sun's own story.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a huge, soft red-orange sphere with a dim, glowing center visible through the gaps in the diffuse shell. For contrast, the small yellow-white sphere sitting beside it marks the star's size when it was on the main sequence.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model, then use the slider to pull the shell away and reveal the interior: a bright, compact core at the center and a thin, fiery ring wrapped just outside it. Toggle `hs-inert-core` to see that the core itself is NOT burning.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-inert-core` to label the dead helium core, `hs-burning-shell` for the thin hydrogen-fusion rind just outside it, `hs-expanded-envelope` for the giant spread of gas, and `hs-red-photosphere` for the cooler, redder visible surface.

## 4. Explain it

Walk the story: the main-sequence core used up its hydrogen → without fusion, nothing props up the core → it contracts and heats → a thin shell around it starts burning the remaining hydrogen → that shell pours heat into the envelope → the envelope expands and its gas spreads thin → thin, spread gas is cooler → the surface drops to ~3,000 K and glows red. Nothing is added — this is the same star, re-dressed.

## 5. Predict it

Before you check the animation, ask yourself: *more fusion energy inside — bigger star or smaller?* Watch the run: when shell burning kicks in, the star swells, not shrinks.

**Prediction prompt:** A balloon of gas that expands grows cooler. Predict which is hotter: a main-sequence surface at 5,800 K or the giant's surface — then check the temperature readout on the model.

## 6. Belongs to

The red giant is the post-main-sequence chapter of the Stars branch: a Main Sequence star becomes one; the exposed core left after its outer into space becomes a White Dwarf.

## 7. Compare it

Set the model side by side with the main-sequence star: same fuel, same gravity, only the core's condition has changed. Which is steady — the one with the burning core, or the one with the dead core and the burning shell? Which one's surface is redder?

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Point to the core and explain why it is not burning in the red giant stage, and name the layer that burns instead.

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain, with no help, why a star whose core has no more hydrogen swells instead of shrinking — and why it turns red?
