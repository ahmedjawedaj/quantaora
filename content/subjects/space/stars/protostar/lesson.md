# Protostar

*Space > Stars > Stellar Lifecycle > Star formation > Protostar*

A star isn't switched on — it's assembled. This is the assembly line.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a flattened disk of gas and dust seen slightly edge-on, a bright lump at its center, and a faint envelope of cloud still feeding material onto the disk from above and below. A dashed line marks the rotation axis through the center.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model to see the disk's flatness from above — notice how the whole system is one spinning plane. Then tilt it edge-on and look at the `hs-collapsing-core` hotspot: the small bright sphere at the center. Toggle the 'infall' layer and watch gas from the surrounding cloud rain down onto the disk.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-collapsing-core` to label the forming star, `hs-accretion-disk` for the planet nursery around it, and `hs-birth-cloud` for the surrounding cloud still feeding the disk. Tap `hs-rotation-axis` to label the spin line the whole system shares.

## 4. Explain it

Walk through the story: a cold cloud collapses under its own gravity → the collapse spins faster and flattens into a disk → the center gathers mass and heats up from gravitational contraction, glowing even though nothing is fusing yet → gas from the disk keeps falling onto the core → in a few million years the core hits ~10 million K and hydrogen fusion switches on.

## 5. Predict it

Before you pull the core's temperature readout, ask yourself: *The protostar will eventually become a star like the Sun. What single event — what change at the core — turns a protostar into a true star?* Then check what the readout says must happen.

**Prediction prompt:** If the disk is spinning faster as it collapses, where else in nature have you seen something spin faster as it shrinks — and why does that make the disk flat?

## 6. Belongs to

Protostars belong to the Stars branch's lifecycle chain: this is step one. The next component in the sequence is the Main Sequence Star, the stage this protostar will reach when its core ignites. The cloud that gives birth to it is a Nebula — a component in the Galaxies branch.

## 7. Compare it

Compare the bright center here with the main-sequence star's conditions: the protostar squeezes and glows from contracting; the star fuses and shines from core energy. Which stage produces its light instantly in the center — and which still has to wait for ignition?

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Without looking, explain why the disk is flat rather than a sphere, and why the star at the center isn't fusing yet.

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain what a protostar is, where its light comes from, and what has to happen at its core for it to become a main-sequence star?
