# Black Hole

*Space > Stars > Stellar Lifecycle > Stellar remnants > Black Hole*

Where gravity refuses to lose — the endpoint of the Stars branch's whole ladder.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a black disk in the middle of a hot, flat swirl of glowing gas. Behind it, the model shows the stars of a background field — and you can see the black hole's gravity lens bending them into arcs and doubling them. No point light shines from the disk itself.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the disk to see its glowing ring and the dark shadow inside. Toggle `hs-event-horizon` and the horizon will light up as a thin ring around the shadow. Then toggle `hs-accretion-disk` to feel the hot whirl — move your hand near the disk in the model and feel it warm as the gas falls faster.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-singularity` for the point at the center, `hs-event-horizon` for the no-return boundary, `hs-accretion-disk` for the hot infalling gas, and `hs-photon-ring` for the halo of bent light around the shadow.

## 4. Explain it

Walk the story: the heaviest star cores collapse past the neutron limit → nothing can stop the fall → matter squeezes to a point, the singularity → around it, gravity becomes so strong that escape speed exceeds light speed → the boundary where that happens is the event horizon, and nothing can ever cross back out → the hole reveals itself only through its surroundings: the accretion disk and the bent light of the stars behind it.

## 5. Predict it

Before you tap the escape-speed readout, ask yourself: *what speed would you need to escape a black hole's horizon — faster than light or slower?* Now check the readout: faster than light — which means nothing can leave.

**Prediction prompt:** A star with exactly the Sun's mass collapses. Predict whether it becomes a black hole — then check what the model's mass dial says about the Sun's fate (it stays a white dwarf).

## 6. Belongs to

The black hole is the last chapter of the Stars branch's remnant ladder: a Supernova creates the heaviest cores, a Neutron Star is the step before, and this is the final rung.

## 7. Compare it

Lay the black hole beside the neutron star: same collapsed core, one rung further. Which one has an event horizon? Which still shows a solid surface? Where would a feather and an anvil fall the same?

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Point to the shadow, the ring, and the disk, and say what each is.

**Checkpoint:** Explain in one minute why a black hole is black, what the event horizon is, and why the Sun cannot become one.
