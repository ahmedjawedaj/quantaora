# Main Sequence Star

*Space > Stars > Stellar Lifecycle > Main sequence > Main Sequence Star*

A main-sequence star is a permanent tug of war — gravity in, fusion pressure out. Most of what you know as 'a star' is this stage.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a yellow-white sphere sliced open on one side, revealing nested layers — a bright core at the center, a dense mid-layer, a churning outer shell, and the visible outer surface. Arrows float above the model: one set pointing inward, one pointing outward.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the sphere and find the two arrow sets — the inward gravity arrows and the outward pressure arrows — and notice they balance in size. Toggle the layers on/near `hs-core` to expose the fusion furnace interior, then switch to the `hs-convective-zone` hotspot and watch the boiling surface layer churn.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-core` to label the fusion furnace, `hs-radiative-zone` for the layer where energy crawls outward as repeatedly absorbed light, `hs-convective-zone` for the churning outer shell, and `hs-photosphere` for the visible 'surface.' Tap `hs-hydrostatic-balance` to label the balance itself.

## 4. Explain it

Walk through the engine step by step: at ~15 million K the core fuses hydrogen into helium, releasing energy → that energy heats the star from inside → the heated gas pushes outward and exactly matches the inward pull of gravity → the star holds a constant size → the energy eventually reaches the photosphere and escapes as light, which takes 8 minutes to reach Earth after its ten-thousand-year journey within the star.

## 5. Predict it

Before you check the color slider, ask yourself: *A star's color is a thermometer. Which end is hot — red or blue?* Slide to the blue end and check the temperature reading.

**Prediction prompt:** A star has 10 × the Sun's mass, so 10 × the fuel. Do you predict it lives 10 × longer, shorter, or about the same as the Sun?

## 6. Belongs to

The main sequence is the core chapter of the Stars branch's lifecycle: a Protostar becomes one at ignition; one runs out of core hydrogen and becomes a Red Giant. It is also the chapter the Sun lives now.

## 7. Compare it

Compare the balance here with the Red Giant's in the next component: in the main sequence, outward pressure exactly matches gravity and the size stays fixed. In the red giant, the core running dry breaks that balance and the star swells. Which star is in equilibrium — this one, or the giant?

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Without the hotspot labels, point to each layer and name what it does — core, radiative zone, convective zone, photosphere.

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain what balance holds a star the same size for billions of years, where fusion happens, and what color tells you about a star?
