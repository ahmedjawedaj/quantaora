# Neutron Star

*Space > Stars > Stellar Lifecycle > Stellar remnants > Neutron Star*

A city of pure neutron matter — the densest remnant that isn't a black hole.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a sphere the size of a marble glowing pale blue, with two bright beams sweeping out of its poles and rotating like a lighthouse. Around it, the faint spread of the supernova debris cloud glows pink and purple.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the star and watch the beams sweep. Toggle `hs-crust` to peel open the crust reveal, then push the slider to cut the sphere in half and look at the interior: a solid crust, a shimmering liquid interior. Tap the density button and feel your hand 'weight' a sugar cube at a billion tons.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-crust` for the iron-lattice surface, `hs-interior` for the neutron superfluid, `hs-beams` for the pulsar beams, and `hs-supernova-outflow` for the surrounding debris cloud.

## 4. Explain it

Walk the birth: a supernova crushes the core harder than anything else in the universe → protons and electrons merge into neutrons → the neutrons pile so tight they hold up the star against gravity → a ball 20 km across, with gravity's pull so strong near its surface that light bends around it. The beams you see are radio light spilled out the magnetic poles, sweeping the sky with every rotation.

## 5. Predict it

Before you start the spin animation, ask yourself: *When a spinning skater pulls her arms in, do she go faster or slower?* Now watch the star spin up as the model shrinks it from white-dwarf size down to city size.

**Prediction prompt:** A sugar cube of neutron star is claimed to weigh about a billion tons. Predict how a solid chunk of crust survives at that density — then check what the readout says about the lattice.

## 6. Belongs to

The neutron star is the remnant chapter of the Stars branch. A Supernova makes it, a Black Hole ends the same ladder, and a white dwarf is its lighter cousin.

## 7. Compare it

Put it beside the White Dwarf: same story — a dead core held up by pressure — but electrons here and neutrons there. Which has gravity strong enough to bend light near the surface? Which spins 30 times a second?

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Without the labels, point and name the crust, the superfluid, and the beams — and say what each does.

**Checkpoint:** Explain in one minute why a neutron star is denser than a white dwarf, and why pulsars tick like clocks.
