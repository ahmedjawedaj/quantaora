# White Dwarf

*Space > Stars > Stellar Lifecycle > Stellar remnants > White Dwarf*

A dead star as dense as it gets — the exposed core that a Sun-like star leaves behind.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a small, dazzling white sphere the size of a marble beside a model of Earth for scale. A faint ring of pale green and blue gas hovers around it — the leftover of the red giant's envelope, drifting away.

## 2. Touch it

Pick the dwarf up and feel the surprise that matters most: it is a sphere of nearly the Sun's whole mass hidden in an Earth-sized ball. Toggle `hs-degenerate-interior` to expose the crystal-like interior. Drag the spotlight to make it dim, and watch the temperature display count downward — the dwarf cooling like a dying ember.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-chandrasekhar-limit` to hear the rule of 1.4 solar masses, `hs-degenerate-interior` for the electron-pressure crowd, `hs-planetary-nebula` for the drifting shell, and `hs-no-fusion-core` to label the dead center.

## 4. Explain it

Walk the chain: the red giant's gravity crushes the core until electrons refuse to squeeze closer → electron degeneracy pressure holds the star open at the size of Earth → with no fusion, the glow is only leftover heat → the heat leaks away over billions of years. Pressure does not need fire here.

## 5. Predict it

Before you tap the density readout, guess: *if you squeezed the whole Sun down into an Earth-sized ball, what would one teaspoon of it weigh on a kitchen scale?* Reveal the answer — around five and a half tons.

**Prediction prompt:** The white dwarf stops collapsing because electrons refuse to be pressed closer. Predict what happens if you try to add more mass than the 1.4-solar-mass limit — then check the note on the Chandrasekhar hotspot.

## 6. Belongs to

The white dwarf is the last stage in the Star's chapter for Sun-like careers: a Main Sequence star grows into a Red Giant, sheds its envelope as a nebula, and leaves this core behind.

## 7. Compare it

Lay the dwarf beside the Neutron Star component: both supports handheld-sized cores with quantum pressure — but electrons here, neutrons there, and different birth stories (wind-blown seed vs. supernova). Which remnant is denser? Which was born in an explosion?

## 8. Review it later

**Practice item:** Without labels, tap the three main structures and name each one's job: the degenerate core, the nebula, the dead furnace.

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain — with no prompts — why a white dwarf is denser than any solid thing you've ever touched, and what holds it up?
