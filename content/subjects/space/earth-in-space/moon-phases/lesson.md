# Moon Phases

*Space > Earth In Space > Earth's Motion & Cycles > Moon · orbiting Earth, lit by the Sun > Moon Phases*

## 1. Half lit, always

Just like Earth, the Moon always has a day side facing the Sun and a night side facing away — that never changes. What changes is which part of that lit half we can see from Earth.

## 2. Drag the Moon around its orbit

At 0° the Moon sits between Earth and the Sun — its lit side faces completely away from us, so we see New Moon (essentially invisible). At 180° the Moon is on the far side of Earth from the Sun, so its entire lit side faces us — Full Moon.

## 3. The phase wheel matches the sky

The eight-phase wheel highlights exactly where the Moon sits in its cycle as you move the angle — compare it against the lit shape on the 3D Moon to see they always agree, because they're computed from the same real geometry.

## 4. Not the same thing as an eclipse

A phase change happens every month, from ordinary geometry. An eclipse needs a near-perfect straight-line alignment and only happens a few times a year — see the Eclipses component for the difference.
