# Tides

*Space > Earth In Space > Earth's Motion & Cycles > Earth · pulled by the Moon (and Sun) > Tides*

## 1. Two bulges, not one

It's natural to expect only one tidal bulge, on the side facing the Moon. There are actually two — the far side bulges too, because Earth's solid body is pulled toward the Moon slightly more than the far water is, leaving that water relatively behind.

## 2. Watch the marker spin through both

Speed up Earth's spin and watch the marker point pass through 'High tide' twice and 'Low tide' twice for every full rotation — that's the real reason most coastlines get two tide cycles a day, not one.

## 3. Spring vs. neap

Toggle the Sun-Moon alignment: when they line up (spring tide), their pulls combine for bigger bulges and more extreme high/low tides. When they're at right angles (neap tide), the Sun's pull partly cancels the Moon's, giving gentler tides.

## 4. Why the Moon wins over the Sun

The Sun is far more massive than the Moon, but it's also far, far farther away — tidal pull weakens fast with distance, so the much-closer Moon actually has the bigger tidal effect, even though the Sun's gravity on Earth overall is much stronger.
