# Axial Tilt & Seasons

*Space > Earth In Space > Earth's Motion & Cycles > Earth · tilted axis, changing seasons > Axial Tilt & Seasons*

## 1. The tilt never changes direction

As Earth orbits the Sun, its axis keeps pointing the same way in space — toward the North Star — the whole year. It doesn't wobble to follow the Sun. That fixed lean is what makes each hemisphere take turns facing toward or away from the Sun.

## 2. Turn the tilt down to zero

Drag the tilt slider to 0° and orbit around the full year — notice the season readout stays the same everywhere, all year. Seasons only exist because the tilt exists.

## 3. Move through a full year

Drag the orbit-position slider (or use the season presets) through March → June → September → December and watch the daylight-hours readout for each hemisphere swing between longer and shorter days.

## 4. Opposite hemispheres, opposite seasons

At every point in the orbit, the Northern and Southern Hemisphere readouts always show opposite seasons — never the same one. That's the tilt in action: one hemisphere's gain in sunlight is always the other's loss.
