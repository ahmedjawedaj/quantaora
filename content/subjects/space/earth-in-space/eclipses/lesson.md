# Eclipses

*Space > Earth In Space > Earth's Motion & Cycles > Sun · Earth · Moon — a near-perfect straight line > Eclipses*

## 1. Two kinds of eclipse, two orders

Solar eclipse: Moon in the middle, its shadow falls on Earth. Lunar eclipse: Earth in the middle, its shadow falls on the Moon. Switch the type toggle to see both alignments.

## 2. Drag the alignment slider

At 0° offset the three bodies line up almost exactly and the shadow lands squarely — a total eclipse. As the offset grows, the shadow starts to miss its target — first a partial eclipse, then none at all.

## 3. Turn the 5.1° tilt on and off

With the Moon's real orbital tilt switched on, most alignment attempts miss — matching real life, where eclipses only happen a handful of times a year. Switch the tilt off and every month lines up, which is not what actually happens.

## 4. Same setup as moon phases, different outcome

A New Moon (Sun-Moon-Earth roughly lined up) happens every month and usually produces no eclipse at all — because "roughly" lined up is not the same as the near-perfect alignment an eclipse needs.

**Prediction prompt:** If the Moon's orbit had zero tilt, would there be a solar and lunar eclipse every single month? Switch the tilt off and check.
