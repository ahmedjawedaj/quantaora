# Line

A line is a straight path that goes on forever in both directions.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a flat board with a straight thin path drawn across it. Arrowheads at both ends point out to the edges and beyond, and two dots labeled A and B sit on the path. Tap `hs-line-path` to label the line.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag the A and B dots along the path — the line stays straight as they slide. Press 'extend' and watch the arrowheads push the drawn path longer and longer at both ends at once. Slide the line up and down the board to see it move while staying straight.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-line-path` to label the line, `hs-arrowhead` to label an arrowhead, and `hs-point-on-line` to label the points it passes through.

## 4. Explain what it does

A line is a straight path of points with no ends — it extends forever in both directions. The arrowheads are a promise that the path continues even though you can only draw a piece of it. A line has no thickness, and you name it by two points on it, like line AB.

## 5. See where it belongs

Lines belong to the basic elements of geometry, made from points. They give birth to two important cousins: stop the line at both ends and you get a segment; stop it at one end and you get a ray. Two lines that share a point form angles.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you press extend, predict how the arrowheads will change.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a line with a segment. The line AB has arrowheads at both ends and never stops; the segment AB has two solid endpoints and a definite length. Compare a line with a ray: the ray has one arrowhead and one endpoint, halfway between the two.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to trace a horizon: mark two boats as points A and B on the line. Then find a third point C on the same line — the line passes through all three in a straight row. Name the line in two different ways.

**Practice item:** On the model, place two points on the line and write the line's name; then count how many directions it extends.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What does the arrowhead on a line mean, and how is a line different from a segment?
