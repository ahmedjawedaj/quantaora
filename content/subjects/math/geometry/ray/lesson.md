# Ray

A ray has one endpoint and goes on forever in one direction — like a flashlight beam.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a board with a straight path starting from a solid dot labeled A, stretching to an arrowhead at the other end. Tap `hs-ray-path` to label the ray.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag the arrowhead end — the ray rotates around its fixed endpoint A like a light beam. Press 'extend' and the beam grows longer in its direction. Add a second ray from the same endpoint and swing it around — the two rays open and close like a pair of scissors.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-ray-path` to label the ray, `hs-endpoint` to label the endpoint, and `hs-arrowhead` to label the direction marker.

## 4. Explain what it does

A ray starts at one endpoint and travels straight forever in one direction. The dot marks where it starts, and the arrowhead marks the way it never stops. Name it with the endpoint first: ray AB starts at A and passes through B.

## 5. See where it belongs

Rays are one of the basic elements of geometry, cousins of lines and segments. The key place rays appear is in angles: two rays sharing an endpoint create an angle, and the shared endpoint is the vertex.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you swing the second ray, predict what shape the two rays will trace together.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a ray with a line: the line extends forever both ways; the ray extends forever one way and stops at its endpoint on the other. Compare a ray with a segment: the segment stops at both ends; the ray stops at only one.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model as a flashlight: point ray AB from the lighthouse A toward the sea. Then add a second ray AC pointing toward the harbor. The two rays together make an angle at A. Name the rays with the endpoint first.

**Practice item:** On the model, make a ray from A through B, then state which point comes first in the name and why.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What makes a ray different from a line, and how do two rays form an angle?
