# Volume

Volume measures how much space a 3D object fills, counted in cubic units.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a clear rectangular prism (a box) on a board. Inside, the unit cubes are visible — 4 across, 3 deep, 2 tall. A counter reads the total. Tap `hs-volume-box` to label the box.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Peel away the front layer of cubes to see the base layer of 12 cubes (4 × 3). Press 'rebuild' and the layers stack up again. Drag the box's depth handle to make it 4 by 3 by 3, then read how many cubes fit now. Press 'fill water' and the box fills with tinted water to the brim, level with the top cubes.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-volume-box` to label the box, `hs-base-layer` to label the base layer of cubes, and `hs-height-layer` to label the height dimension.

## 4. Explain what it does

Volume counts how many unit cubes pack the solid. The base layer holds length × width cubes; the height multiplies the layers: 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 cubic units. Water filling the box to the brim shows the same space — the box's capacity is its volume.

## 5. See where it belongs

Volume is the 3D cousin of area: area tiles a flat surface in square units, volume packs a solid in cubic units. It builds on the rectangle's area formula and extends to cylinders and spheres. Pools, tanks, and packing boxes are its real-world home.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you extend the box from height 2 to 3, predict how many new cubes will appear.

## 6. Compare it

Compare volume with area. The flat base layer is area — 12 square units. Stack 2 tall and the full box is volume — 24 cubic units. Compare also a prism with a flat rectangle: the rectangle has no height; the box adds the third dimension.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to pack a moving box: the box is 5 units long, 4 wide, and 3 tall. Count or multiply to find how many unit cubes (boxes) fit inside: 5 × 4 × 3 = 60. Then find the volume of a 2-by-2-by-2 cube.

**Practice item:** On the model, set the box to 3 by 3 by 3 and state its volume, then explain the formula you used.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What does volume measure, why is it in cubic units, and how do you find the volume of a box?
