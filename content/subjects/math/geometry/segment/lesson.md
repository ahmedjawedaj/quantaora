# Segment

A segment is the part of a line between two endpoints — a straight piece with a definite length.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a board with a straight stroke between two solid dots labeled A and B. A ruler sits along the bottom, and the stroke lines up at the 3 mark. Tap `hs-segment-stroke` to label the segment.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag endpoint B along the board — the segment stretches and shrinks, and the ruler reading updates. Drag endpoint A too and watch the segment slide while staying straight. Press 'measure' and a tape line snaps onto the segment showing its exact length.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-segment-stroke` to label the segment, `hs-endpoint-a` and `hs-endpoint-b` to label the two endpoints.

## 4. Explain what it does

A segment is a piece of a line that stops at both ends. The two solid dots are its endpoints, and the stroke between them has a real, measurable length. Since it stops, unlike a line or ray, you can read its length directly with a ruler.

## 5. See where it belongs

Segments are the sides of every polygon — a triangle has three segment sides, a rectangle has four. Add all the side segments together and you get the perimeter. Segments are the parts of geometry you can actually measure.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you drag endpoint B to the 8 mark, predict how the ruler reading will change.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a segment with a line. The line has arrowheads at both ends and no length to measure; the segment has two solid dots and a definite length. Compare a segment with a ray: the ray stops at only one end, so it is still half-endless.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to measure a tabletop: mark the table's left edge as point A and right edge as point B, then read the segment AB's length on the ruler. Measure the back edge between B and C the same way. Which segment is longer?

**Practice item:** On the model, drag the endpoints to make a segment of length 5, then state how you know it is exactly 5.
