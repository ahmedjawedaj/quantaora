# Circle

A circle is every point at the same distance from one center — perfectly round.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a flat wheel standing upright on a board. A center dot labeled O, a radius segment from O to the rim, and a diameter segment across through O are highlighted. A tape wraps around the rim reading the circumference. Tap `hs-circle-rim` to label the circle.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag the rim outward to make the wheel bigger — the radius and diameter labels grow together. Press 'unroll' and the rim tape peels off and straightens into a line as long as the circumference. Press 'roll' and the wheel rolls across the board, showing how one turn travels one circumference distance.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-circle-rim` to label the circle, `hs-radius` to label the radius, and `hs-diameter` to label the diameter.

## 4. Explain what it does

Every point on the rim is the same distance from the center. The radius runs center-to-rim; the diameter runs across through the center — two radii. The distance around the rim is the circumference. Whatever the wheel's size, circumference ÷ diameter is always pi (π ≈ 3.14), so circumference = π × diameter.

## 5. See where it belongs

Circles are the smooth, curved family of geometry, built from a center point and rim points. The radius connects to segments; pi connects circumference to area (area = π × r²). Wheels, orbits, and clocks all live here.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you press unroll, predict whether the straightened tape will be longer or shorter than the wheel's width across.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the circle with a polygon: a square has straight sides and corners, while a circle has one smooth curve and no corners at all. Compare the radius with the diameter: the radius is half the diameter, center-to-rim.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to measure a bicycle wheel: set the wheel's radius to 1 unit, then press unroll to see one full turn travel about 6.28 units. Find the diameter by doubling the radius, then multiply by π to check the unrolled length matches.

**Practice item:** On the model, read the radius and diameter of the wheel, then predict the circumference before pressing unroll.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What is a circle, how are radius and diameter related, and what does π have to do with the circumference?
