# Angle

An angle is the amount of turn between two rays that share an endpoint.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: two rays spring from a shared vertex point labeled V. One ray points right, the other points up-right, and between them an arc shows the turn, tagged with 45°. Tap `hs-angle-arc` to label the angle.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Swing the second ray around the vertex — the arc grows and shrinks and the degree label updates as it passes 90°, then 180°, then back. Press 'show protractor' and a protractor ring snaps around the vertex so you can read the exact degrees.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-angle-arc` to label the angle, `hs-vertex` to label the vertex, and `hs-ray-side` to label one ray side.

## 4. Explain what it does

An angle measures the turn between two rays from a shared vertex. We count turns in degrees: 90° is a quarter turn (a right angle), 180° is a straight line, 360° is a full circle. Angles under 90° are acute; between 90° and 180° they are obtuse.

## 5. See where it belongs

Angles belong to geometry between basic elements and shapes. Two rays create an angle; three segments closing up create a triangle whose three angles always add to 180°. Angles appear in triangles, clocks, ramps, and corners.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you swing the ray past the up position, predict whether the angle label will pass 90°.

## 6. Compare it

Compare an acute angle with an obtuse angle: at 30° the two rays hug closely; at 150° they spread wide. Compare a right angle with a straight angle: 90° is a quarter turn, 180° is a half turn making a flat line.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to read a clock: set the two rays to match 3 o'clock hands — one ray up (12), one ray right (3) — that angle is 90°, a right angle. Then swing the ray to the 2 o'clock position and estimate the new angle.

**Practice item:** On the model, make an angle of about 30°, then swing it to 120°, and name each type.
