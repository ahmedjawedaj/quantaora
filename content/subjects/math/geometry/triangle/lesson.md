# Triangle

A triangle is a closed shape with three straight sides, three vertices, and three angles adding to 180°.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a solid triangular plate standing on edge, with its three sides drawn, three corner dots labeled A, B, C, and small arcs at each corner showing the three angles. Tap `hs-triangle-plate` to label the triangle.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag the top vertex — the triangle stretches and skews, but watch its three angle tags update so they always add to 180°. Press 'press test' and push on the side; the triangle refuses to flex. Drag the vertices one at a time to turn it into an equilateral or a right triangle and read the names.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-triangle-plate` to label the triangle, `hs-side` to label a side, and `hs-vertex-a` to label a vertex.

## 4. Explain what it does

A triangle has three straight sides joined at three vertices, making three angles. Skew it any way and its angles still sum to exactly 180°. Its three sides lock together rigidly — unlike a four-sided frame, a triangle cannot be pushed out of shape.

## 5. See where it belongs

Triangles belong to the polygon family; they are the smallest closed shape made of segments. They connect to angles (sum 180°) and to quadrilaterals (split any quadrilateral along a diagonal and you get two triangles). Bridges and roofs use them for strength.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you drag the vertex, predict whether the sum of the three angle labels will stay 180°.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a triangle with a quadrilateral. The triangle has 3 sides and stays rigid; the 4-sided quadrilateral flexes like a gate when pushed. Compare an isosceles triangle with a scalene one: two equal sides vs. all different.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to design a roof truss: place the two roof beams from the apex down to the walls — hold them rigid by adding the third triangle side (the base beam). Then compare a triangle side arrangement with a loose square frame and see which one sways.

**Practice item:** On the model, make a triangle that is equilateral, then one that is right-angled, and read its angle sum.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What are the three parts of a triangle, why do its angles always add to 180°, and why is it rigid?
