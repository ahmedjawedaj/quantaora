# Outlier

An outlier is a data point that sits far away from the rest of the dataset.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a dot plot on a board. A tight cluster of dots sits in the middle, and one lone dot sits far off to the right with a question mark above it. Tap `hs-outlier-dot` to label the outlier.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag the lone dot toward the cluster and back — watch a 'distance' tag grow and shrink. Press 'show effects' and three summary plates light up: the mean slides toward the outlier, the range arrow jumps wide, but the median line stays almost still. Press 'inspect' and a magnifier reads the outlier's label — is it a real observation or a typo?

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-outlier-dot` to label the outlier, `hs-cluster` to label the main group, and `hs-distance-gap` to label the gap that makes it an outlier.

## 4. Explain what it does

An outlier is a real data point but unusually distant. Its distance changes the summaries unevenly: the mean is pulled toward it, the range stretches to include it, and the median holds still. Spotting it is the first step — then you investigate what caused it.

## 5. See where it belongs

Outliers are the dramatic points of Data And Statistics. They connect directly to the mean (which they pull), the range (which they stretch), and the median (which they cannot move). In real reports they are the anomalies worth investigating.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you press show effects, predict which of the three summaries will change most.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the outlier with a normal point in the cluster. The cluster point sits among friends and barely moves the mean; the outlier stands alone and swings it. Compare also the outlier's effect on the mean versus the median — one bends, one holds.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to check a class average: test scores cluster around 70-85, but one score reads 12. Press inspect and decide — is it a real very low score or a data entry error? If it's a typo for 72, fix it and watch the mean recover.

**Practice item:** On the model, drag the outlier to various spots and state how the mean moves compared with the median.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What makes a point an outlier, and how does it affect the mean, the range, and the median differently?
