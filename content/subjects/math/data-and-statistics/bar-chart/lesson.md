# Bar Chart

A bar chart shows data as bars whose heights show the counts.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a standing chart plate with a bottom axis labeled with colors — Red, Blue, Green — and a side axis labeled 0, 2, 4, 6, 8. Three bars rise from the bottom axis. Tap `hs-bar-tallest` to label the tallest bar.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Press 'grow' and each bar animates up from the axis to its height. Tap a bar and drag a handle to change its value — the bar resizes and the axis reading updates. Press 'compare' and a dashed line stretches from each bar top to the value axis so you can read every count at once.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-bar-tallest` to label a bar, `hs-category-axis` to label the bottom axis, and `hs-value-axis` to label the side axis.

## 4. Explain what it does

Each category gets one bar whose height matches its count. The bottom axis names the categories; the side axis measures the counts. Reading a bar means following from its top to the value axis. The tallest bar is the biggest category — instantly visible.

## 5. See where it belongs

Bar charts are the visual stage of the data pipeline: data points fill a table, and the table's counts become bars. They sit beside line graphs — bars for comparing categories, lines for tracking changes over time.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you press grow, predict which bar will end up tallest.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a bar chart with a line graph of the same data. The bar chart compares the categories side by side; the line graph connects dots across time to show a trend. Same numbers, two different questions answered.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to count a class vote: the three bars show how many students voted for each color. Read the value on the Blue bar, then decide which color won and by how much compared to the runner-up.

**Practice item:** On the model, read all three bar values, then state which category is smallest and why you know from the bars alone.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What do the two axes do, how do you read a bar's value, and what does a bar chart make easy to compare?
