# Median

The median is the middle value of data after sorting it low to high.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a shelf of five blocks with heights 3, 7, 1, 5, 2 set in a messy row. Above it sits an empty sorted track. Tap `hs-median-block` to label the middle block.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Press 'sort' and the blocks slide into the track in order: 1, 2, 3, 5, 7. The middle block 3 glows. Press 'add block' to make six blocks: the two middle blocks both glow and move together, meeting at the center as their average. Drag a block to a huge value and watch the median stay put.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-median-block` to label the median, `hs-sorted-track` to label the sorted line, and `hs-low-high` to label the low and high ends.

## 4. Explain what it does

The median is the value at the middle position of a sorted list. Odd count: one middle block. Even count: average the two middle blocks. Because it looks at position rather than size, a giant outlier block does not move it.

## 5. See where it belongs

The median is one of the summarizing statistics in Data And Statistics, a sibling of the mean, mode, and range. It is the go-to summary when data is skewed — income, prices, and times where one big value would unfairly pull the mean.

**Prediction prompt:** Before pressing sort, predict which block will end up in the middle.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the median with the mean of 1, 2, 3, 5, 7. The median is the middle block 3. The mean is the fair share, (1+2+3+5+7)÷5 = 3.6. Now change the 7 block to 100: the mean jumps to 22, the median stays 3. Same data, very different summaries.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to summarize house prices on a street: five prices 150k, 180k, 200k, 220k, and a mansion at 900k. Sort and find the middle price. Which summary — median or mean — better describes the 'typical' house? State why.

**Practice item:** On the model, sort four blocks 4, 9, 2, 7 and find the median by averaging the two middle ones.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** How do you find the median for odd and even counts, and why does an outlier barely move it?
