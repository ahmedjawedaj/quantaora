# Range

The range measures the spread of data — the largest value minus the smallest.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a number track from 0 to 100 on a board. Five score blocks sit on the track: 55, 70, 82, 90, and a highlighted high block at 90 and low block at 55. Between them a double-headed arrow spans the gap. Tap `hs-range-arrow` to label the range.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Slide the low block down and the high block up — the arrow stretches and its length label updates. Press 'slide to bunched' and the blocks cluster together, the arrow shrinking to a small span. Tap the blocks to add a single gray outlier at 5 and watch the arrow jump wide.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-range-arrow` to label the range, `hs-high-block` to label the largest value, and `hs-low-block` to label the smallest value.

## 4. Explain what it does

The range is the gap between the smallest and the largest value — high minus low. Clustered blocks give a small range; widely spaced blocks give a large range. Add one far-out block and the range jumps even though the middle barely changed.

## 5. See where it belongs

The range is the spread partner of the mean, median, and mode in Data And Statistics. The mean tells you the center; the range tells you how spread out. On a line graph the vertical spread of the line is the range of the data.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you slide the low block from 55 to 40, predict how the range arrow will change.

## 6. Compare it

Compare two classes with the SAME mean but different ranges. Class A scores 70, 71, 69 — mean 70, range 2 (bunched). Class B scores 90, 70, 50 — mean 70, range 40 (spread). The mean cannot tell them apart; the range does.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to forecast a day: track temperatures across a week — 18, 20, 24, 26, 20. Find the range (26 − 18 = 8°). Then compare with another week moving from 5 to 30 — range 25. Which week's forecast covered more weather?

**Practice item:** On the model, slide blocks to heights 10 and 30 and state the range in one step.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What is the range, how do you compute it, and how does adding an outlier change it?
