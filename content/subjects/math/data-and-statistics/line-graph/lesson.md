# Line Graph

A line graph connects data points with lines to show change over time.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a standing chart plate with a grid. Seven dots sit on the grid from left to right, connected by a line that rises and dips. A small thermometer icon at the top labels the topic 'hourly temperature'. Tap `hs-line-trend` to label the connecting line.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Press 'build' and the dots appear one at a time from left to right, a segment connecting each new dot to the previous one. Drag a dot higher or lower and the two segments around it stretch and adjust. Press 'trace' and a pointer glides along the line while telling you whether the temperature is going up or down.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-line-trend` to label the connecting line, `hs-dot` to label a data point, and `hs-time-axis` to label the time axis.

## 4. Explain what it does

Each dot is one data point at one moment in time. Connecting the dots turns them into a continuous path, so you can see the direction of change — up is rising, down is falling, flat is steady. The line is a guide between measured points, showing the trend.

## 5. See where it belongs

Line graphs are the 'change over time' member of the chart family in Data And Statistics. They are siblings to bar charts (which compare categories) and are built from the same data points that feed a table and the statistics.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you press trace, predict whether the pointer will mostly be climbing or dropping.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a line graph with a bar chart of the same data. The bar chart shows each hour as a separate bar, good for comparing heights; the line graph connects the hours, good for watching the change. Same numbers, different story.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to follow a day's temperature: build the graph and read the morning high, the midday dip, the evening climb. Read the value on Tuesday's dot, then say whether the trend over the week is rising or falling.

**Practice item:** On the model, tap a dot and state its time and value, then say whether the line was rising or falling after it.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What does each dot represent, what does the connecting line show, and how can you tell a rise from a fall?
