# Mean

The mean is the average — every data value shared out evenly.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a sharing table with four bins holding shell piles of 2, 4, 6, and 8. At the front, a level line shows a height of 5 across all four bins. A plate reads 'Total 20, Count 4, Mean 5'. Tap `hs-mean-line` to label the mean.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Press 'share' and watch the shells redistribute — moving from the tall piles to the short ones until every bin reaches the same height. Drag the count number to add a fifth friend, and the shells re-share into equal piles again. Press 'sum' and a glow counts up 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 = 20.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-mean-line` to label the mean line, `hs-bin` to label a bin of shells, and `hs-total-plate` to label the total.

## 4. Explain what it does

The mean is computed from every data point: add them all up, then divide by the number of points. With 2, 4, 6, 8: total 20, count 4, mean 20 ÷ 4 = 5. The mean line shows the height every bin would have if the shells were shared fairly.

## 5. See where it belongs

The mean is one of the statistics in Data And Statistics that summarize a dataset, alongside median, mode, and range. It is computed from the data points and is the star of reports that quote 'averages'.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you press share, predict the final height of the shells in every bin.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the mean with the median of 2, 4, 6, 8. The mean is the fair share, 5 (even though no bin actually holds exactly 5 — it is the shared value). The median is the middle point between 4 and 6, also 5 here. Add an outlier like 30, and the mean jumps while the median barely moves.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model for a math quiz: four scores of 70, 80, 90, and 100. Add the scores (340) and count them (4), then find the mean 85. Would adding a fifth score of 0 change the mean a lot? Share it on the model and see.

**Practice item:** On the model, press sum on bins 3, 5, 7 and divide by count 3 to find the mean.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What is the mean, why is the total divided by the count, and how does an outlier affect it?
