# Data Point

A data point is one single piece of information — one height, one score, one observation.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a measuring board with a row of small cubes. One cube is lifted out of the row and sits at the front with a tag reading 'Ali = 150 cm'. Tap `hs-point-cube` to label the data point.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Lift each cube out of the row, one at a time, and read its tag — each cube is one child's height. Press 'add measurement' and a new cube slides in with a new height. Drag a cube onto the chart plate behind and watch it turn into a dot on a growing line graph.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-point-cube` to label the data point, `hs-value-tag` to label its value, and `hs-dataset-row` to label the whole row of points.

## 4. Explain what it does

A data point is one single observation with a value — Ali's height is 150 cm. One point alone tells a small story; line up many points and you have a dataset. Each chart is built from these individual points, and every statistic is computed from them.

## 5. See where it belongs

Data points are the atoms of Data And Statistics. They collect into tables, build bar charts and line graphs, and feed the mean, median, mode, and range. Everything in this branch starts from a single point.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you press add measurement, predict whether the new cube will be taller or shorter than Ali's cube.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a single data point with the mean of the dataset. Ali's cube alone is just one height; drag it to the chart and add four more cubes, then press 'mean' and watch a dashed line mark the average of all five points.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to log a week of temperatures: add a cube for each day's high temperature — Monday 22°, Tuesday 25°, and so on. Read each point's tag as you add it, then slide a cube into the chart and see where that one day sits.

**Practice item:** On the model, lift one cube and state its label and value, then identify which cube in the row is the tallest data point.
