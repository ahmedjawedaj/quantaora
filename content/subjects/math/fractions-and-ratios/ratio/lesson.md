# Ratio

A ratio compares two quantities — 3 red beads to 5 blue beads is written 3:5.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a small tray holding 3 red beads and 5 blue beads in two tidy groups, with a label reading 'red : blue = 3 : 5'. Tap `hs-ratio-pair` to label the ratio.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Tap the scale control to multiply both groups by 2 — now 6 red and 10 blue beads, while the ratio label stays 3:5. Then toggle the view to 'part-to-whole' to see red : all beads, which reads 3:8.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-ratio-pair` to label the ratio, `hs-part-group` to label one of the compared groups, and `hs-whole-total` to label the total when toggled to part-to-whole.

## 4. Explain what it does

A ratio compares two quantities and keeps its meaning when both are scaled by the same factor. 3:5 = 6:10 = 9:15 — same mix of beads. Order matters: red:blue is 3:5, but blue:red is 5:3.

## 5. See where it belongs

Ratios belong to Fractions And Ratios as comparisons. They connect to fractions (part-to-whole), scale into proportions (3:5 = 6:10), and simplify just like fractions do.

**Prediction prompt:** If you double the beads again, predict what the new counts will be and whether the ratio label changes.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a ratio with a fraction. A fraction compares a part to the whole — 3/8 of beads are red. A ratio can compare part to part — 3 red to 5 blue — which a fraction alone cannot express.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to solve a recipe problem: *A lemonade recipe uses 2 cups of lemon juice for every 5 cups of water. You want to make double. What ratio of juice to water do you use?* Scale both groups by 2 to get 4:10, which still equals 2:5.

**Practice item:** On the model, show 3:5 and its scaled form 6:10 side by side. Say why they describe the same mix.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** Write the ratio of 4 red beads to 6 blue beads in simplest form, and explain why order matters in a ratio.
