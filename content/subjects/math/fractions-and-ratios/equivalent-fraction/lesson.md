# Equivalent Fractions

Equivalent fractions are different fractions that name the same amount — 1/2, 2/4, and 3/6 are all half.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: two fraction circles side by side. The left circle is cut into 2 parts with 1 filled; the right is cut into 4 parts with 2 filled. Both show the same shaded amount. Tap `hs-equivalence` to label the pair.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag the scale slider to ×3 and watch both circles redraw — 1/2 becomes 3/6 while staying the same shade. Then tap the simplify control on a 6/8 circle and watch it reduce to 3/4 as both numbers divide by 2.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-equivalence` to label the equal pair, `hs-scale-rule` to label the 'multiply both by the same number' rule, and `hs-simplify` to label the reverse (divide both) process.

## 4. Explain what it does

To create an equivalent fraction, multiply or divide both the numerator and denominator by the same number. 1/2 × 2/2 = 2/4 — still half. Simplifying is the reverse: dividing both numbers by a common factor until only 1 divides them, giving simplest form.

## 5. See where it belongs

Equivalent fractions belong to Fractions And Ratios. They build on the fraction and denominator ideas, and they are the tool that makes adding, subtracting, comparing, and simplifying all possible.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you drag the slider, predict: if you multiply 1/2 by 4/4, what fraction will you get, and will it still be half?

## 6. Compare it

Compare equivalent fractions with the same-number fractions. 1/2 and 2/4 ARE equivalent — they name the same amount. 1/2 and 2/3 are NOT — same numerator idea, different amounts. Equivalent means equal amounts, not similar-looking numbers.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to solve a real problem: *You need to add 1/4 of a cup and 1/8 of a cup. Rewrite 1/4 as eighths using the model, then combine them.* Show 1/4 × 2/2 = 2/8, then count 2/8 + 1/8 = 3/8.

**Practice item:** On the model, use the simplify control to reduce 8/12 to simplest form, and explain what number you divided by.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** How do you make an equivalent fraction, and how do you check that 2/3 and 4/6 really name the same amount?
