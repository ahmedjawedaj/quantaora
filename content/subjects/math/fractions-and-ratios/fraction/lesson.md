# Fraction

A fraction is part of a whole — 3/4 of a pizza, 1/2 of a cup.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a whole pizza flat on a mat, with a knife that can cut it into 4 equal slices. Three of the slices are lifted away and stacked. Tap `hs-fraction-pizza` to label the fraction being shown.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag the slider to change the number of equal slices — cut the pizza into 2, then 3, then 4 parts — and watch the pieces resize to stay equal. Lift away different numbers of slices and read the fraction plate update, like 1/2, 2/4, or 3/4.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-fraction-pizza` to label the fraction, `hs-numerator` to label the top number (slices taken), and `hs-denominator` to label the bottom number (equal parts in the whole).

## 4. Explain what it does

A fraction names part of a whole. The denominator counts the equal parts the whole is split into; the numerator counts how many parts you have. 3/4 means 3 out of 4 equal slices. All parts must be equal — that is what makes the fraction honest.

## 5. See where it belongs

Fractions belong to Fractions And Ratios. They build on division (a whole split into equal parts), and connect forward to equivalent fractions, decimals, percents, and ratios.

**Prediction prompt:** If you cut the pizza into 6 equal slices and lift away 2, what fraction will the plate show? Make your guess before you move the slider.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a fraction with a whole number. 3 is three whole apples; 3/4 is three of four equal parts of one apple. Fractions like 3/4 are less than 1 because the parts are smaller than the whole.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to solve a real problem: *You eat 2 of the 8 equal slices of a pizza. What fraction of the pizza did you eat?* Set the slider to 8 slices and lift 2 away — read the answer.

**Practice item:** On the model, show 3/4 of the pizza. Then find the fraction that shows the SAME amount with 6 parts, and explain the change.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What does the denominator of a fraction tell you, what does the numerator tell you, and why must the parts be equal?
