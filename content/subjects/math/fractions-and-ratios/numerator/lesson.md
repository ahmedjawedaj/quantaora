# Numerator

The numerator is the top number of a fraction — it counts how many equal parts you have.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a fraction bar split into 4 equal segments, with 3 segments filled in and one left blank. Above the bar, the fraction '3/4' is shown with the numerator lit. Tap `hs-numerator-label` to label the top number.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag the fill slider to change the numerator from 0 to 4 — watch more or fewer segments light up while the denominator stays fixed at 4. Notice the fraction reads 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 as the numerator climbs.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-numerator-label` to label the numerator, `hs-filled-segments` to label the lit parts it counts, and `hs-denominator-label` to label the bottom number that stays put.

## 4. Explain what it does

The numerator counts the equal parts you have. In 3/4, the 3 means three of the four parts. Raising the numerator gives you more of the whole — 4/4 fills the bar completely, and 5/4 would spill past it.

## 5. See where it belongs

The numerator is the top half of every fraction. It belongs inside the Fraction component, and it works together with the denominator, which names the parts. Together they produce the amount the fraction represents.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you drag the slider, predict: at what numerator will the bar become completely full (equal one whole)?

## 6. Compare it

Compare the numerator with the denominator. The numerator counts the parts you have — how many. The denominator tells what kind of part — the size. 3 in '3/4' counts slices; the 4 says they are fourths.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a real question: *A chocolate bar has 6 equal rows. You eat 2 rows. What fraction is the numerator, and what does it mean?* Set the bar to 6 parts, fill 2, and read the answer.

**Practice item:** On the model, set the bar to 5 parts and fill 3. Say the fraction aloud, pointing out which number is the numerator and what it counts.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What does the numerator of a fraction count, and what happens to the amount when the numerator gets larger but the denominator stays the same?
