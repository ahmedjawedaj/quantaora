# Factors

Factors are numbers that multiply together to make a product — 3 and 4 are factors of 12 because 3 × 4 = 12.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a central number card showing 12, with factor-pair cards arranged around it — 1×12, 2×6, 3×4 — each with its own multiplication tiles. Tap `hs-factor-pair` to label a factor pair.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Flip each factor-pair card over to see the division check (12 ÷ 4 = 3 R 0). Drag two factor tiles together to verify they multiply back to 12. Then use the slider to switch the center number to 18 and watch the factor cards rearrange into the full factor set.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-factor-pair` to label a pair, `hs-factor-list` to label the complete list of factors, and `hs-division-check` to label the even-division proof under each pair.

## 4. Explain what it does

A factor divides a number evenly or multiplies with a partner to equal it. Factors come in pairs: 1×12, 2×6, 3×4. Every factor divides the number with no remainder, so finding factors means testing which numbers divide evenly.

## 5. See where it belongs

Factors belong to Number Relationships. They connect multiplication and division, pair with multiples (3 is a factor of 12, 12 is a multiple of 3), and support fractions, ratios, and prime factorization.

**Practice item:** On the model, set the center number to 15 and list all its factor pairs aloud (1×15 and 3×5).

## 6. Compare it

Compare factors with multiples. Factors multiply together to build a number — 3 and 4 build 12. Multiples are the results when a number multiplies — 12, 24, 36 are multiples of 12. Same relationship, opposite direction.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to solve a real problem: *You are arranging 24 chairs into equal rows. What row sizes (numbers of chairs per row) work?* Each working row size is a factor of 24.

**Practice item:** Set the center to 24, list the full factor set, and explain why 5 is NOT a factor (24 ÷ 5 leaves a remainder).

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What are all the factors of 18, and why do factor pairs always cover the middle of the number?
