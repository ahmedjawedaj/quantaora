# Prime Number

A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a grid of number tiles from 2 to 50 — the Sieve of Eratosthenes. Primes like 7 and 11 glow a calm amber, while composites like 12 and 15 stay muted. Tap `hs-prime-tile` to highlight a prime tile.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Tap the sieve control to run the classic sift: start at 2, tap it to mark it prime, then strike out every multiple of 2; move to 3, strike out its multiples; and so on. Watch only primes survive the sift. Then tap `hs-factor-test` on a composite tile to see its factor pairs light up as a small factor chain.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-prime-tile` to label a prime, `hs-composite-tile` to label a composite, and `hs-factor-chain` to label the prime factorization shown for one number.

## 4. Explain what it does

A prime has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. That is the whole test. 1 is not prime (one factor), and any even number above 2 is composite. Every number above 1 is either prime or composite — there is no third kind.

## 5. See where it belongs

Primes belong to Number Relationships, growing out of factors. Every whole number is either prime or a product of primes, which is why primes sit beneath fractions, factors, and even cryptography.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you tap the sieve, predict: which tile will survive longest — 5 or 17 — and why?

## 6. Compare it

Compare a prime with a composite. 7 is prime — only 1 and 7 divide it. 8 is composite — 1, 2, 4, and 8 divide it. More factors means composite; exactly two means prime.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to solve a real question: *Can you tile a 13-cube row with equal groups of 2, 3, or 4?* Since 13 is prime, no group size other than 1 and 13 divides it evenly — the row cannot be split.

**Practice item:** On the sieve, run the sift and list the primes from 2 to 20. Then confirm each one by counting its factors.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What makes a number prime, why is 1 not prime, and what is the prime factorization of 60?
