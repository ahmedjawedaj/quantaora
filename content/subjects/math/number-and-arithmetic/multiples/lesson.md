# Multiples

Multiples of a number are the results of multiplying it by 1, 2, 3, 4... — multiples of 3 are 3, 6, 9, 12.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a skip-counting track with a marker hopping from 3 to 6 to 9 to 12 and beyond, and a times-table strip below showing 3 × 1 = 3, 3 × 2 = 6, 3 × 3 = 9. Tap `hs-multiple-example` to label the results.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Tap the marker forward along the track and watch it land on each multiple in turn. Switch the base number with the slider — set it to 4 and watch the track rearrange to 4, 8, 12, 16 — then drag the marker far to the right to see the multiples continue past the visible edge.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-multiple-example` to label a multiple, `hs-times-table` to label the multiplication that generates it, and `hs-common-multiple` to highlight a number that appears on two different multiples lists.

## 4. Explain what it does

A multiple is base × whole number. 3, 6, 9, 12 are multiples of 3 because each is 3 times a counting number. The list never ends. When two lists share a number — like 12 on both 3's and 4's lists — that is a common multiple.

## 5. See where it belongs

Multiples belong to Number Relationships. They come from multiplication, pair with factors (12 is a multiple of 3; 3 is a factor of 12), and feed fraction work, where common multiples become common denominators.

**Practice item:** Set the slider to 5 and list the first five multiples aloud by tapping the marker along the track.

## 6. Compare it

Compare multiples with factors. Multiples are produced by a number (3, 6, 9, 12...). Factors are numbers that multiply together to make it (factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12). One is 'what it makes', the other is 'what it's made of'.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to solve a real problem: *Every 4th block in a long row is blue. Which block numbers will be blue?* Read the multiples of 4 — 4, 8, 12, 16 — to answer.

**Practice item:** The model shows multiples of 4. Find a number on the track that is ALSO a multiple of 3, and explain why it is a common multiple.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What are the first four multiples of 6, and how do multiples and factors describe the same number relationship?
