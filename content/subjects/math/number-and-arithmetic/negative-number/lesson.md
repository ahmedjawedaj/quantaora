# Negative Numbers

A negative number is any number below zero, shown left of zero on the number line.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a number line stretching from −5 to 5 with a thermometer marker above zero and a frost-tinted block below zero. Tap `hs-negative-side` to label the negative half of the line.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag the marker to −4 and watch the thermometer drop below freezing, tinting the scene frost-cold. Then drag to 3 and back to −2, noting which numbers sit left of zero. Use the arrow control to run −3 + (−2) — the marker steps left from −3 and lands on −5.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-negative-side` to label negative numbers, `hs-zero` to label the zero boundary, and `hs-add-negative` to label the 'adding moves left' arrow.

## 4. Explain what it does

Negatives describe below-zero amounts: −5° is five below freezing. On the number line, left is smaller, so −3 is greater than −5. Adding a negative steps left; subtracting a negative steps right (4 − (−2) = 6, because taking away a debt is a gain).

## 5. See where it belongs

Negative numbers extend the number line left of zero, extending Number Sense into amounts below zero. They build on subtraction and are essential in algebra, where equations can have negative answers.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you drag the marker, predict: is −5 greater or less than −2? Now check on the line.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a negative number with its positive mirror. −3 and 3 are the same distance from zero but on opposite sides. Every negative number is less than every positive number, even −0.1 < 0.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to solve a real problem: *The temperature is 2°C and drops 5 degrees. What is the new temperature?* Start the marker at 2 and subtract 5 — step left five times — to find −3°C.

**Practice item:** Run 4 − (−2) on the model and explain why the result is higher than 4.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** Order −4, 2, and −1 from smallest to largest, and explain why subtracting a negative number makes a result larger.
