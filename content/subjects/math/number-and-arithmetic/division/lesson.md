# Division

Division splits a number into equal groups or shares it evenly.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a mat holding 12 cubes, next to it a set of 4 empty sharing bowls, and a repeated-subtraction track showing 12 − 4 − 4 − 4 = 0. Tap `hs-dividend` to label the 12 cubes, and `hs-divisor` to label the 4 bowls.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Drag the 12 cubes, one at a time, into the 4 bowls until all are shared evenly — each bowl gets 3. Then watch the repeated-subtraction track step down from 12 to 8 to 4 to 0 as each group of 4 is dealt.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-dividend`, `hs-divisor`, and `hs-quotient` to label the three parts of the division. Tap `hs-repeated-subtraction` to label the track showing the count of groups.

## 4. Explain what it does

Division splits a total into equal groups: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 means 4 equal groups of 3. The dividend (12) is shared, the divisor (4) is the group size, and the quotient (3) is how many in each group. It is the inverse of multiplication and can be read as repeated subtraction.

## 5. See where it belongs

Division belongs to Arithmetic. It is the inverse of multiplication, builds on subtraction as repeated subtraction, and connects to factors — a divisor that leaves no remainder is a factor.

**Practice item:** Share 15 cubes into 5 bowls on the model. Say the division fact and quotient aloud.

## 6. Compare it

Compare division with multiplication. Multiplication makes products from equal groups; division takes a product back apart. 3 × 4 = 12, 12 ÷ 4 = 3, and 12 ÷ 3 = 4 all belong to one fact family.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to solve a real sharing problem: *20 cookies are shared equally among 4 friends. How many cookies does each friend get?* Deal the cookies into 4 bowls and read the quotient.

**Practice item:** Share 14 cubes into 4 bowls. Describe what is left over after each bowl gets 3 — that leftover is the remainder.
