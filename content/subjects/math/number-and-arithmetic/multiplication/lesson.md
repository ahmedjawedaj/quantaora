# Multiplication

Multiplication is repeated addition of equal groups — 3 × 4 means three groups of four, which is 12.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a 3-by-4 dot array on a mat — 3 rows, each with 4 dots — and a number line below that has been marked with three jumps of 4. Tap `hs-factors` to label the 3 (rows) and 4 (columns), and `hs-product` to label the total of 12 dots.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Tap the dots one group at a time to watch the array build from repeated addition: 4, 8, 12. Then drag the array to rotate it 90 degrees — a 4-by-3 grid with the same 12 dots. Watch the product label stay the same.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-factors` to label the factors, `hs-product` to label the product, and `hs-array` to label the array that models the multiplication.

## 4. Explain what it does

Multiplication combines equal groups. 3 × 4 = 12 means 'three groups of four'. An array shows it as rows × columns. It is commutative — flipping the array gives the same total — and division is its inverse: 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

## 5. See where it belongs

Multiplication belongs to Arithmetic. It builds on addition (repeated addition), pairs with division as its inverse, and connects to factors since 3 and 4 are factors of 12.

**Practice item:** On the array, tap out 2 rows of 5 dots and say the multiplication fact and product aloud.

## 6. Compare it

Compare multiplication with addition. Addition joins any groups — equal or not. Multiplication only works for equal groups: 3 + 4 is not 3 × 4. When the groups are equal, multiplication is the shortcut.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the array to solve a real problem: *A chocolate box has 6 rows with 4 chocolates each. How many chocolates in all?* Read the array to find the product, then flip it and check the product is unchanged.

**Practice item:** Build 4 × 6 on the array. Say which multiplication fact undoes it (the division pair) and explain why.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** Using factor, product, and array, explain how to find 3 × 5 and why 3 × 5 = 5 × 3.
