# Force

*Physics > Mechanics > Forces > Forces*

A force is a push or a pull — an arrow with a size in newtons and a direction. In this model you grab a wooden box between two arrow-makers and pull with two forces at once, then watch the box slide only when the arrows stop cancelling.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a warm wooden box sitting on a mat, with two long arrows — one coming from the left, one from the right — touching the box. A scale plate floats beside it. Tap `hs-force-arrows` to label the two force arrows, `hs-force-box` to label the box, and `hs-force-scale` to see the scale that measures each pull in newtons.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom around the box. Drag the handle of each arrow to change how hard it pulls, and watch the scale read newtons. Make both arrows the same length — notice the box doesn't budge. Then stretch one arrow longer than the other and press Go: the box slides toward the stronger pull. Feel what balanced vs. unbalanced does to motion.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-force-arrows` to label the two opposing pulls, `hs-net-force` to label the leftover net arrow that appears when the two don't cancel, and `hs-force-scale` to name the newton scale reading.

## 4. Explain what it does

A force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons, drawn as an arrow because it has direction. Two forces can partly or fully cancel. When they cancel exactly, net force = 0 N and motion doesn't change. When they don't, a net force points toward the stronger pull — and only that net force makes the box accelerate.

## 5. See where it belongs

Force lives in Mechanics, inside the Forces unit, with mass, gravity, friction, and the normal force. It connects back to acceleration (net force causes it) and forward to momentum — every object with unbalanced forces is building or changing momentum.

**Prediction prompt:** Two teams pull a crate: left team pulls with 80 N, right team with 60 N. Predict the net force and direction before you set the two arrows.

## 6. Compare it

Compare force with momentum. A force is a push or pull happening right now — an arrow you can draw on the box. Momentum is the motion the box already has, like a freight train that keeps rolling. The force is what changes the momentum; it isn't the momentum itself.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to win a real tug: *A crate has two forces: 100 N left and 40 N right. Find the net force and the way the crate will move.* Set the arrows, read the net arrow, and state your answer.

**Practice item:** Make the two arrows exactly cancel. Then add 15 N to one side and describe what the net force becomes and what the box does when you press Go.

## 8. Review it later

**Prediction prompt:** If the box is already sliding at steady speed and both forces become equal, what happens to its motion?

**Checkpoint:** What is a force, what unit measures it, and why does the box only accelerate when the two forces don't cancel?
