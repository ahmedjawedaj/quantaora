# Velocity

*Physics > Mechanics > Describing Motion > Speed and Velocity*

Velocity is speed with a direction — the full answer to "how fast, which way?" In this model a car drives a loop track while an arrow attached to it always points the way it's heading; watch the arrow turn even when the speed needle holds still.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a round track laid flat, a bead-sized car gliding along it, and a long arrow sprouting from the car's nose. A floating readout shows the car's speed as a number and its velocity as the arrow. Tap `hs-velocity-arrow` to label the arrow, `hs-speed-value` to label the raw speed, and `hs-direction-label` to prove velocity = speed + direction.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in on the track. Run the car at a fixed speed and watch the arrow stay the same length (same speed!) while it slowly spins to keep pointing where the car heads. Pause the car, switch the direction slider to reverse, and watch the arrow flip 180° — the velocity changed while the speed never moved.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-velocity-arrow` to label the velocity arrow itself, `hs-direction-label` to label the compass ring that gives the arrow its direction words, and `hs-speed-value` to label the speed number that rides on the arrow's length. Spot the `hs-negative-arrow` note on the diagram when the car backtracks.

## 4. Explain what it does

Velocity = speed plus its direction. Mathematically, velocity = displacement ÷ time. Because a direction is tacked on, velocity is a vector — so two cars doing exactly 3 m/s, one north and one south, have different velocities. What the arrow shows is that constant speed doesn't mean constant velocity: on the loop, speed is steady yet the arrow always turns, so velocity is always changing.

## 5. See where it belongs

Velocity stands inside Mechanics' Describing Motion unit, a full step past speed. It's the bridge to acceleration (which is literally any change in velocity), and from acceleration come forces, momentum, and everything else that follows.

**Prediction prompt:** If the car does one full loop at the same speedometer reading, predict the size of the velocity arrow — same, bigger, smaller — and where it points at the end.

## 6. Compare it

Compare velocity with speed. A car has one speed on the straight; on the curve it still has the same speed number, but a whole new velocity every instant — the arrow is always repointing. Only vector quantities can be 'same size, different result' — a scalar (speed) can't distinguish the curve.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the loop to check a real situation: *A cyclist rounds a hairpin turn at a constant 15 km/h. Describe how the cyclist's velocity changes at the turn.* Run the car through the loop and read the arrow.

**Practice item:** Set the car to constant speed forward, then flip direction reverse before rerunning. State two things that changed (velocity) and one that didn't (speed).

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** How is velocity different from speed, and why does turning at constant speed still change velocity?
