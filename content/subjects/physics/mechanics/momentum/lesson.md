# Momentum

*Physics > Mechanics > Momentum > Momentum*

Momentum is how much 'motion' an object carries — mass times velocity, with direction. In this model two carts on a track roll into each other; set their masses and speeds, press Go, and watch them swap velocity while the total momentum banner never changes.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a straight track with two carts facing each other. Each cart wears a velocity arrow, and a banner floats overhead with the total momentum. Tap `hs-cart-a` to label the left cart, `hs-cart-b` to label the right cart, and `hs-momentum-bar` to see the total momentum readout.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom along the track. Set Cart A's mass slider to 2 kg and Cart B's to 6 kg; set Cart A rolling right and Cart B moving left. Press Go and watch the collision — the lighter cart bounces harder while the total banner holds steady. Tweak the mass knobs and rerun; the conservation banner is the truth the carts can't change.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-mass-slider` to label the mass knob, `hs-velocity-arrow` to label the direction-and-size arrow, `hs-p-value` to label the momentum value (mass × velocity) on each cart, and `hs-momentum-bar` to label the total conserved.

## 4. Explain what it does

Momentum = mass × velocity (p = mv). A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s has 6 kg·m/s of momentum. It has a direction, so it's a vector. When the carts collide, momentum flows between them — one loses what the other gains — so the total before and after stays exactly the same. That's conservation of momentum.

## 5. See where it belongs

Momentum belongs in the Mechanics Momentum unit, built directly on mass and velocity. It's the doorway to collisions and impulse (force × time), and later it connects to energy — two different ways of 'banking' what's happening in a crash.

**Prediction prompt:** A 4 kg cart moves at 2 m/s. Predict its momentum in kg·m/s before you set it on the model.

## 6. Compare it

Compare momentum with force. Force is a push or pull applied right now. Momentum is the motion an object already has. When the carts touch, the contact force transfers momentum — the force is the mover, the momentum is what moves between them, and the total stays fixed.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to size up a real collision: *A 3 kg ball moving at 4 m/s meets a 2 kg ball moving the opposite way at 3 m/s. Compute each momentum, then check the total before and after on the banner.* Set the sliders and read the numbers.

**Practice item:** Set Cart A to 2 kg at 4 m/s and Cart B at rest. Predict the total momentum before the collision, then confirm the banner reads the same total after impact.
