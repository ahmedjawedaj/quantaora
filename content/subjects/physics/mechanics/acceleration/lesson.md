# Acceleration

*Physics > Mechanics > Describing Motion > Acceleration > Acceleration*

Acceleration is how quickly velocity changes. In this model you drive a cart with two controls — a gas pedal and a brake — and watch a velocity-time graph bend as the cart speeds up, cruises, and stops.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a small cart on a straight mat, a gas and brake control beside it, and a lifted velocity-time screen showing two tilted axes. Tap `hs-cart` to see the cart itself, `hs-velocity-graph` to see the screen it will share, and `hs-accel-arrow` for the live acceleration number.

## 2. Touch it

Zoom and rotate around the cart. Push Gas and watch the cart surge forward while the graph line tilts up steeply. Then let off — the line goes flat (velocity steady). Now press Brake: the line bends DOWN as the cart slows. Each run retraces how fast the velocity was changing.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-gas` to label the acceleration control, `hs-brake` to label the deceleration control, `hs-velocity-graph` to label the velocity-time line, and `hs-accel-arrow` to see the little arrow drawn on the readout that shows the sign of the acceleration.

## 4. Explain what it does

Acceleration names *how fast velocity changes*. The calculation is (final velocity − initial velocity) ÷ time. Gas makes the change positive; brake makes it negative — but both are accelerations. Let the cart roll on flat ground with no gas again; the graph line holds steady and reads zero acceleration, even though the cart is moving.

## 5. See where it belongs

Acceleration belongs in Mechanics, in Describing Motion, in the unit that takes you from position through velocity to this one. It's the meeting point with Forces: pull the gas and you practically feel Newton's second law, F = ma, waiting next door.

**Prediction prompt:** A cart goes from rest to 15 m/s in 10 s. Predict its acceleration in m/s² before you press Gas and let the graph check you.

## 6. Compare it

Compare acceleration and velocity. Velocity: how fast — 15 m/s, a real arrow. Acceleration: how fast the 15 changes per second. The two are layers, not rivals. The graph literally shows both at once — the velocity curve's *slope* is the acceleration, and a straight rising line means steady acceleration, while a breaking down tells you it's slowing.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to test a real story: *A motorcycle goes from 0 to 20 m/s in 4 seconds. What is its acceleration, and what would the graph look like?* Drive it, read the m/s², and trace the graph.

**Practice item:** Drive the cart, reach some speed, then brake to a stop. Sketch whether the graph pointed up, down, or straight for each phase, and label which phase has an negative acceleration.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** Define acceleration, give its formula and unit, and name two different ways velocity can change (giving acceleration a positive or negative sign).
