# Normal Force

*Physics > Mechanics > Forces > Normal force*

The normal force is the push a surface gives back — the reason a book rests on a table instead of dropping through it. In this model you watch a book on a table, add extra press, then tilt the table into a ramp and see the support arrow shrink as the slope rises.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a table with a book resting on its top, and a blue arrow rising straight up from the tabletop to the book's underside. A small hand-press button sits at the side. Tap `hs-book` to label the book, `hs-normal-arrow` to label the upward push, and `hs-ramp-tilt` to label the table surface's tilt control.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom to tabletop level and see the arrow meet the book from directly below at a right angle. Press the hand-press button: the arrow grows. Now grab the tilt handle and lift the table edge into a ramp — watch the arrow rotate to stay perpendicular to the surface and shrink as the ramp climbs.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-normal-arrow` to label the normal force — perpendicular to the surface, `hs-weight-arrow` to label the downward weight arrow it's balancing, `hs-press-button` to label the extra press control, and `hs-ramp-tilt` to label the tilt that slims the support.

## 4. Explain what it does

A surface always pushes back perpendicular (at a right angle) to itself — that push is the normal force. On a flat table it balances the book's weight, pushing up exactly as hard as gravity pulls down. Press on the book and the normal force grows to include your press. Tilt the table and the surface now only needs to support part of the weight, so the perpendicular arrow shrinks while the weight arrow stays the same.

## 5. See where it belongs

The normal force lives in the Forces unit, shoulder to shoulder with gravity, weight, and friction. On flat ground it equals the weight; on ramps and in accelerating lifts it breaks free from that simple match — and it's the force that pairs with friction when things slide.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you tilt the table, predict what happens to the size of the blue arrow as the ramp climbs toward vertical.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the normal force with friction. Both come from a surface touching an object, but the normal force pushes PERPENDICULAR to the surface (holds things up), while friction pushes ALONG the surface (resists sliding). One keeps the book on the table; the other decides whether it slides off.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to explain a familiar feeling: *In a fast elevator that starts upward, you feel heavy — then at the top you feel light. Set the model to elevator mode and explain how the normal force changes in each phase.* Read the arrow changes.

**Practice item:** Place the book flat, then tilt the ramp halfway. State how the normal arrow changes in size and direction, and why the weight arrow stays the same.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What is the normal force, what direction does it always point, and why does it not always equal the object's weight?
