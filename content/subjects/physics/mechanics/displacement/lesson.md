# Displacement

*Physics > Mechanics > Describing Motion > Position and Displacement*

Displacement is the net change in position — one straight arrow from start to finish, no matter how long the road. In this model you chain together journey arrows (4 m north, then 3 m east) and watch them tip-to-tail merge into a single net arrow.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a flat grid with two dots — a blue start dot and a red target dot — connected by one short, straight, orange arrow. Around the grid, a grey, dotted path winds far around. Tap `hs-displacement-arrow` to label the net arrow, `hs-start-position` to label the start, and `hs-final-position` to label the finish.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom over the grid. Drag the start dot to a new square — the orange arrow between start and target instantly redraws as the new shortest connection. Then use the `+` button to add journey legs and watch the model merge them tip-to-tail into one single net arrow.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-displacement-arrow` to label the net displacement with its size and direction (arrow pointing NE), `hs-start-position` to label the initial position, and `hs-final-position` to label the final position. Tap `hs-zero-test` to see a special case: start and end at the same dot leaves a zero-magnitude arrow.

## 4. Explain what it does

Displacement is the straight-line change in position from the starting dot to the closing dot. It has a direction, so it's a vector. Add up a chain of legs: if your path loops out and ends where it began, displacement is zero even though you traveled. The model always shows you the one net arrow.

## 5. See where it belongs

Displacement sits in Mechanics, in Describing Motion, next to position and distance. It's the direction-carrying cousin that powers velocity = displacement ÷ time, and later shows up again whenever motion quantities add like vectors — in momentum and in net force.

**Prediction prompt:** The grid shows a fly that buzzes 5 m east then 5 m west. Predict what the displacement arrow will look like — how long, pointing which way — before you press the Forge button.

## 6. Compare it

Compare displacement with distance: displacement = net straight line from start to stop (can be zero), distance equals the total bumped path (always grows). Both on the same grid: hike the whole loop and the dotted distance reads 24 m while displacement sits at zero — the arrow and the road are telling two different truths.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to answer a route bug: *A kayak paddles 3 m north, then 4 m east. Build the journey and read off the net displacement — the straight arrow from launch to rest.* Drag the legs and let the model merge them.

**Practice item:** Build a two-leg journey east 4 m then east 2 m. Read both distance traveled and displacement, and say which total is always smaller.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What makes displacement different from distance, why can it be zero after a long walk, and what direction information must it always include?
