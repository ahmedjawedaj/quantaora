# Gravity

*Physics > Mechanics > Forces > Gravity*

Gravity is the force that pulls masses together — it makes apples fall, keeps the Moon circling Earth, and gives everything its weight. In this model you drop a bowling ball and a feather inside a vacuum tube, watch field arrows point to the planet's heart, and send a satellite around an orbit.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a tall transparent vacuum tube with a bowling ball and a feather resting at its top, a glowing planet beside it with thin arrows pointing inward, and a satellite tracing a circle around it. Tap `hs-falling-balls` to label the tube's contents, `hs-field-arrows` to label the inward arrows, and `hs-satellite-orbit` to label the orbiting satellite.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom around the scene. Press Drop and watch the two objects fall — pay attention to whether they hit the bottom at the same moment. Then drag the satellite closer to the planet and watch its path tighten; drag it out and the orbit widens. Tip the tube and the arrows still point to the planet's center, no matter how you turn.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-planet` to label the planet that makes the gravity, `hs-falling-balls` to label the heavy and light objects, `hs-satellite-orbit` to label the path gravity forces the satellite to follow, and `hs-gravity-accel` to label the 9.8 m/s² tag on Earth's field arrows.

## 4. Explain what it does

Gravity is a force that pulls every mass toward every other mass. Earth's pull accelerates everything at about 9.8 m/s² — the bowling ball and the feather accelerate the same, because the ball's extra mass needs a bigger force but also resists more; the two effects cancel. Gravity points toward the planet's center (that's the field arrows), and that same inward pull is what keeps the satellite circling instead of flying off straight.

## 5. See where it belongs

Gravity lives in the Mechanics Forces unit, right beside mass and weight — it is the origin of weight (W = mg). It reaches beyond mechanics too, governing the solar system and connecting to every force that keeps things from flying apart.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you press Drop, predict: which object — the bowling ball or the feather — hits the bottom of the vacuum tube first?

## 6. Compare it

Compare gravity with friction. Gravity is a long-range force that pulls masses together — it works through empty space, no contact needed. Friction is a contact force between surfaces that usually opposes motion. Drop a ball and roll a ball: gravity starts the fall, friction stops the roll — two very different forces doing different jobs.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to solve an orbit puzzle: *A satellite needs to circle closer to Earth to orbit faster. Drag the satellite inward and describe what happens to its path — and say what force is holding it there.* Read the orbit ring and the field arrows.

**Practice item:** Drop the bowling ball and the feather, note that they land together, then tilt the tube and explain why the field arrows still all point toward the planet's center.
