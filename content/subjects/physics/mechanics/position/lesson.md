# Position

*Physics > Mechanics > Describing Motion > Position and Displacement*

Position answers the question "where is it?" — but only compared to something else. This model puts a child on a long number line: slide the child forward and back, read off its position, then drag the reference point and watch the same location get a brand-new number.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a long warm-wooden number line on a mat with a child character standing on it. A bright marker arrow points from the line's reference point (at 0) to the child. Tap `hs-position-value` to see the child's position read as a distance and direction, and `hs-reference-point` to show what everything is measured from.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in along the number line. Drag the child slider to move it left and right and watch the readout update. Then grab the reference-point slider and slide the 0-marker — the child stays put without moving, yet the position number flips. Play with it until you can predict the new number before you let go.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-position-value` to label the object's position, `hs-reference-point` to label the spot everything is measured from, and `hs-direction` to label the arrow that tells you which side of 0 the object sits on. Tap `hs-grid` to switch the line into a 2D grid and label the coordinate pair.

## 4. Explain what it does

Position answers "where?" The answer is always a recipe: a reference point (the fixed 0), a distance (how far), and a direction (which way) — like "6 m right of the gate." On a grid it's two distances, one for each axis, written as a pair like (3, 2). Position is relative, which is the whole trick: move the reference point and the numbers change even though the object went nowhere.

## 5. See where it belongs

Position belongs to Mechanics, the study of how things move — and it's step one. It sits inside the Describing Motion unit, first among the words we use to say what's going on with movement: position, then distance, displacement, speed, velocity, and acceleration.

**Prediction prompt:** If the child stands 7 m RIGHT of the marker and you move the reference marker 2 m to the right, does the child's position number get bigger or smaller? Predict it before you drag the slider.

## 6. Compare it

Compare position with distance. Position points to a spot — "the book is 2 m left of the lamp" — while distance measures a path length — "I walked 10 m." Direction makes position a full answer; distance cares only about how much. On the model, once the child moves 3 m right then 3 m back, its distance traveled is 6 m but its position is back where it started.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the number line to solve a real puzzle: *A rabbit sits 4 m to the RIGHT of its burrow. Where is the rabbit's position if you measure instead from the burrow the opposite way?* Re-drag the child, switch direction, and read off your answer.

**Practice item:** On the model, set the child to position -3 m, then set the reference point to +2 m, and state the new position reading. Say what you kept the same and what changed.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What three parts does a complete description of position need, and why does moving the reference point change the number even though the object never moved?
