# Speed

*Physics > Mechanics > Describing Motion > Speed and Velocity*

Speed is how fast — the distance covered every second. In this model a race car rolls around a track in front of a motion sensor, and the sensor draws a distance-time graph the moment you run.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a straight track beside a wall-mounted motion sensor. A small car sits at the start. A graph screen floats above, ready to draw. Tap `hs-speed-car` to label the moving car, `hs-speed-sensor` to label the motion sensor, and `hs-speed-graph` to preview the distance-time graph it will draw.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom around the track. Set the car's speed on the slider (slow, medium, fast), then press Run. Watch the car roll, the distance ticker climb, and the graph draw the familiar straight slanted line. Try bumping speed to "stop once" and see the graph flatten — while the car is stopped, distance is not climbing.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-speed-car` to label the moving car's instant speed readout, `hs-speed-sensor` to label how the motion sensor measures it, and `hs-speed-graph` to label the slope of the distance-time line as the speed value.

## 4. Explain what it does

Speed asks "how much distance per unit time?" and answers with one number: distance ÷ time. Steady 2 m/s means every second that passes, the car gains 2 more meters — so the distance-time line climbs at a steady slope. Faster speed = steeper slope. When standing still, the slope is flat — distance not increasing.

## 5. See where it belongs

Speed lives in Mechanics, in the Describing Motion unit, right next to velocity and acceleration. It is the first quantity that ties distance (how much) to time (how long), and it's the steer from position to the whole family of motion measurements.

**Prediction prompt:** The car runs 40 m in 8 s. Predict the car's average speed before you read the model — then check it on the sensor.

## 6. Compare it

Compare speed with velocity. Speed just says how fast — 60 km/h with no direction. Velocity says how fast AND which way — 60 km/h north. The same trip down the street is one speed; down and back are two different velocities. The graph for speed records only the size; velocity would need an arrow.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to time an actual race: *A train covers 300 km in 3 hours. What was its average speed in km/h?* Set the track to scale, press Run, and read the answer the graph shows.

**Practice item:** Run the car, then pause it for 2 s mid-run and restart. What happens to the distance-time graph during the pause, and what does that tell you about the car's speed in that moment?
