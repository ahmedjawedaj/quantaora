# Weight

*Physics > Mechanics > Forces > Mass and Weight*

Weight is the force of gravity pulling on an object's mass. In this model you hang a backpack from a spring scale, read the pull in newtons, then spin a location dial to the Moon and watch the needle fall to about a sixth — while the kg tag never changes.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a metal spring scale mounted on a stand with a hook at the bottom, a backpack hanging from the hook, and a location dial beside it with Earth, Moon, and space icons. Tap `hs-weight-backpack` to label the object, `hs-spring-scale` to label the scale, and `hs-location-dial` to label the world selector.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom around the scale. Read the stretched spring's needle against the newton marks — the marker points at the pull. Then grab the location dial and spin it to the Moon: the needle slides down to about a sixth of its reading. Spin to space: the needle eases to zero while the backpack still hangs there, full of the same matter.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-newton-mark` to label the newton scale on the spring, `hs-weight-value` to label the force reading in newtons, `hs-kg-tag` to label the mass tag that doesn't move, and `hs-location-dial` to label the dial that changes the gravity.

## 4. Explain what it does

Weight is a force — the pull of gravity on an object's mass. The formula is weight = mass × g, where g is how strong gravity is where you are (about 9.8 m/s² on Earth, 1.6 on the Moon). Hanging the same 5 kg backpack: 49 N on Earth, 8 N on the Moon. The mass tag stayed 5 kg; only the force (the needle) changed.

## 5. See where it belongs

Weight lives in the Mechanics Forces unit, between mass and gravity. It is one of the everyday forces you feel constantly, and it sits beside the normal force (the ground pushing back up) that keeps you from sinking into the floor.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you spin the dial to the Moon, predict how far the spring needle will fall — half, a sixth, or double — and why.

## 6. Compare it

Compare weight with mass. Weight is a force in newtons, and it changes with gravity. Mass is matter in kilograms, and it doesn't. On the Moon the spring needle reads a sixth while the kg tag says the same number; the two quantities are answering different questions about the very same object.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to pack for a trip: *An astronaut's suit has a mass of 60 kg. On Earth the scale reads 588 N. Drag the dial to the Moon and decide what the suit weighs there, and what its mass still is.* Read the model's answers.

**Practice item:** Hang the 2 kg weight block, note the newton reading on Earth, spin to the Moon, and write the new weight plus the unchanged mass in one sentence.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What exactly is weight, what units measure it, and why does the same object weigh less on the Moon but keep its full mass?
