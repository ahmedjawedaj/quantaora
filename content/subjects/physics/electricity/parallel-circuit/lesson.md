# Parallel Circuit

*Physics > Electricity > Circuits > Building Circuits*

In a parallel circuit every component rides its own branch between two shared rails — every bulb gets the full battery voltage, each branch works independently, and the current splits then rejoins at the junctions. This model wires three bulbs on three separate tracks.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a top and bottom rail, three bulbs each on their own branch between them, and a battery feeding both rails. Tap `hs-top-rail` to label the shared entry, `hs-branch-a` / `hs-branch-b` / `hs-branch-c` to label each bulb lane, and `hs-bottom-rail` to label where the branches rejoin.

## 2. Touch it

Unplug the middle bulb (`hs-remove-a`): the other two keep shining. Plug it back and watch all three hold steady at full brightness. Now sweep the meter around the junction (`hs-junction`) and watch the current numbers split as it passes, then recombine toward the battery.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-top-rail` to label the shared rail, `hs-branch-a` / `hs-branch-b` / `hs-branch-c` for the three lanes, `hs-battery` to label the source all branches share, and `hs-junction-a` for the place current splits.

## 4. Explain what it does

Every branch connects straight across the battery, so each bulb sees the FULL voltage — three bulbs on 3 V each feel 3 V, not a third each. Because the branches are independent, unscrewing one doesn't touch the others. And the current isn't one number everywhere: it splits at the top junction into the branches, then recombines at the bottom — the battery's total current equals the sum of the branch currents.

## 5. See where it belongs

Parallel is the second great arrangement in Electricity's Circuits unit — the one real homes adopt because a single bulb break must never darken a whole room. The battery (push), current (flow), and bulbs (loads) all show up again, this time on lanes.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you unplug the middle bulb, predict what happens to the other two — then pull it and check.

## 6. Compare it

Compare parallel with series: series gives one shared loop, equal current, shared volts, one-breaks-all; parallel gives separate lanes, full voltage each, independent survival. The two models are the same bulbs on opposite topologies — flip between them on one board.

## 7. Use it in a task

Decide the wiring: *Your bedroom has three lights that must each keep glowing if any other bulb fails. Should you wire them in series or parallel, and why?* Use the model to prove your answer.
**Practice item:** Unplug and replace each branch bulb in turn; note which stay lit each time and state the parallel rule your test proved.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What are the two defining rules of a parallel circuit, and why does one removed bulb leave the others lit?
