# Electric Charge

*Physics > Electricity > Electric Charge > Charge and Current*

Electric charge is the property that pushes particles together or apart — the root of all electricity. In this model two charge balls sit on a bench, and you can set their signs, read the force arrows, and let a spark rebalance them.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a bench holding two charge balls, thin arrows stretched between them, and a slide switch on the bench's side. Tap `hs-ball-p` to label the positive ball, `hs-ball-n` to label the negative ball, and `hs-arrows` to label the force arrows between them.

## 2. Touch it

Give the two balls opposite signs and watch the arrows pull them toward each other: attraction. Flip one ball to the same sign and the arrows shove them apart: repulsion. Now tap `hs-discharge` — a spark jumps, the balls settle, and the arrows drop to zero as the charges rebalance through a path.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-ball-p` to label the positive ball, `hs-ball-n` to label the negative ball, and `hs-discharge` to label the spark path where charge flows.

## 4. Explain what it does

Charge comes in two kinds: positive and negative. The golden rule of charge: like signs repel, opposite signs attract. The electrons (negative) are the mobile part — rubbing shifts a few from one object to another, leaving an imbalance. That imbalance pushes on everything and, given a path such as the spark, marches as flowing charge — the start of electric current.

## 5. See where it belongs

Charge is the root of the Electricity branch's Electric Charge unit, standing just before current. Every other model — current, bulb, battery, circuits — reassembles the electrons this bench shows stranded. Give this imbalance a wire and that tiny 'flows' step becomes the next model's job.

**Prediction prompt:** Put opposite signs on the balls, predict the arrow direction, then release and check your prediction.

## 6. Compare it

Compare positive with negative charge: protons (positive) sit tight inside the nucleus, while electrons (negative) are the light carriers that actually move. The two balls feel equal force toward each other — same strength, opposite wants. Put like signs up and the pair repels; opposite and they attract; the rule never flips.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to win a balloon debate: *You rub a balloon on your hair: electrons move from the hair to the balloon. Which ball is the balloon, and which sign is left on your hair? Set the two balls, read the arrows, answer.*
**Practice item:** Set up attraction on the bench, then repulsion; write both charge pairs and the rule that predicted each arrow.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What are the two kinds of charge, and how do the arrows decide between attraction and repulsion?
