# Switch

A switch is a gate in the circuit that opens or closes the road for current. This model puts a lever switch on a loop with a bulb, so you can be the doorman.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a small switch base with a rocker lever between two contact posts, wired into a loop with a bulb and battery. Tap `hs-lever` to label the rocker, `hs-posts` to label the contact posts, and `hs-wire` to label the wires in and out.

## 2. Touch it

Flip the lever closed (`hs-lever`): the bulb snaps on and the ammeter jumps. Flip it open: the glow dies and the needle drops to zero the same instant. Now slide the ammeter knob up to a big current and watch the switch's contacts start to warm — that's the 'too many amps' warning.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-base` to label the switch body, `hs-posts` to label the metal contacts that touch when closed, and `hs-wire` to label the path that stops or flows.

## 4. Explain what it does

Closed switch = contacts touching = loop complete = current flows. Open switch = a physical gap = loop broken = current stops everywhere in that series path. It has no middle setting: the switch is purely an on/off gate. Because it decides the road, the whole loop obeys instantly — and because current can overheat a too-small switch, safety ratings matter.

## 5. See where it belongs

The switch is the gatekeeper of Electricity's Components unit, sitting between source and load. Paired with the battery (push) and bulb (load), it completes the classic trio: push, gate, payoff. Series and parallel circuits (next models) will lean on exactly this on/off idea.

**Prediction prompt:** With the lever open, predict the ammeter reading; close it and confirm the jump.

## 6. Compare it

Compare open and closed on the same loop: one click, and the whole circuit obeys. The switch itself adds no brightness and no drag — it only opens or closes the road. That's its whole personality, and it's why 'just a switch' can still stop a dangerous current stone-cold.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to plan a room light: *You want one wall switch to control a lamp. Where on the loop must it sit, and what does it do to the bulb when opened? Answer, then flip the model's lever to prove it.*
**Practice item:** Flip the switch five times; record bulb state and ammeter for each, and state what a 'closed' switch physically means.
