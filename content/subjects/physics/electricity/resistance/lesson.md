# Resistance

Resistance is how much a material fights the flow of current, measured in ohms. This model slides a resistor block along a rail you can lengthen, and a material switch pulls copper or nichrome into the path.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a long slide rail, a resistor block sliding on it, an ammeter at the power corner, a bulb, and a material switch. Tap `hs-block` to name the resistor, `hs-rail` to name the current path, and `hs-ammeter` to name the amp gauge.

## 2. Touch it

Slide the block far from the battery (`hs-lengthen`) so current must cross a long path: the ammeter sags and the bulb dims. Slide it back (`hs-shorten`) and the needle and bulb surge as the drag shrinks. Now flip the material switch `hs-material` to copper and even the long path pours current — resistance is geometry AND material.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-ohm` to label the ohms readout, `hs-bulb` to label the dimmer brightens with the drag, and `hs-material` to label the copper/nichrome switch.

## 4. Explain what it does

Resistance is the opposition a material offers to moving charge. The same voltage has to push against it, and current = voltage ÷ resistance (Ohm's law). Lengthen the path, current drops; switch to a better conductor, it soars. The bulb is the referee — more resistance always means less current and dimmer light.

## 5. See where it belongs

Resistance is the drag half of Electricity's Voltage and Resistance unit. Voltage pushes, resistance holds back: the two decide the current. Conductors and insulators (a later model) are the far ends of the same continuum — the rail shows every point of that journey.

**Prediction prompt:** With the block slid long, predict the ammeter reading after you switch to copper — then measure it.

## 6. Compare it

Compare resistance with voltage: both shape current. Voltage is the push making it faster; resistance is the drag making it slower. On one loop you can slide the push up and the drag down — the bulb's brightness is the report card of both. Understand the pair and circuits become a balance of forces: pedal to the metal, brake to the road.

## 7. Use it in a task

Design a dim lamp: *You want the bulb dim, NOT off. Explain in one sentence whether to lengthen the resistor path or switch to copper — then set the model to check.*
**Practice item:** Read the ammeter with the block at two positions and with the two materials; rank all four by current.
