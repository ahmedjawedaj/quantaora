# Voltage

*Physics > Electricity > Voltage > Voltage and Resistance*

Voltage is the pressure pushing current around a circuit. This model shows a battery bank you can stack cell by cell, with a voltmeter and a bulb reading the push.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a battery bank holding 1.5 V cells, a wire loop to a bulb, and a voltmeter straddling the bank. Tap `hs-cells` to label the battery bank, `hs-voltmeter` to label the volt readout, and `hs-bulb` to label the bulb that feels the push.

## 2. Touch it

Tap `hs-add-cell` to slide a new 1.5 V cell into the stack: the voltmeter ticks up and the bulb glows brighter. Remove a cell (`hs-remove-cell`) and the meter and bulb sink together. The push and the light move as one.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-voltmeter` to label the two probes reading volts, `hs-cells` to label the 1.5 V cells, and `hs-loop` to label the wire that loops the current back to the battery.

## 4. Explain what it does

Voltage is the pressure or potential difference that pushes current. Increase the volts and, with the same wire and bulb, more current flows — the 'push' and 'flow' are partners, with voltage the boss of the pair. The model makes the link visible: every added cell raises the push, and the bulb spends it on brighter light.

## 5. See where it belongs

Voltage sits in Electricity's Voltage and Resistance group — the push half of the pair that also includes resistance, the 'hold back'. Current (earlier) is the flow; this model shows what powers it. Batteries and mains are both sources of volt rated pressure.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict the bulb brightness change after adding one more 1.5 V cell, then test on the model.

## 6. Compare it

Compare pressure to flow: current is the flow of charge (amps), voltage is the push (volts). On this loop, doubling the push roughly doubles the bulb's power rate at the same resistance. The pair works like a hand: the push forces the paper — one supplies, the other delivers.

## 7. Use it in a task

Design a brighter flashlight: *Your torch uses two 1.5 V cells in a battery bank. To double the brightness, how many volts do you want the bank to push? Stack cells on the model and confirm with the bulb.*
**Practice item:** Stack two and four cells, and record volts and relative bulb glow for each; describe the push-flow link in one sentence.
