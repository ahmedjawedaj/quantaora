# Battery

A battery is a compact store of chemical energy that becomes electrical push. This cutaway model shows the cell's layers, terminals, and what happens as it drains into a bulb.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a cutaway cell showing stacked layers inside, a cap marked + on top and a base marked −, plus a small bulb wired to it. Tap `hs-case` to label the outer case, `hs-layers` to label the chemical layers, and `hs-pos` to label the + terminal.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the cutaway to see the layers: `hs-layers` stack from base to cap. Close the switch (`hs-circuit`) and the bulb lights as the cell pushes current out. Now turn `hs-usage` up to drain it: the layers visibly shrink and the glow fades — the chemistry getting spent.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-case` to label the metal jacket, `hs-layers` to label the reacting chemicals, and `hs-neg` and `hs-pos` to label the two terminals whose difference is the voltage.

## 4. Explain what it does

Inside, chemicals react to push electrons onto the − terminal and pull them from the +, creating a potential difference — the voltage. Connect a loop and electrons travel to rebalance — current — while the chemistry keeps refilling the push. Voltage tells how hard it pushes; capacity (mAh) tells how long that push lasts before the chemical layers run out.

## 5. See where it belongs

The battery is the source in Electricity's Components unit: the push that drives every other component. It pairs with the bulb (the load), the switch (the gate), and the wire (the road). Its volts and capacity are the two numbers every appliance asks for.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict what will happen to the bulb when you set the usage knob high — then try it.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the battery with the bulb it powers: the battery supplies chemical energy as push; the bulb eats that push as light and heat. One creates the voltage, the other spends the current. The cutaway makes their partnership visible: finish one layer, and the glow pays the price.

## 7. Use it in a task

Match a battery to a toy: *The toy needs 3 V; the shelf holds 1.5 V cells. How many cells must you stack and why? Explain using the cutaway, then count the layers on the model.*
**Practice item:** Run the usage knob at 0%, 50%, 100%; record layer height and bulb brightness at each, and name the two battery ratings involved.
