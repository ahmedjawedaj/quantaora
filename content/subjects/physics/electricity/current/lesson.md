# Electric Current

Electric current is charge flowing along a wire — a stream of electrons, clicking through a loop. In this model a transparent wire carries electrons from a battery to a bulb, and an ammeter shows the flow in amps.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a transparent wire loop with a battery at one end, a small bulb at the other, dots streaming between, and an ammeter by the bend. Tap `hs-wire` to name the wire, `hs-battery` to name the battery, and `hs-ammeter` to name the amp gauge.

## 2. Touch it

Tap `hs-flow-slider` to speed the flow and watch two indicators agree at once: the dots race and the ammeter climbs. Now move `hs-break` to open a gap in the line — every dot stops. No loop, no flow, no amps.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-battery` to name the battery that drives the electrons, `hs-wire` to name the path, `hs-bulb` to name where current turns into light, and `hs-ammeter` to name the instrument reading the flow in amps.

## 4. Explain what it does

Current is the rate at which charge flows: one ampere means one coulomb of charge passing a point each second. The dots are the electrons marching together; their speed is the flow rate. A single gap anywhere stops the whole loop — because current is the same at every point in a series loop, it flows everywhere together or nowhere at all.

## 5. See where it belongs

Current lives in the Electricity branch, Charge and Current unit, next to charge. Charge is the still state; current is the same stuff on the move. The ammeter reading leads straight into voltage and resistance, which decide how hard the battery pushes and how hard the wire holds the flow back.

**Prediction prompt:** Turn the flow slider halfway up, then open `hs-break`; predict the ammeter reading before it resets to zero.

## 6. Compare it

Compare fast flow and slow flow on the same loop: the dot speed and the ammeter both change together because both are the same current. Opening the loop is not "less current" — it is zero. A loop does not half-flow; it is all-on or off, and that all-or-nothing fact is what makes switches work.

## 7. Use it in a task

A phone charger label says 2 A. *Use the model to answer: how much charge passes the ammeter per second at that rate? Then slow the slider and state which indicator proves the flow dropped.*
**Practice item:** Run the flow at three different speeds, record the ammeter each time, and write each reading in coulombs per second.

**Checkpoint:** What is electric current, what unit measures it, and what single break stops the flow everywhere in the loop?
