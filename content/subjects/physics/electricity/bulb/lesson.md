# Light Bulb

The light bulb turns electric current into light by pushing it through a thin filament that glows white-hot. This clear-glass model shows the inside of a bulb wired into a loop with a switch.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a clear glass bulb, the coiled filament inside, support wires, a little switch, and a battery. Tap `hs-filament` to label the glowing coil, `hs-globe` to label the glass, and `hs-base` to label the screw base that makes contact.

## 2. Touch it

Close the switch `hs-switch` and watch the filament snap from grey to white as current arrives; open it and the glow dies instantly. Now trace the loop: the current leaves the battery, runs up a wire to the bulb, and returns through the base. Tap `hs-current-path` to highlight the whole round trip.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-filament` to label the resistance that glows, `hs-support` to label the wires holding it, `hs-base` to label the contact ring, and `hs-globe` to label the glass that lets the light out.

## 4. Explain what it does

The filament is deliberately thin and coiled — a big resistance in a small place. Current squeezes through, friction-like resistance turns some of its energy into heat, and heat makes the metal glow. More current (from more voltage) → hotter filament → brighter light. No loop, no current, no glow: the switch is simply the door that closes the loop.

## 5. See where it belongs

The bulb is the star component of Electricity's Components unit: the place where current becomes useful light. It needs a battery (the push), a wire (the road), and a switch (the gate) to do its one job. Everything a circuit is for, the bulb shows.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict whether opening the switch affects the filament's heat — then close and open it on the model to check.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the bulb with the switch that serves it: the switch has no resistance and does no work — it just opens or closes the road. The bulb is where the energy lands. Together they are the perfect pair: the gate that decides, and the window that shows whether current came.

## 7. Use it in a task

Design a flashlight that can be left on: *Explain, using the model, which single part you would remove to stop the glow without damaging the battery — the bulb or the switch — and what the current does in each case.*
**Practice item:** Open and close the switch three times; note what happens to filament color, ammeter, and the room glow each time.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** Why does the filament glow, and what single condition must the loop meet for the bulb to light at all?
