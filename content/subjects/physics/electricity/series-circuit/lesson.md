# Series Circuit

*Physics > Electricity > Circuits > Building Circuits*

In a series circuit every component shares one single loop — the same current through each, the volts split across them, and one break stops them all. This model strings three bulbs on that single path.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a chain of three bulbs on one curving loop, a battery at one end, and meters you can hover over each bulb. Tap `hs-loop` to label the single path, `hs-bulb-a` to label the first bulb, and `hs-meter` to label the amp meter at any point.

## 2. Touch it

Follow the loop around: click each bulb and read the meter — identical amps at every stop, because there's only one road. Now tap `hs-remove-b` to unscrew the middle bulb; the other two go dark instantly. Screw it back (`hs-put-back`) and the whole chain relights together.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-loop` to label the one-shared-path, `hs-bulb-a` / `hs-bulb-b` / `hs-bulb-c` to label the chain, and `hs-battery` to label the source that all three share.

## 4. Explain what it does

One loop, one current: every bulb carries exactly the same electrons and the same amps. The battery's total voltage splits across the chain — three equal bulbs each feel only a slice — so each glows a bit weaker than alone. Unscrew one bulb and you've broken the only road: nothing flows, everything goes dark. That's the series triple rule: same current, shared volts, all-or-nothing survival.

## 5. See where it belongs

Series is the first arrangement in Electricity's Circuits unit — the default answer before parallel shows its smarter branching. The battery (push), current (flow), and bulb (load) are all here in their faces; series just forces them to share one pipe.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you remove the middle bulb, predict the ammeter readouts at the other two — then pull it and check.

## 6. Compare it

Compare series with its cousin parallel: series gives one loop, equal current, and one-breaks-all; parallel gives each bulb its own loop, shared volts but independent survival. The model's trio is the perfect demo — screw one down and both arrangements diverge at the fuse.

## 7. Use it in a task

Decide the tangle: *You have three identical bulbs and one battery. Series wiring makes them all shine dimmer than parallel. Explain with the model why adding the second bulb dimmed the first, using current-or-volt the language.*
**Practice item:** Remove and replace each bulb in turn; record which family group darkens with each and state the series rule you proved.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What are the two defining rules of a series circuit, and why does one removed bulb take the whole loop dark?
