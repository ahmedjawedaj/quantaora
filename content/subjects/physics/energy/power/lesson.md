# Power

*Physics > Energy > Work and Power > Work and Power*

Power is the speed of work — the rate at which energy is transferred. This model is a lift belt: you raise a box the same height on slow or fast gears while the watt meter tells you the rate.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a vertical lift belt carrying a box, a speed knob, a watt meter, and a work total. Tap `hs-box` to label the lifted load, `hs-belt` to label the lift, and `hs-watt` to label the watt gauge.

## 2. Touch it

Turn the speed knob to SLOW and lift the box — note the low watt reading. Now flip to FAST and lift the same box the same height: the work total matches, but the watts spike far higher. Same joules, different tempo.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-speed-knob` to label the time knob, `hs-watt` to label watts (joules per second), and `hs-work-total` to label the work counter.

## 4. Explain what it does

Power = work ÷ time, and its unit is the watt (one joule per second). The model shows two truths at once: same work finished faster → higher power; and faster isn't doing more work, just doing it sooner.

## 5. See where it belongs

Power belongs to Energy's Work and Power unit, as the clock—twin of work. Work says how much energy moved; power says how fast. Every appliance sticker, engine nameplate, and cellphone charger is power's home — 'watts'.

**Prediction prompt:** Time the slow lift and the fast lift. Which of the two does more work — or is it the same total?

## 6. Compare it

Compare power with the same joules from work: work is the total energy transferred; power is work ÷ time. The belt demonstrates: total counter stays equal between slow and fast runs; the watt meter is the runner that changes. It's about tempo, not a total.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to size an appliance: *Wanting a kettle and a toaster: if both move the same energy in 2 min but the toaster does it in 30 s, which is more powerful? Lift the box fast and slow; read the watts; justify.*

**Practice item:** Run the belt slow and fast, note the work total (should match!) and the watt reading for each; describe the divide by time.
