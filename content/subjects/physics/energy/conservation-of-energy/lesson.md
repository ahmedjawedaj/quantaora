# Conservation of Energy

*Physics > Energy > Energy Conservation > Energy Conservation and Efficiency*

Energy is never created or destroyed — it just changes form. This model is a roller-coaster loop with a live readout; watch the stored-height bar drain into the motion bar and back again, again and again.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a coaster with a high first hill and a loop, a red cart, and a split energy readout. Tap `hs-coaster` to label the track, `hs-cart` to label the rider, and `hs-bar` to label the energy readout.

## 2. Touch it

Launch the cart and watch the bar as it crests the hill — tall as stored energy. Watch it plunge into the loop: stored energy drains as the velocity sliver climbs. Zoom in as it brakes at the end: the heat line still claims its share, and the total never blinks.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-stored` to label stored-height energy, `hs-motion` to label motion energy, `hs-heat` to label the heat share, and `hs-total` to label the total that refuses to change.

## 4. Explain what it does

Conservation of energy says the total stays the same in a closed system — energy only changes form. Up the hill, energy is stored (potential); down it, stored becomes motion; scraped friction warms the rails but never adds to the total, because heat is just another account in the same book. The bar always sums to the starting drop.

## 5. See where it belongs

Conservation belongs to Energy's Energy Conservation unit, as the master law ruling every other branch. Kinetic, potential, heat — all are entries on its ledger, and efficiency (the other member of this unit) is what we compute when we ask how much of that total tips into useful work.

**Prediction prompt:** Watch the bar at the crest of the hill, then predict its stored share at the bottom of the loop.

## 6. Compare it

Compare conserved total with efficiency: conservation says the whole sum can't change; efficiency asks what fraction of it actually did the useful job. The coaster's total is constant, but its heat carve-out means only part of that total could ever turn the wheels — that gap is what efficiency counts.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to explain a braking secret: *Why does the cart's surface warm up when it brakes at the end? Trace where the missing-motion energy went on the bar, then state the law using the heat line.*
**Practice item:** Note the total before, during, and after one full loop; state which two forms swapped and whether the total moved at all.
