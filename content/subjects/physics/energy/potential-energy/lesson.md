# Potential Energy

*Physics > Energy > Energy Forms > Kinetic and Potential Energy*

Potential energy is stored energy — a block on a shelf holds it because gravity could pull it down. In this model you lift the block, read the stored-energy meter, and release it to watch stored energy become motion.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a wooden block resting on a liftable shelf above a floor. Tap `hs-block` to label the stored-energy holder, `hs-shelf` to label the platform whose height matters, and `hs-meter` to label the joule meter at the side.

## 2. Touch it

Drag the shelf up one step and watch the meter climb with it — stored energy rising from height. Now push it down; the meter drops. Release the block (tap `hs-release-button`) and it falls: the meter runs down as the block speeds up, potential energy cashing out into kinetic.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-height-guide` to label the height measured from the floor, `hs-block` to label the object with the stored energy, and `hs-meter` to label the PE = mgh readout.

## 4. Explain what it does

Potential energy is energy stored because of position: PE = mass × g × height, with g ≈ 10 N/kg on Earth. Bigger mass or higher shelf means more stored joules. It doesn't do anything while it sits — it waits, then converts to kinetic energy (or heat, if the block thuds) the instant it's released.

## 5. See where it belongs

Potential energy belongs to Energy, in the Energy Forms unit, alongside kinetic energy — the two trading back and forth. The shelf block is a perfect example of stored energy from height, ready to hand over to motion.

**Prediction prompt:** Lift the shelf to a new height, then release — predict the meter's readout as the block passes the halfway mark.

## 6. Compare it

Compare potential with kinetic energy. The block at the top = potential (stored); the block falling = kinetic (moving). The meter is the referee — at the top all energy is potential, at the bottom all is kinetic, and at any height the total is the same, just split differently.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to place a water dam: *Raise the shelf to 3 m with a 2 kg block, then to 6 m. Which stores more energy, and about how many times more? Set both heights and read the meter to check the two-fold jump.*

**Practice item:** Set the shelf to two different heights and write the meter readings, then say which change — mass or height — would double the stored energy fastest.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What is potential energy, what is its height formula, and what happens to it the moment the block is released?
