# Work

*Physics > Energy > Work and Power > Work and Power*

In physics, work happens when a force moves something: work = force × distance. In this model you push a cart along a track and the work counter climbs — with the cart's kinetic energy matching every joule you put in.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a cart on a straight track with a push handle, a work counter, and an energy arrow. Tap `hs-cart` to label the cart, `hs-track` to label the run, and `hs-counter` to label the joule counter.

## 2. Touch it

Press `hs-push-handle` to shove the cart one meter and watch the counter tick up a full joule: work done. Now drag the cart further with a bigger push — the counter reaches higher faster. Stop pushing and watch it freeze: no motion, no work.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-counter` to label the work readout, `hs-push-handle` to label the force control, and `hs-distance-mark` to label the meter marks on the track.

## 4. Explain what it does

Work is energy handed to an object by a force that actually moves it: work = force × distance, in joules. Every meter the cart slides adds force × meter of work, and the cart's kinetic energy rises by the same amount. Hold the push handle and don't move — the counter stays zero. Nothing moved, nothing transferred, zero work.

## 5. See where it belongs

Work belongs to Energy, in the Work and Power unit. It's the delivery process between kinetic energy and potential energy: push a cart up a hill and your work becomes potential, push it level and work becomes motion. Work is the transaction; energy is the currency.

**Prediction prompt:** Push the cart under a small force, then under a big force; predict which gets the higher counter and why.

## 6. Compare it

Compare work with the power that spends it. Work = force × distance counts the total energy transferred. Power = work ÷ time tells if the same joule-stack for one second or ten. Work is the meal; power is how fast you eat — same plate, different watch.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to design a delivery: *Push the cart 2 m with force 5, then 4 m with the same strength; which does more work? Set both and let the counter show it.*

**Practice item:** Write the work for pushing the cart two distinct (force, distance) pairs found on the track, then state the formula that linked them.
