# Efficiency

*Physics > Energy > Energy Conservation > Energy Conservation and Efficiency*

Efficiency measures how much of the energy you put into a machine actually does the useful job. This model is a little engine with a fuel slider and two output bars — useful work and waste heat.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a compact engine block, a fuel input slider, an output pair of bars, and a percent badge. Tap `hs-engine` to label the machine, `hs-fuel` to label the input, and `hs-useful` to label the useful-output bar.

## 2. Touch it

Slide the fuel input up and watch both bars climb — but not equally: the green useful bar rises while the grey heat bar always steals a share. Now slide the engine's friction knob down and watch the same fuel deliver a bigger green slice. Less waste, more got work.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-useful` to label the useful output, `hs-waste` to label the heat waste, and `hs-percent` to label the big efficiency percent at the top.

## 4. Explain what it does

Efficiency (%) = useful output ÷ total input, times 100. Input energy splits two ways: part turns into useful work, the rest escapes as heat, sound, or drag. Because some always leaks, the percent is always below 100. Slide the fuel and you're sliding both the numerator (useful) and denominator, and the heat bar proves nothing is free.

## 5. See where it belongs

Efficiency lives in Energy's Energy Conservation unit, hand-in-hand with conservation of energy. Conservation says the energy total never changes — the 'lost' joules are still there as heat. Efficiency is what we compute next: how much of that conserved total was actually useful work. Two sides of the same account.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict the percent before you slide fuel to maximum; now measure it — did the percentage itself change, and why?

## 6. Compare it

Compare efficiency with conservation itself: conservation counts everything (the total never changes), efficiency counts useful works only (the percent). The grey bar is the shadow conservation throws — energy exists but isn't useful. Boost efficiency, and the useful share grows; the total, meanwhile, is frozen by the law.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to shop a bulb: *An LED makes 90 J of light from 100 J of electricity; an old bulb makes 5 J from the same 100 J. Which fraction is efficiency, and what percents do the two get? Set the input to 100 on the engine and read the useful share to check your numbers.*

**Practice item:** Run the engine at two input values and record input, useful, heat, and percent each time; state the formula that joined them.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What does efficiency measure, what is its formula, and why can it never reach 100%?
