# Kinetic Energy

*Physics > Energy > Energy Forms > Kinetic and Potential Energy*

Kinetic energy is the energy of motion. In this model a cart rolls down a ramp onto a flat track; you control its mass and speed, and watch an energy bar fill — with speed counting twice, because the formula squares it.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a ramp with a cart at the top, a flat track running away from it, and a tall energy bar standing at the track's side. Tap `hs-cart` to label the moving object, `hs-energy-bar` to label the kinetic-energy bar, and `hs-track` to label the run the cart glides on.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom around the track. Push the speed slider up a notch — each step the energy bar surprisingly SOARS because speed is squared. Now nudge the mass slider: the bar grows too, but only step-for-step. Release the cart at the ramp top and watch the bar light up as it rolls — kinetic energy building from the stored height.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-speed-slider` to label the speed control, `hs-mass-slider` to label the mass control, and `hs-energy-bar` to label the joule energy meter.

## 4. Explain what it does

Kinetic energy is the energy something has because it moves: KE = ½ × mass × speed². The squared speed is the star — if you double the speed, the energy jumps by 2² = 4, so the bar quadruples. Doubling the mass only doubles it. Rolling down the ramp, the cart's kinetic energy comes from the potential energy it was given up high.

## 5. See where it belongs

Kinetic energy belongs to Energy, inside the Energy Forms unit, wearing the position of temporary motion energy. It's the partner of potential energy — the two trade roles whenever a cart rolls, a ball flies, or a pendulum swings.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you double the speed slider, predict the energy bar — twice as tall or four times?

## 6. Compare it

Compare kinetic energy with potential energy. Kinetic = energy of motion (the rolling cart). Potential = stored energy of height (the cart at the top). The ramp is the bridge — going down converts potential into kinetic; going up converts kinetic back. Watch the bar to see one drain as the other fills.

## 7. Use it in a task

Use the model to win a safety argument: *Two identical cars: car A at 50 km/h, car B at 100 km/h. How many times bigger is the kinetic energy of car B? Set the speed slider and read the four-fold jump yourself.*

**Practice item:** Double the speed slider and note exactly how the energy bar grows; then double the mass and compare the shown change. Write both in one sentence.

## 8. Review it later

**Checkpoint:** What is kinetic energy, what is its formula, and why does doubling speed quadruple it rather than double it?
