# DNA

*Biology > Genetics > Molecular Genetics > Nucleic Acids & Genetic Material > DNA*

DNA is the instruction manual packed inside almost every cell in your body. Picture a super-long, twisted ladder — that twist is called a double helix. The two side rails are the sugar-phosphate backbone, and each rung is two chemical letters holding hands: A always pairs with T, and C always pairs with G. Read in order, stretches of these letters spell out genes — instructions for building the proteins and traits that make you, you.

## 1. See it

Look at the full 3D model of DNA slowly auto-rotating. Notice it looks like a twisted ladder or a spiral staircase, not a flat strand — the twist is a real 3D feature of the molecule, not just an artistic choice in diagrams.

## 2. Touch it

Zoom and rotate the model yourself. Drag to spin it and see how the two side rails wind around each other while the rungs stay evenly spaced along the axis.

**Predict:** One strand of a short DNA segment reads **A - T - G - C - C - T**. Before the app reveals the answer, write down what you predict the matching (complementary) strand reads.

## 3. Name it

Tap the hotspots to label the four parts:

- `hotspot-double-helix` — the double helix (overall twisted shape)
- `hotspot-backbone` — the sugar-phosphate backbone (the two outer rails)
- `hotspot-base-pairs` — the nucleotide base pairs (the rungs)
- `hotspot-genes` — genes (highlighted stretches along the strand)

## 4. Explain it

See how each rung is actually two bases holding hands in the middle — A always holds hands with T, and C always holds hands with G. That's why the two strands are called "complementary": one side tells you exactly what the other side must be. Running up each side of the ladder, the sugar-phosphate backbone is the strong structural rail that holds every rung in place; it doesn't carry the code itself, it just holds the code-carrying rungs steady.

**Reveal:** The complementary strand for A-T-G-C-C-T is **T-A-C-G-G-A** — each base swapped for its fixed partner (A↔T, C↔G), in the same left-to-right order.

## 5. Belongs to

DNA sits in **Biology > Genetics > Molecular Genetics > Nucleic Acids & Genetic Material**. It's coiled up inside the nucleus — the same organelle covered in the Cell Biology branch — and packaged there into chromosomes (humans carry 23 pairs).

## 6. Compare it

Compare DNA to RNA, its single-stranded cousin. Where DNA is a double helix built from deoxyribose sugar and uses thymine, RNA is single-stranded, built from ribose sugar, and swaps thymine for uracil. DNA stays put in the nucleus; RNA carries a gene's instructions out to where proteins get built.
