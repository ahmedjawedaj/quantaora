# Small Intestine

*Biology > Human Anatomy > Digestive system > Small Intestine*

The small intestine is a long, coiled tube (about 6 meters in an adult) where most digestion finishes and nearly all nutrients are absorbed. Its inner surface is not smooth - it is lined with millions of tiny finger-like projections called villi. Each villus is covered with even tinier microvilli and contains blood vessels and a lymph vessel. This gives the small intestine an enormous surface area, like a huge rug folded into a small space, so nutrients can be absorbed quickly. Food is pushed through by gentle muscle waves while the intestine finishes breaking down what the stomach started.
