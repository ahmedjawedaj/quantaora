# Blood Vessels

*Biology > Human Anatomy > Cardiovascular system > Blood Vessels*

Blood vessels are the tubes that carry blood around the body. There are three main types. Arteries carry blood away from the heart; they have thick, stretchy walls because the heart's pumping makes blood flow through them at high pressure. Veins carry blood back toward the heart; they have thinner walls and small one-way valves to stop blood flowing backward. Capillaries are the tiniest vessels, so thin that materials can pass through their walls - this is where oxygen, nutrients, and waste are exchanged with the cells. So the network works like this: arteries are the highways out, capillaries are the driveways where the delivery happens, and veins are the highways back.
