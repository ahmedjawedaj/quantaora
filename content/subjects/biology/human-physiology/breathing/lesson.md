# Breathing

*Biology > Human Physiology > Circulatory & Respiratory Processes > Respiratory system > Breathing*

Breathing is how your body pulls fresh air in and pushes used air back out, over and over, without you ever having to think about it. Every time you inhale, you're collecting oxygen — a gas your cells need to release energy from food — and every time you exhale, you're getting rid of carbon dioxide, a waste gas your cells don't need. Watch one full cycle: the diaphragm contracts, the lungs expand, air rushes in, oxygen and carbon dioxide swap places at the alveoli, and then the diaphragm relaxes to push air back out.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D chest cutaway. Spot the ribcage, the two lungs (notice the right lung has three lobes and the left has two, making room for the heart), and the diaphragm stretched like a dome underneath them.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model and tap the `hs-diaphragm` hotspot to watch the diaphragm contract and flatten downward, then relax back up, in a repeating loop.

**Predict:** Before watching the full breathing-cycle animation, think about this — when the diaphragm pulls down and flattens, does the space inside your chest get bigger or smaller? What do you think that does to the air pressure inside your lungs compared to the air outside your body?

## 3. Name it

Trace the path air takes as it enters, in order: nose/mouth → trachea → bronchi → alveoli. Then name the five stages of one breathing cycle in the order they actually happen: **Diaphragm contraction → Lung expansion → Inhale → Gas exchange at alveoli → Exhale**.

## 4. Explain it

Watch the full breathing-cycle animation and narrate what's happening at each stage:

- The diaphragm contracts (`hs-diaphragm`) and flattens downward, enlarging the chest cavity.
- The elastic lungs expand (`hs-lung-expansion`) to fill that extra space.
- That expansion drops the air pressure inside the lungs below the pressure outside your body, so air flows in — this is the inhale (`hs-inhale`).
- At the alveoli, oxygen and carbon dioxide swap places (`hs-gas-exchange`): oxygen moves from the air into your blood, and carbon dioxide moves from your blood into the air.
- The diaphragm relaxes and domes back up, the chest cavity shrinks, and the lungs recoil — pushing the carbon-dioxide-rich air back out. This is the exhale (`hs-exhale`).

**Reveal:** So yes — the chest cavity gets *bigger* when the diaphragm contracts, and that drop in pressure inside your lungs is exactly what pulls air in from outside during the inhale. No "sucking" muscle in the lungs themselves is needed; the lungs are passive, elastic tissue that simply follows the space the diaphragm makes for them.

## 6. Compare it

Compare breathing to blood circulation (the cardiac cycle). Both are rhythmic, involuntary, muscle-driven cycles that never stop and work as a team: breathing loads oxygen into the blood at the lungs, while the heartbeat-driven cardiac cycle carries that oxygen to every cell in the body and brings carbon dioxide back to be exhaled on the next cycle.
