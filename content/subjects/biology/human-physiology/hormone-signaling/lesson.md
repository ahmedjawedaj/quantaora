# Hormone Signaling

*Biology > Human Physiology > Body Systems Processes > Hormone Signaling (process) > Hormone Signaling*

Think of your body as a huge building with no phones — just a mail system. Hormone signaling is that mail system. A gland writes a "message" — a hormone — and drops it into your bloodstream, which acts like a delivery truck that drives past every cell in your body. Most cells ignore the message completely. But some cells have a special mailbox called a receptor that's shaped to fit that exact hormone, and when the hormone finds one, it "unlocks" the mailbox and the cell changes what it's doing.

## 1. See it

Look at the model as a single left-to-right story, not a full body. You'll see one stylized gland on the left, a curved bloodstream vessel carrying small traveling hormone particles, a single target cell on the right with a receptor facing the vessel, and a thin dashed feedback arc looping from the target cell back over the top of the scene to the gland. This is a generic, schematic pathway — not a specific organ like the pancreas or thyroid — built to show the general pattern every hormone follows: release, travel, bind, respond, feedback.

## 2. Touch it

Tap the `hs-gland-release` hotspot on the gland to open its structure card, then watch the small ring at the gland's base pulse — that pulse is the moment of hormone release. Press play on the sequence and follow a hormone particle as it travels from the gland along the bloodstream toward the target cell.

## 3. Name it

Label the five stages in the order they animate: gland releases hormone, hormone travels in bloodstream, target cell receptor binds hormone, cell response triggered, feedback regulation. Tap each of the five hotspots in turn — `hs-gland-release`, `hs-bloodstream`, `hs-receptor-bind`, `hs-cell-response`, `hs-feedback` — to confirm each label against the part of the scene it names.

## 4. Explain it

Open the model's cross-section view on the target cell so you can see inside it.

- Tap `hs-receptor-bind`, at the tip of the small receptor facing the vessel. Name it: this is the receptor. Explain it: only a hormone with the matching shape can bind here — like a key fitting one specific lock. That's why a hormone can flow past most cells in the body with no effect at all.
- Tap `hs-cell-response`, the internal marker near the cell's center, now visible through the cutaway. Name it: this is the cell's response site. Explain it: binding at the receptor sets off a chain of changes inside the cell — the cell starts doing something differently, like turning a gene on or speeding up a process.
- Tap `hs-feedback`, along the thin dashed arc looping back to the gland. Name it: this is the feedback loop. Explain it: once the response happens, a signal travels back along this arc to the gland, and you can watch the gland's release-ring animation visibly dampen for one cycle — the body dialing hormone release back down once the message has done its job.

## 6. Compare it

Compare hormone signaling to **nerve signaling**. Nerve signals are fast electrical impulses that travel along a fixed cell-to-cell wire and trigger quick, short-lived responses, like yanking a hand off a hot stove. Hormone signals are chemical messengers released into the bloodstream, so they take longer to arrive and reach the whole body rather than one wired path — but their effects, like growth or changes in metabolism, often last much longer. The two systems are complementary, not competing, ways the body coordinates itself.
