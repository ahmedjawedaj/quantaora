# Digestion

*Biology > Human Physiology > Body Systems Processes > Digestive System (process) > Digestion*

Digestion is your body's way of turning food into fuel. Think of it like a factory assembly line running from your mouth to the other end of your body: at each station, a different tool breaks the food down a little more, until what's left is small enough for your blood to carry it to every cell you have. The whole system is basically one long, winding tube — about 9 meters of it — with each section built to do one job really well.

## 1. See it

Look at the full digestive tract model as one long, coiled tube running from mouth to the end of the body, and notice it's really just one continuous pathway, not several separate organs floating apart.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and tap each of the five stations in order — **mouth**, **stomach**, **small intestine**, **large intestine**, **waste elimination** — and watch the highlighted animation of food moving through that section. Tap the `hs-mouth` hotspot first, then follow the tract downward.

## 3. Name it

Label each of the five parts on the model and match each one to what it mainly does:

- **Mouth** — mechanical and chemical breakdown
- **Stomach** — acid breakdown
- **Small intestine** — nutrient absorption
- **Large intestine** — water absorption
- **Waste elimination (rectum)** — storage and release

## 4. Explain it

For each part, explain in your own words what changes about the food as it passes through:

- Tap the **stomach** hotspot (`hs-stomach`) and open the cross-section view. Name it: this is the stomach. Explain it: hydrochloric acid and the enzyme pepsin turn chewed food into a semi-liquid mixture called chyme — you can see the internal folds (rugae) that let the stomach stretch and churn.
- Tap the **small intestine** hotspot (`hs-small-intestine`), still in cross-section. Name it: this is the small intestine. Explain it: bile, pancreatic enzymes, and villi-lined walls finish breaking food down and pull nutrients directly into the bloodstream — solid to chewed pulp, pulp to acidic mush, mush to absorbed nutrients plus leftover material.
- Tap the **large intestine** hotspot (`hs-large-intestine`). Name it: this is the large intestine. Explain it: it reclaims water and salts from what's left, turning leftover material from liquid to more solid waste.

## 5. See where it belongs

Digestion belongs to **Biology > Human Physiology > Body Systems Processes > Digestive System (process)**. The organs this model shows — mouth, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, and rectum — are the same organs mapped as anatomical structures in the **Human Anatomy** branch's Digestive System unit; this component is the *process* view of that same system. Zooming out further, digestion connects to two other physiology processes in this branch: **Blood circulation** carries the nutrients the small intestine absorbs out to the rest of the body, and **filtration by kidneys** handles the body's other, separate elimination pathway (liquid waste, rather than the solid waste digestion produces).

## 6. Compare it

Compare digestion to the **respiratory system**. Both are input-processing systems that take something from outside the body (food vs. air) and extract what cells need (nutrients vs. oxygen) while expelling what's left over (waste vs. carbon dioxide) — comparing them highlights the shared pattern of intake, breakdown/exchange, absorption, and elimination.
