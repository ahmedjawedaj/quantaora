# Temperature Regulation

*Biology > Human Physiology > Body Systems Processes > Temperature Regulation (process)*

Your body is like a house with a smart thermostat. No matter if it's freezing outside or blazing hot, a house with a good thermostat keeps its inside temperature steady. Your body does the same thing — it works hard to keep your core temperature close to 37°C (98.6°F), even when the world around you changes a lot.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a body with two feature sites highlighted — a cross-section of the head exposing a small structure deep in the brain, and a skin cross-section on the chest that can be switched between two states. Notice how these sites are connected by nerves and blood vessels even though they're in very different locations.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom into the head first. Find the **hypothalamus** — it's tiny, about the size of an almond, but it controls a huge response across the whole body. Then move to the chest skin cross-section and toggle between its two states to see the same patch of skin respond differently depending on whether the body is too hot or too cold.

## 3. Name it

Tap the `hs-hypothalamus` hotspot to label the hypothalamus. Toggle the skin cross-section to its cooling state and tap `hs-cooling-response` to label the widened blood vessel and sweat droplet. Toggle to its warming state and tap `hs-warming-response` to label the narrowed blood vessel and the raised hair follicle (goosebump) next to the contracted muscle.

## 4. Explain it

Walk through what happens step by step when you get too hot: the hypothalamus detects rising temperature → it signals sweat glands to release sweat → it signals skin blood vessels to widen (vasodilation) → heat leaves the body through evaporation and radiation → temperature drops back to normal.

## 5. Predict it

Before toggling the model to its warming state, ask yourself: *If you jump into a cold swimming pool, what do you think your blood vessels near your skin will do — widen or narrow? Why do you think that helps your body?*

## 6. Belongs to

Temperature regulation belongs to the bigger system of **homeostasis** — the body's constant effort to keep internal conditions stable. Compare it to another homeostatic process you may already know, like how the body regulates blood sugar or water balance: different signals and different organs, but the same basic pattern of sensing a change and triggering a response that cancels it out.

A quick note on a related twist: a **fever** works by turning this same thermostat up on purpose. The immune system resets the hypothalamus's set point higher, so even though the body's actual temperature hasn't dropped, it now reads itself as "too cold" compared to the new setting — which is why someone with a fever can feel chilly and shiver even while their temperature is above normal.

## 7. Compare it

Compare humans to **ectotherms** like reptiles. Humans are endotherms — we generate our own internal heat through metabolism and actively regulate it with responses like sweating and shivering. Ectotherms instead rely on external heat sources and behaviors, such as basking in the sun or seeking shade, to control body temperature.
