# Filtration by Kidneys

*Biology > Human Physiology > Filtration by Kidneys*

Think of your kidneys as two bean-shaped recycling centers, one on each side of your lower back, each about the size of your fist. Every minute, blood flows into them through a big blood vessel. Inside each kidney are about a million tiny filtering units called nephrons — so small you'd need a microscope to see one, but together they do an enormous job. Each nephron works like a super-fine coffee filter: blood pressure pushes fluid out of tiny blood vessels into a cup-shaped filter, which catches water, salts, and waste products like urea while keeping blood cells and important proteins inside the blood. Then, as that filtered fluid travels through a long, winding tube, the nephron pulls back almost all the water, sugar, and salts your body still needs — what's left becomes urine.

## 3. Name it

Tap the `hs-blood-enters-kidney` hotspot at the hilum, where the renal artery brings blood in. Then find the enlarged nephron inset just outside the kidney's silhouette, connected by a leader line — real nephrons are microscopic, so this inset shows one blown up to a visible size. Label the five key parts in order: **blood enters kidney**, **nephron filters waste**, **reabsorption of needed substances**, **urine formation**, **waste exits via ureter**.

## 4. Explain what it does

Walk through what happens to a drop of blood step by step.

- Tap **Nephron Filters Waste** (`hs-nephron-filters-waste`) on the glomerulus, the tangled ball of capillaries cupped inside Bowman's capsule. Explain it: blood pressure forces water, salts, and small waste molecules out of the blood here, while blood cells and large proteins stay behind.
- Tap **Reabsorption of Needed Substances** (`hs-reabsorption`) along the tubule. Explain it: as the filtered fluid travels down the tubule, useful water, glucose, amino acids, and salts are pulled back into the surrounding blood vessels — this is what keeps the body from losing everything it just filtered out.
- Tap **Urine Formation** (`hs-urine-formation`) at the collecting duct and renal pelvis, where the pyramid tips funnel inward. Explain it: the fluid that's left — mostly water plus wastes like urea — becomes concentrated urine here.
- Tap **Waste Exits via Ureter** (`hs-waste-exits-ureter`) on the tube leaving the hilum. Explain it: urine drains from the renal pelvis through the ureter, carrying dissolved waste out of the kidney toward the bladder.

## 5. See where it belongs

Zoom out to see how the kidney fits into the whole urinary system — two kidneys, two ureters, one bladder, one urethra — and where it connects to the circulatory system for blood in and out. The kidney's structure (cortex, medulla, pyramids) is covered as an organ in the Human Anatomy branch's Urinary system unit; this component is about the filtering *process* happening inside that organ.

## 6. Compare it

Place the kidney model next to the liver model. Both organs clean the blood, but they do it differently: the kidney physically filters blood under pressure and reabsorbs what it needs, while the liver chemically breaks down toxins and drugs and sends the waste out through bile and digestion. Comparing them highlights "filtering" versus "chemically processing" as two different cleanup strategies the body uses side by side.
