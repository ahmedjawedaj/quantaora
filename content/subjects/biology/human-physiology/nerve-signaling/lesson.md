# Nerve Signaling

*Biology > Human Physiology > Nervous System Processes > Nerve Signaling Pathway > Nerve Signaling*

A nerve signal is your body's version of a text message — but instead of Wi-Fi, it travels as a tiny electrical pulse racing down a thread-like cell called a neuron. When something touches your skin, hits your eye, or your brain decides to move a finger, a neuron flips into "on" mode for a split second, and that "on" state zips down its long tail (the axon) like a flame racing along a fuse. When the signal reaches the very end of the neuron, it can't jump the tiny gap to the next cell electrically — so it releases a burst of chemical messengers (neurotransmitters) that drift across the gap and dock onto the next neuron, restarting the electrical pulse all over again. Chain enough of these hand-offs together — skin to spinal cord to brain to muscle — and you get everything from flinching off a hot stove to reading this sentence.

## 4. Explain it

Walk through the 5-step chain in order, using what each hotspot's structure card told you:

- **Stimulus received** (`hs-1`): the presynaptic neuron's dendrites detect an incoming stimulus and depolarize.
- **Electrical impulse travels axon** (`hs-2`): the impulse races down the myelin-insulated axon, jumping between nodes of Ranvier — saltatory conduction.
- **Neurotransmitter release at synapse** (`hs-3`): the impulse triggers synaptic vesicles in the axon terminal boutons to release neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft.
- **Signal received by next neuron** (`hs-4`): neurotransmitters cross the cleft and bind receptors on the postsynaptic neuron, restarting the electrical signal there.
- **Response triggered** (`hs-5`): the signal reaches the effector tissue and produces a physical response, such as contraction or secretion.

## 5. See where it belongs

Nerve Signaling sits in **Biology > Human Physiology > Nervous System Processes > Nerve Signaling Pathway** — it is the *process* a nerve cell carries out. The cell doing the work is the neuron you already met in the **Cell Biology > Specialized Cells > Neuron** component: that lesson explored the single cell's shape (dendrites, soma, axon, myelin, terminals); this lesson follows what that shape actually *does* when a signal has to travel from one neuron to another and on to a muscle or gland. Zoomed out further, this process is what the **Nervous System** (covered in the Human Anatomy branch) is built to carry out, billions of times a second, throughout your brain, spinal cord, and nerves.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a nerve signal to a **phone call**: the electrical impulse racing down the axon is like a signal traveling through a wire, but crossing the synapse — where the signal has to convert into a chemical, drift across a gap, and get picked up by a receiver — is like a phone converting your voice into a signal that a tower has to catch and relay onward. You can also compare it to a **domino chain**: each domino must fall for the next one to fall, but unlike a domino, a neuron isn't "used up" — it resets within milliseconds (the refractory period) and can fire again. Or compare it to a **garden-hose relay**: each neuron passes the "water" of the signal to the next one waiting at the gap, rather than one long hose running the whole distance.
