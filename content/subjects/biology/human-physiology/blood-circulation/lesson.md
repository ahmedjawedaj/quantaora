# Blood Circulation

*Biology > Human Physiology > Circulatory & Respiratory Processes > Cardiovascular system > Blood Circulation*

Your blood is like a delivery truck that never stops driving. Your heart is the engine that pushes it through a giant network of tubes called blood vessels, which stretch all through your body. As blood travels, it drops off oxygen and food to every cell, and picks up waste like carbon dioxide to carry away. Blood makes two loops on every trip around your body: a short loop to your lungs to grab fresh oxygen and dump carbon dioxide, and a long loop out to your whole body to deliver that oxygen and pick up waste. Then it comes back to the heart and does it all again — about once every minute, all day and all night, without you ever having to think about it.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D heart-and-vessel model and watch the whole-body circulation animation. Notice the blood moving out from the heart in one color (oxygen-rich) and returning in another color (oxygen-poor), and notice the vessels branching from thick trunks near the heart down to a fine mesh out at the edges of the body.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model and tap the heart to feel that it's mostly hollow muscle chambers, not a solid lump. Drag through the cross-section to reveal the four chambers and the wall (septum) between the left and right sides — this is the single most important thing to notice: the two sides never mix.

## 3. Name it

Tap each hotspot in order and name the parts: `hs-heart` (heart), `hs-arteries` (arteries), `hs-capillaries` (capillaries), `hs-veins` (veins), `hs-pulmonary` (pulmonary circuit), `hs-systemic` (systemic circuit).

## 4. Explain it

Trace one drop of blood on the animation as it leaves the heart, travels through an artery, squeezes through a capillary bed to trade gases with a cell, then returns through a vein. Explain, in your own words, what changes about the blood at each stop: arteries carry it away from the heart under pressure, capillaries are where the actual swap of oxygen, nutrients, and waste happens, and veins carry it back.

One important exception to learn here before moving on: **in the pulmonary circuit only**, the pulmonary artery carries oxygen-*poor* blood toward the lungs, and the pulmonary vein carries oxygen-*rich* blood back to the heart — the reverse of every other artery and vein in the body. Direction (away from vs. back to the heart) always defines an artery or vein; oxygen content does not.

## 6. Compare it

Compare blood circulation to **breathing (the respiratory cycle)**. Both are rhythmic, involuntary, muscle-driven cycles that never stop and work as a team: the heartbeat-driven cardiac cycle covered here carries oxygen to every cell in the body and brings carbon dioxide back to the lungs, while breathing is what loads fresh oxygen into the blood and unloads that carbon dioxide at the lungs in the first place. The pulmonary circuit is the literal handoff point between the two.

## 7. Use it in a task

Every cell in your body needs a steady supply of oxygen and nutrients to stay alive, and needs waste carried away just as constantly — that's the whole reason circulation exists, and it's why your heart races when you exercise, why a cut bleeds, and why doctors check your pulse and blood pressure.
