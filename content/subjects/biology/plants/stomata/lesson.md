# Stomata

*Biology > Plants > Plant Function > Plant surface structures > Stomata*

Stomata are the tiny pores, or openings, on the surface of leaves. Each one is a small gate that opens to let carbon dioxide in for photosynthesis and lets oxygen out - and it can close to save water.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a smooth leaf surface shown enlarged and tilted toward you, with tiny pore openings scattered across it. Pick one stoma and notice the two curved guard cells surrounding its opening.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in on a single stoma. Toggle the leaf surface layer off to look at one pore closely. Then use the open/close slider to watch the two guard cells swell up and bend apart (opening the pore) and relax together (closing it). Watch tiny dots enter and leave as you toggle.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-stoma` to label the pore opening, `hs-guardcells` to label the two guard cells, and `hs-surface` to label the leaf surface they sit in. Tap `hs-gas` to label the tiny carbon dioxide entering and check the oxygen leaving.

## 4. Explain what it does

Follow gas exchange: during the day, light shines on the leaf → the guard cells swell and bend, opening the stoma → carbon dioxide gas from the air enters the pore → it is used in photosynthesis → the leaf releases oxygen out through the same pore. This is how the plant breathes and takes in what it needs to make food.

**Prediction prompt:** If a plant loses a lot of water on a hot, dry afternoon, predict what you think the stomata will do - stay wide open as possible, or close up? Say why before you toggle the water-save mode.

**Practice item:** On the model, open the stoma and point to which gas enters and which leaves. Then describe the trade that happens when it opens.

## 5. See where it belongs

Stomata belong to the leaf and stem surfaces of a plant, the plant's out feat surfaces that meet the air. They connect to two big processes: photosynthesis (which needs carbon dioxide in and lets oxygen out) and transpiration (water loss, made up by water pulled up the xylem). They are the leaves' gates to the outside air.

## 6. Compare it

Compare **open** versus **closed** stomata. Open: carbon dioxide can enter and oxygen leaves, but water also escapes. Closed: water is saved, but carbon dioxide is kept out. It's a trade-off the plant uses to balance breathing water and saving it.

## 7. Use it in a task

It's a hot, dry afternoon and the leaves start to droop. Predict whether the plant will leave its stomata wide open or closed, and explain what it gains and what it gives up for each choice. Then tell why daytime usually is the time they open.

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain what happens when a stoma opens, and why a plant might close its stomata?
