# Phloem

*Biology > Plants > Tissue > Plant transport tissues > Phloem*

Phloem is the plant tissue that carries food. The sugar that leaves make by photosynthesis is moved by phloem to every part of the plant that needs it - roots, seeds, fruits, and growing shoots.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a vertical slice of the plant with a bundle of tubes running down, shown in a warm golden color (the food color). Sweet golden droplets move downward from the leaf at the top into tubes that fork to a root and a seed at the bottom.

## 4. Explain what it does

Follow a molecule of sugar: a leaf makes it from sunlight+water+CO2 → the sugar is moved into the phloem tube → the food moves out of the leaf into the stem → it travels down and along to the roots, seeds, and growing parts → each use the food the plant's energy or stores it as starch. So phloem's one job is carrying the food that the leaves made to the rest of the plant.

**Prediction prompt:** Where's the food the leaves make come from? Predict which plant part 'produces' the food and which parts of the plant always receive it. Then check that the tube flows from leaf.

**Practice item:** Using the model, name the tube that brings food, the part that makes it, and name two parts that receive it.

## 5. See where it belongs

Phloem is the food delivery half of the plant's transport tissue. It works with xylem, the water-bringing tissue, to supply the whole plant. It hands food to growing parts and storage, keeping every living cell of a green plant fed.

## 6. Compare it

Compare phloem and xylem. Phloem carries the food sugar from the leaves (mainly down and around). Xylem carries water and minerals up from the root. Two opposite directions, two different cargos, same end - a fed and watered plant.
