# Mitochondrion

*Biology > Cell Biology > Cell Structures > Cell structures (organelles) > Mitochondrion*

The mitochondrion is the powerhouse of the cell. It turns food and oxygen into a usable form of energy that power everything a cell does.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a small, bean-shaped structure floating among other organelles. Notice it has two walls (membranes) and that the inner wall folds into many shelf-like channels. That folded inner surface is the key to its power.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model and zoom in. Use the 'peel away outer membrane' control to show the inner folds (cristae). Watch small glowing dots move through the cristae to represent energy being made, then replay to see the process begin again.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-outer-membrane` to label the outer membrane. Tap `hs-cristae` to label the folded inner membrane. Tap `hs-matrix` to label the fluid inside, and `hs-energy` to label the spot where usable energy (ATP) is made.

## 4. Explain it

Walk through what the mitochondrion does: it takes small sugar molecules and, using oxygen, breaks them down to release energy. That energy is packaged into ATP, the 'coin' the cell can spend. The folds make lots of surface area so the cell fits many of its energy machines and makes energy fast.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict whether a muscle cell or a skin cell would need more mitochondria, then find the reason listed in the key facts.

## 5. Belongs to

The mitochondrion is an **organelle** that floats in the cytoplasm of almost every eukaryotic cell. It is part of: organelle -> cell -> tissue -> organ -> organism. Its supply of energy connects to blood circulation (which brings oxygen and sugar) and to breathing in the human body.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the mitochondrion with the **chloroplast** (plant only). The mitochondrion breaks down food to release energy in every cell type; the chloroplast uses sunlight to build food in plant cells. One is the power generator used by all; the other is the solar panel used by plants.
