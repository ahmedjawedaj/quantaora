# Endoplasmic Reticulum

*Biology > Cell Biology > Cell Structures > Cell structures (organelles) > Endoplasmic Reticulum*

The endoplasmic reticulum is a long, folded network of membranes that spreads through the cytoplasm like a maze of roads. It has two parts with two jobs. The rough ER is dotted with ribosomes, so it is where many proteins are built and folded, then packed into bubbles to ship. The smooth ER has no ribosomes; it makes lipids (fats) and helps process and move many molecules. Together they are the cell's workshop and delivery system, moving materials to other organelles and toward the Golgi for shipping out.

## 4. Explain it

The rough ER builds and folds proteins because it wears ribosomes; the smooth ER makes fats and processes molecules. Both send finished products off in bubbles to the Golgi.
