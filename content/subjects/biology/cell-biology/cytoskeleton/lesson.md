# Cytoskeleton

*Biology > Cell Biology > Cell Structures > Cell structures (organelles) > Cytoskeleton*

The cytoskeleton is a web of protein fibers running through the cytoplasm that acts like the cell's skeleton, muscles, and road system all in one. It has three types of fibers: thin filaments (like ropes), thicker tubes called microtubules (like support beams and tracks), and middle-sized intermediate filaments (like cables). This framework gives the cell its shape, anchors organelles in place, and provides tracks along which materials are moved. It can even change shape to move the whole cell, which is how some cells crawl. So the cytoskeleton is the cell's internal frame and delivery rail combined.
