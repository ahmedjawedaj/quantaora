# DNA

*Biology > Cell Biology > Cell Structures > Cell structures (organelles) > DNA*

DNA is the molecule that carries the instructions for building and running a living thing. It looks like a twisted ladder and lives mostly inside the nucleus.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a long strand shaped like a twisted ladder. Notice the two smooth rails running around the roof, and pairs of colored pieces forming the rungs between them. This is the famous double helix.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model to see the twist clearly from above and the side. Zoom in and toggle the 'pairing' mode (watch two rung pieces snap together). Try the 'unzip' control to separate the two halves, then snap them back.

## 3. Name it

Tap the `hs-backbone` hotspot to label the sugar-phosphate backbone. Tap `hs-base-pair` to label a base pair (a rung). Tap `hs-helix` to label the double helix shape whole, and `hs-gene` to label a segment that forms a gene.

## 4. Explain it

Walk through what DNA is: two strands twist into the double helix, and the sequence of base rungs (A-T and G-C) is the code. That code tells the cell how to make things. Because the bases pair in a fixed way, one strand can build a copy of the other when the cell divides.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict which base would pair with an adenine (A) letter - then toggle the pairing mode to check the rule.

## 5. Belongs to

DNA belongs to the **nucleus** of the cell (and a little to the mitochondria). From DNA we move up the hierarchy: DNA -> gene -> chromosome -> nucleus -> cell -> organism. It is the link between cell-biology life and genetics heredity.

## 6. Compare it

Compare DNA with **RNA**. DNA is double-stranded, stays in the nucleus, and holds the master copy. RNA is single-stranded, can leave the nucleus, and carries copies of the instructions to the machinery that builds proteins.
