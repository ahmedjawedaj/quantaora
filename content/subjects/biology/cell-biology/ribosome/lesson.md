# Ribosome

*Biology > Cell Biology > Cell Structures > Cell structures (organelles) > Ribosome*

The ribosome is the tiny factory that makes proteins. It reads a copy of an instruction from the cell's DNA and, following it like a recipe, joins small building blocks called amino acids together in the exact order shown. The chain folds into a protein that does real work, like building structure, moving molecules, or fighting germs. Ribosomes can float free in the cytoplasm or clamp onto the rough endoplasmic reticulum. Either way, their one job is to read the code and build protein to match.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: two rounded parts snugged together, with a ribbon of message threading between them and small beads stacking up on one side. This is the ribosome - the cell's protein builder.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model and zoom in. Toggle the 'animation' layer to watch the message slide through while the beads (amino acids) snap on one at a time. Pause and replay to see the order clearly.

## 3. Name it

Tap the `hs-small-subunit` hotspot to label the small subunit, then `hs-large-subunit` for the large subunit. Tap `hs-read` to label where the message is read, and `hs-join` to label where amino acids join.

## 4. Explain it

The ribosome reads the coded message like a recipe, then links amino acids in the exact order the recipe says. When the chain is done it folds into a working protein.
