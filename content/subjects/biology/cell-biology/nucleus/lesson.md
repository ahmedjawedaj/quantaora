# Nucleus

*Biology > Cell Biology > Cell Structures > Cell structures (organelles) > Nucleus*

The nucleus is the command center of a cell — a rounded sac wrapped in a double membrane, holding the cell's DNA and directing everything it does. Think of it as the brain or the control-room of the cell.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a large, rounded sphere floating inside a transparent slice of cell. Notice the double boundary around it, the network of small openings in that boundary, and the denser sphere nestled inside. Watch how the nucleus sits off to one side of the cell rather than being the only structure present.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the whole cell and zoom into the nucleus. Run your pointer over the outer surface and feel how the envelope is made of two layers. Try the isolate control so only the nucleus remains, then look through it as if it were a window — can you spot the dense inner structure?

## 3. Name it

Tap the `hs-nuclear-envelope` hotspot to label the nuclear envelope. Tap `hs-nuclear-pore` to label a single pore in that envelope. Tap `hs-nucleolus` to label the dense inner sphere, and `hs-nucleoplasm` to label the fluid space that fills the nucleus around it.

## 4. Explain it

Walk through what the nucleus does: the DNA stored inside holds the instructions, and when a cell needs a protein, the nucleus copies that instruction and sends it out through the pores. So the nucleus is like the dispatcher — it keeps the master plan safe and hands out copies when a job needs doing.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you turn on the isolate mode, predict which structure you think stays inside the nucleus when the rest of the envelope is hidden — then check.

## 5. Belongs to

The nucleus belongs to the **eukaryotic cell** — it sits in the cytoplasm alongside the other organelles and coordinates their work. It's part of the bigger story: nucleus → cell → tissue → organ → organism, and its instructions connect directly to genetics (DNA replication and transcription).

## 6. Compare it

Compare the nucleus with the **bacterial cell**. A bacterium has no nucleus — its DNA floats freely in the cytoplasm. That means the 'control room' of a bacterium is open to the rest of the cell, unlike the sealed, organized compartment found in plant and animal cells.
