# Cell Membrane

*Biology > Cell Biology > Cell Structures > Cell structures (organelles) > Cell Membrane*

The cell membrane is the thin, flexible wrapping that surrounds every cell and controls what enters and leaves. It is the security gate between the cell's inside and the outside world.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a thin but continuous sheet shown in a cutaway slice of cell. Notice it is a double layer made up of many small 'head' and 'tail' pieces, with larger blob-shaped proteins dotted along it. This is the boundary every cell shares.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model and zoom in on the edge. Bend the view around to see both the inner face and the outer face of the membrane. Toggle the 'transport' layer and watch how some small blue dots pass through freely while larger red objects stop at the surface until a protein door opens for them.

## 3. Name it

Tap the `hs-bilayer` hotspot to label the phospholipid bilayer. Tap `hs-protein` to label an embedded protein. Tap `hs-selective-gate` to label the selective gate, and `hs-boundary` to label where the membrane separates cytoplasm from the outside.

## 4. Explain it

Walk through the main job: the membrane is selectively permeable. Water and tiny simple molecules slip straight through the oily bilayer, but bigger or charged ones need special protein doors. So the cell is always choosing — letting in what it needs, keeping out what it doesn't — and releasing waste.

**Prediction prompt:** Predict whether a large sugar molecule and a small water molecule pass through the membrane the same way — then check the 'transport' layer to confirm.

## 5. Belongs to

The cell membrane wraps **every** cell — animal, plant, and bacterial alike. In plant cells it runs just inside the stiffer cell wall. It is the outermost limit of the cytoplasm, and it belongs to the bigger idea of cell structure and cellular transport.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the cell membrane with the **cell wall** of a plant. The membrane is thin, flexible, alive, and present in all cells — it controls what enters and leaves. The wall is thick, rigid, outside the membrane, and present only in plants, fungi, and some bacteria; it gives support and shape but does not control entry.

Use the model to answer a real question: *'Your cells need nutrients and oxygen but must keep out some waste materials. Why is it useful that the membrane is selective rather than open to everything?'*

**Practice item:** In the transport view, drag a water drop and a large nutrient, then tell which path each takes to get inside the cell.
