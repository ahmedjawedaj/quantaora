# Vacuole

*Biology > Cell Biology > Cell Structures > Cell structures (organelles) > Vacuole*

A vacuole is a membrane sac that stores materials. In plant cells there is usually one large central vacuole, mostly filled with water and dissolved substances, that acts like a water tank. When it is full of water, it presses against the cell wall and helps the plant stand tall; when it is empty, the plant wilts. The vacuole can also store nutrients, pigments, and waste. Animal cells have several smaller vacuoles that help store and move materials. So the vacuole is the cell's storage space and, in plants, its water support system.

## 4. Explain it

The vacuole stores water, salts, and materials. In plants the large central vacuole's water pressure pushes against the cell wall to keep the cell (and the plant) firm. Animal cells use smaller vacuoles for moving things.
