# Golgi Apparatus

*Biology > Cell Biology > Cell Structures > Cell structures (organelles) > Golgi Apparatus*

The Golgi apparatus looks like a stack of flattened pancakes or a delivery depot. It receives proteins and lipids from the ER in transport bubbles, then sorts and packages them for their next destination. It may tag them with labels that say where they belong, or wrap them in new membrane bubbles. Some products are shipped out of the cell entirely. In this way the Golgi is the cell's mailroom: receiving, finishing, addressing, and dispatching.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a neat stack of flattened pouches like a pile of pancakes, with small bubbles arriving at one side and leaving from the other.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate the model and zoom in. Toggle the 'traffic' layer to watch bubbles arrive, travel through the stack, and leave as packaged cargo.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-cisternae` to label the stacked pouches, `hs-in` for an arriving bubble, `hs-out` for a departing one, and `hs-label` for the labeling region.

## 4. Explain it

The Golgi receives products from the ER, then sorts, labels, and packages them. Finished bubbles then head to other organelles or out of the cell - it is the cell's mailroom.
