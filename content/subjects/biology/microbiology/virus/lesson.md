# Virus

*Biology > Microbiology > Microbes > Microbial types > Virus*

A virus is the smallest kind of infectious agent. It is not a cell - it is a package of genetic material wrapped in a protein coat. It cannot reproduce on its own; it must invade a living cell and force it to make copies of the virus.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a single virus floating, shaped like a small capsule or ball with spikes sticking out. Its outer surface is a protein coat. Several new viruses appear nearby, apparently being produced. A line shows them approaching an actual host cell in the next field.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Toggle the protein coat away to reveal the genetic material inside. Toggle the infect animation to watch the virus attach to a host cell, deliver its material in, and watch the host cell pop out many new virus particles.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-coat` to label the protein coat, `hs-genetic` to label the genetic material (DNA or RNA) inside, `hs-spikes` to label the attachment spikes, and `hs-host` to label the host cell it invades.

## 4. Explain what it does

A virus is a hijacker. Walk through it: the virus floats until it finds a host cell → its spikes attach to the target cell's surface → the virus delivers its genetic material inside → the host cell's machinery reads that material and starts copying the virus → many new virus particles are built → they leave the cell to infect more cells. Because a virus has no machinery of its own, it must use the host to keep making copies.

**Prediction prompt:** A virus alone in water, with no cells nearby. Predict whether it can make copies - or why not - before you look at how it must find a host.

**Practice item:** On the animation, count the new viruses that come out of one host cell; then name the two components a virus is made of.

## 5. See where it belongs

A virus is part of the microbial world along with bacteria, fungi, and protozoa, but it is the odd one out because it is not a living cell. In the body it is fought by the immune response (a Human Physiology process), and understanding it is behind both infections and vaccines. Put it with the microbes you already know.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a **virus** to a **bacterium**. A bacterium is a living single cell that can grow and divide on its own. A virus carries only genes in a coat and must take over a living cell to reproduce. That difference is why bacteria and viral illnesses affect cells and treatments differently.

## 7. Use it in a task

A box of viral particles is left alone with no living cells at all. Predict what will happen - why a virus cannot multiply by itself. Then think: since your body's cells are living, what happens if a virus enters one? Give the likely next-step of the copying, tightly to the model.

**Checkpoint:** Can you explain why a virus is not a cell, and how it needs a host cell to make more of itself?
