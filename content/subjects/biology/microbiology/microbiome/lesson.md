# Microbiome

*Biology > Microbiology > Microbes > Microbial communities > Microbiome*

The microbiome is the community of trillions of tiny organisms - mostly bacteria, plus some fungi - that live in and on your body. The biggest community lives in your gut. These microbes are normally your partners, not your enemies.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a gentle outline of a human body, with the skin and the digestive tract highlighted. On the skin and along the gut, thousands of tiny colored dots (microbes) swarm - a vast community. A magnifying inset zooms into a stretch of gut wall covered in them.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in on the gut. Toggle the 'microbes' layer to see them spread densely along the gut lining and sparsely on the skin. Toggle the 'jobs' layer to watch labels light up - digesting, vitamins, immune help. Toggle 'balance' to see the community stay mixed and healthy.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-gut` to label the gut microbiome, `hs-skin` to label the skin residents, `hs-bacteria` to label the mostly-bacteria members, and `hs-jobs` to label the helpful jobs they do. Tap `hs-balance` to mark where the community is in balance.

## 4. Explain what it does

Follow what your microbiome does for you: food enters your gut → the gut bacteria help break down parts you cannot digest → some bacteria make vitamins you absorb → the presence of all these microbes trains your immune system to respond correctly → and by filling the space they crowd out harmful germs. In return, you feed them. It is a partnership that keeps you healthy when the community is balanced.

**Practice item:** On the model, name the two main places the microbiome lives, and name two helpful jobs you see labeled.

## 5. See where it belongs

The microbiome is the community of microbes living in and on you - a special example of the microbial world covered throughout this branch. It connects to the ecosystem idea from Ecology: like any ecosystem, it is made of many interacting species whose balance supports health. It also works hand in hand with your immune system.

## 6. Compare it

Compare the microbiome to a **forest ecosystem**. A forest is a community of species that support each other and stay in balance. Your microbiome is a community of microbes doing the same inside you - many species, each with a job, kept in balance.

## 7. Use it in a task

Someone takes a strong course of antibiotics and afterward has stomach trouble. Predict what likely changed inside their gut, and name two habits that could help the microbiome recover. Then explain why 'germs' are not always the enemy.

**Checkpoint:** Can you name two places your microbiome lives, two helpful jobs it does, and one thing that can upset its balance?
