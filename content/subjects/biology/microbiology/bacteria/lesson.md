# Bacteria

*Biology > Microbiology > Microbes > Microbial types > Bacteria*

Bacteria are tiny single-celled living things found almost everywhere - in the soil, in water, in our bodies, and even in the air. Most are harmless, many are helpful, and a few can make us sick.

## 4. Explain what it does

A bacterium is a complete living thing in one cell. It takes in food from around it, uses energy, grows, and reproduces by binary fission - it copies its DNA, then splits down the middle into two identical daughter cells. Because it has no nucleus, its DNA lies free in the cell. These cells can divide very fast, which is how bacteria can become a whole crowd in hours.

**Prediction prompt:** A single bacterium splits into 2, then those 2 become 4. Predict how many there will be after the next round, and think about why that fast growth matters if they're harmful.

**Practice item:** On the model, run the division animation once and count the cells; then name the two parts you saw at the surface of the cell (wall and membrane).

## 5. See where it belongs

Bacteria are part of the microbial world, and also one type of cell studied in Cell Biology (a prokaryotic cell with no nucleus). In your body, bacteria live on the skin and in the gut as part of your microbiome, helping digest food. In nature, they recycle dead material and enrich soil. They belong everywhere life exists.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a **bacterium** to a **virus**. A bacterium is a living single-celled organism that can grow, feed, and divide on its own. A virus is not a cell and cannot reproduce by itself - it must invade a living cell to make more copies. That is why bacteria and viruses need different treatments.

## 7. Use it in a task

A student forgets to refrigerate some food overnight and the next day it smells sour. Predict the most likely microbe behind it and whether it reproduces by splitting. Then think: how does the fact that bacteria divide fast explain why keeping food cold helps?

**Checkpoint:** Can you name one helpful and one harmful role of bacteria, and explain how a bacterium makes more of itself?
