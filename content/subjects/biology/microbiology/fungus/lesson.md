# Fungus

*Biology > Microbiology > Microbes > Microbial types > Fungus*

A fungus (plural fungi) is a living organism such as a mushroom, mold, or yeast. It belongs to its own kingdom, is neither plant nor animal, and does not photosynthesize - it absorbs food by breaking down material around it.

## 1. See it

Look at the 3D model: a clean mushroom above a patch of soil, with the soil partly cut away to reveal the real body of the fungus beneath it - a web of fine threads called hyphae spreading through the soil. Small specks of spores drift near the mushroom cap.

## 2. Touch it

Rotate and zoom in. Toggle the soil away to see more of the hyphae threads running underground. Toggle a spores layer to watch tiny specks release from the mushroom. Toggle a yeast mode to see a separate single-celled fungus.

## 3. Name it

Tap `hs-fruit` to label the mushroom fruiting body, `hs-hyphae` to label a single thread, `hs-mycelium` to label the whole thread network, `hs-spores` to label the tiny spores, and `hs-yeast` to label the single-celled yeast.

## 4. Explain what it does

A fungus feeds by decomposition: it releases enzymes that break down material around it, then it absorbs the nutrients. Follow its reproduction: a spore lands and starts a thread (hypha) → the thread branches into a dense mycelium network absorbing food → when food and moisture are right, a fruiting body (mushroom) grows up → the mushroom makes and releases spores → the spores spread to start new fungi. So the mushroom you see is only the spore-releasing part; the large food-absorbing body is the hidden mycelium.

**Prediction prompt:** Before you check the light layer, predict whether a mushroom makes its food by catching sunlight like a plant, or gets food another way.

**Practice item:** On the model, name the two body parts you see (the hidden threads and the visible mushroom), and say which of them eats the food.

## 5. See where it belongs

The fungus is its own kingdom, separate from plants and animals. It is a key decomposer in nature, recycling dead material, and it lives alongside other microbes in soil, on skin, and in our gut. Alongside bacteria, protozoa, and viruses, fungi are part of the microbial world.

## 6. Compare it

Compare a fungus to a **plant**. A plant catches sunlight and makes its own food by photosynthesis. A fungus cannot photosynthesize - it breaks down food around it with enzymes and absorbs it. They look somewhat alike, but feed in very different ways.

## 7. Use it in a task

A fallen log slowly rots away in the forest. Predict which kind of microbe does the heavy work of breaking it into reusable nutrients, and name the thread network the fungus spreads. Then explain why decomposers like fungi are important to new plants growing in the soil.

**Practice item:** Name the two ways a mushroom spreads children (spores), and tell why yeast is important in bread-making.

**Checkpoint:** Can you say what makes fungi different from plants, how they reproduce, and where their main body is?
